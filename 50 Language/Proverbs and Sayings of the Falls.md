# AITM — Proverbs and Sayings of the Falls
### *Aylì'fyavi* — the old sayings, and who says them

> Every saying here is **canon Na'vi**: published by Paul Frommer (mostly on Na'viteri) or recorded in Reykunyu, and checked word by word against Reykunyu (October 2026). None were invented for AITM. What *is* AITM's is who says them, and when. Literal glosses come from Reykunyu.

---

## 'Ipxerey — the Tsahìk

She learned wisdom by walking through her own darkness, so her sayings are about patience, and about the gap between doing right and getting what you want.

**Payìl a lipx tskxeti ripx.**
*Dripping water pierces a stone.*
Literally: water that drips pierces the stone (*pay* water · *lipx* drip · *tskxe* stone · *ripx* pierce).
For students who think they are not progressing. She says it to Sänrri, to Sänume, to anyone learning something sacred and slow.

**Kem amuiä kum afe'.**
*A proper action, a bad result.*
Literally: deed right, result bad (*kem* deed · *muiä* right, proper · *kum* result · *fe'* bad).
Her quietest and heaviest saying: doing the right thing does not promise a good outcome, and you do it anyway. She knows the opposite too, from her old life: the wrong deed that "worked."

**A story beat, if wanted:** Waynhì's favorite saying is *fwäkì ke fwefwi*, "a mantis does not whistle," people do not act against their nature (see below). 'Ipxerey is the one person at the Falls who has the right to answer: "*This* mantis learned."

## Eyfuwen — the Olo'eyktan

A leader's sayings are about choosing, and about knowing which fights cannot be won.

**Txìm a'aw ke tsun hiveyn mì tal mefa'liyä.**
*One butt cannot sit on two direhorses' backs.*
Literally: one butt cannot sit on the backs of two direhorses (*txìm* butt · *heyn* sit, here h‹iv›eyn · *txal* back, lenited after *mì* · *pa'li* direhorse, dual *mefa'li* + genitive).
Frommer's own gloss: you cannot bet on two horses; you have to decide. Eyfuwen says it at council when someone wants it both ways, and it lands with full ceremonial gravity, every vowel long in the Astikasya way, which is exactly why it is funny.

**Ätxäle palulukanur.**
*Asking a thanator.*
Literally: a request to a thanator (*ätxäle* request · *palulukan* thanator, dative).
A futile attempt. His verdict on negotiating with the RDA, or with Varang.

**Säfpìl asteng tìkan ateng.**
*Similar thought, same aim.*
Great minds think alike. What he and Tse'hayu say to each other when two old war companions arrive at the same plan without speaking.

## Mitsìyo — the weaver, the clan's emotional anchor

Her sayings are the soft ones: comfort, and permission to go slowly.

**Tam tam.**
*There, there.*
Straight from the film (2009). Calm, be content. What she murmurs to anyone who comes to her crying, of any age.

**Ngari txe'lan mawey livu.**
*Do not worry.*
Literally: as for you, may the heart be calm (*nga* + topical *-ri* · *txe'lan* heart · *mawey* calm · *lu* with ‹iv›).

**'Ivong nìk'ong.**
*May it bloom slowly.*
Literally: let it blossom slowly (*'ong* blossom, with ‹iv› · *nìk'ong* slowly). Frommer's gloss: "slow is fine." What she told Txewan through every anxious spiral, and what Txewan now tells her own children.

**Kxetse sì mikyun kop plltxe.**
*The tail and the ears also speak.*
Body language matters. It is how she always knows when someone is struggling before they say a word.

**Maite / Maitan.**
*My daughter / my son.*
Canon forms of address. Mitsìyo calls every child at the Falls this, whether they are hers or not.

## Tse'hayu — the cook, the blunt perfectionist

**Txo ke nìyo', tsakrr nìyol.**
*If you cannot be perfect, at least be brief.*
Literally: if not perfectly, then briefly (*nìyo'* perfectly · *nìyol* briefly). His entire teaching philosophy in four words, delivered to music students and kitchen helpers alike.

**Srefwa sngap zize'.**
*Before the hellfire wasp stings.*
Literally: before the hellfire wasp stings (*srefwa* before · *sngap* sting · *zize'* hellfire wasp). Frommer: the Na'vi ASAP ("SFSZ"). How he wants the firewood. Now.

**Nitram nì'aw!**
*Happy only!*
The Na'vi toast. He raises it over the evening meal when a dish comes out right, which, for him, is rarely and therefore loudly.

## Waynhì — the storyteller

**Slä hayalo alahe.**
*But that is for another time.*
Literally: but another next time (*slä* but · *hayalo* next time · *lahe* other). Canon phrase for ending a story. Her signature: she always stops at the best part, and every child at the Falls has learned to groan at these four words.

**Fwäkì ke fwefwi.**
*A mantis does not whistle.*
Do not expect someone to act against their nature. Usually about Tìfyaw's drinking.

**Pewn torukä.**
*A toruk's neck.*
A method that will not work. Her verdict on most of what the younglings try.

## Sayings that belong to everyone else

**Loreyu 'awnampi.** *A touched helicoradian.* Someone extremely shy, curling up like the spiral plant when touched (*loreyu* helicoradian · *'ampi* touch, passive participle). The clan's affectionate name for how Txewan was, and sometimes still is.

**Na txim a txìmmì.** *Like a thorn in the butt.* Something extremely annoying. What Notä calls Kxayì. What Kxayì calls Notä.

**Ngeyä kxetse lu oeru!** *Your tail is mine!* "Got you!" in the children's chasing games.

**Kenten mì kumpay.** *A fan lizard stuck in gel.* Like a fish out of water. Lamaytxa's description of Kobeni at the Falls.

**Zawprrte' nìtokat.** Guilty pleasure at someone's misfortune; Frommer's word for schadenfreude (used with *ne*). Lamaytxa, constantly.

**Polite answers.** Na'vi has canon replies that work like Japanese politeness formulas, which makes them Sänrri's natural register: **ke pxan** "not worthy" (to a compliment), **ngaru tsulfä** "to you the mastery" (you are better at it than I am), **oeru meuia** "an honor to me" (to thanks), **pum ngeyä** "yours" (no, *I* should thank *you*), **kea tìkin** "no need."

## New from the LearnNavi Dictionary (v. 16.2.1, October 24, 2026)

The LearnNavi dictionary's "Useful Phrases" appendix collects Frommer's phrases from Na'viteri. Its words were already in Reykunyu; these **phrases** are what is new for AITM. All checked word by word in Reykunyu.

**Fwa kan ke tam; zene swizawit livonu.** — *To aim is not enough; one must release the arrow.* Intent is not enough; action counts. **Rewon's**, without question: the Unerring, the best archer of the Haykxa, who aims at everything and then actually lets go.

**Taronyut yom smarìl.** — *The prey eats the hunter.* A totally botched situation, chaos. What the clan says about any plan Kxayì is involved in, and the only fair summary of *The Night the Warriors Lost Their Dignity*.

**Sasya! / Siva ko! / Soleia!** — *I will rise to the challenge!* / *Rise to the challenge, courage, you can do it!* / *Congratulations, you did it!* A canon call-and-response for any dare, race or duel. Kxayì shouts *Sasya!* at Kaymi before every sparring match she will lose; the whole clan yells *Siva ko!* during the Great Games.

**Fnu, ma 'evi. Sa'nur leru hawtsyìp. Tsivurokx ko.** — *Quiet, little one. Mommy is taking a nap. Let her rest.* A canon line that uses *sa'nu* "mommy." Kaymi to Lì'may, about Sänrri; Txewan to the twins, about Syulang.

**Etrìpa syayvi.** — *Good luck* (lit. "favorable luck"). It contains Syayvi's own name: every time someone at the Falls wishes good luck, they say her name. For the Penitent, that can be a blessing or a wound.

**Nga yawne lu oer.** — *I love you* (lit. "you are beloved to me"). **Txon lefpom.** — *Good night, peaceful night.* **Kìyevame.** — *Goodbye, see you again soon.* **Makto zong.** — *Take care, travel safely* (what the Falls say to the Troupe when they fly out). **Tolätxaw nìprrte'.** — *Welcome back* (what they say when the Troupe returns; *tätxaw* is the same verb as Sìltsyu's *"Oe frakrr terätxaw ngane"*).

**Tsun pehem?** — *What can one do?* **Ke tare.** — *It does not matter.* **Stum ke** — *almost not, barely.* **Yewla!** — *What a shame!* **Tìyawr ngaru / Tìkxey ngaru** — *You are right / You are wrong.* **Tìomummì oeyä** — *as far as I know.* **Tì'efumì oeyä** — *in my opinion.* **Nìmip** — *anew* (a new adverb).

**A small surprise.** Frommer's own example for "my name is" is ***Oeru syaw fko Txewì*** — "My name is Txewì." Txewan's nickname is the name Frommer used to teach introductions.
