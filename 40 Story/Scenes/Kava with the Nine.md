### A night on the high platform: the Nine Spears, Nì'syu, Syulang and Sänrri

> All dialogue is in Na'vi, each line checked word by word with Reykunyu's parser and against canon usage examples (October 2026). Three lines come straight from canon example sentences in the Annotated Dictionary: Ketxe's line about the helicoradian, Tsa'rawm's "Drink up!", and his "Perhaps I drank too much." The only words the parser does not know are the characters' names and *avolaw* ("nine"), which follows the number rule in the project's Na'vi reference.

---

The Falls are never truly quiet. After the children sleep, the noise only changes: the waterfall stays, and the laughter changes key.

Someone had dragged out the *swoasey ayll*, the great kava bowl for sharing, carved from a seed pod so old that nobody remembered whose hands had first hollowed it. It sat in the middle of the circle on the high platform under the blue glow of the moss, and everyone dipped a small *tsngal* into it, more often than any Tsahìk would have approved of.

'Ipxerey was not there. That was the point.

Syulang raised her cup with both hands. She was the shortest in the circle by a head and a half, and somehow the only one who could make twelve warriors sit down at once.

**"Tukrur avolaw! Nitram nì'aw!"**
*"To the Nine Spears! Happy only!"*

**"NITRAM NÌ'AW!"** Tsa'rawm roared, loud enough that something in the canopy took offense and flew away.

Rewon did not even look at him. **"Ayeveng herahaw."**
*"The children are sleeping."*

**"Fo lu oeyä. Fo frakrr hahaw!"**
*"They are mine. They always sleep!"*

**"Kxawm,"** said Rewon, in a tone that put *perhaps* a long way from *yes*.

Txewan dipped her cup, looked into it as if it might ask her something difficult, took the smallest sip anyone had ever seen, and held it in her lap like something that might bite. Tsa'rawm leaned across the circle and pointed at it.

**"Ngeyä tsngal lumpe lu mek? Näk nì'ul ko!"**
*"Why is your cup empty? Drink up!"*

**"Kehe! Pol tsat näk nìk'ong,"** said Syulang, without looking up.
*"No! She drinks it slowly."*

Ketxe tilted her head toward Txewan, fond, and said it the way the old saying goes:

**"Lu por mokri amiklor, slä loreyu 'awnampi lu. Ke tsun rivol eo sute."**
*"She has a beautiful voice, but she is extremely shy. She cannot sing in front of people."*

Txewan's ears went flat and her tail tried to hide behind Syulang, which did not work, because Syulang was very small.

When the bowl came to Syayvi, she held her cup over it a moment, breathed in the smell, and set the cup down empty. Nobody said anything. Nobody needed to. Sìlpey was not born yet, but she had been sitting in the middle of every circle for months. Nì'syu pressed a gourd of water into Syayvi's hands, and for once the Penitent let herself be taken care of.

---

It was Tsa'rawm, of course, two cups in, who brought up the duel.

**"Ma Tsu'ong, plltxe ayngaru!"**
*"Tsu'ong, tell them!"*

**"Kehe,"** said Tsu'ong.

**"Po wolem wä Atanvi, Mìreyaru! Ulte po snolaytx!"**
*"He fought against Atanvi, for Mìreya! And he lost!"*

Atanvi had gone a deep, blotchy violet. **"R-r-rä'ä plltxe!"**
*"D-d-do not say it!"*

**"Ulte po zolup mì ngoa!"**
*"And he fell in the mud!"*

Ketxe covered her face with her hand. Her shoulders were shaking.

Tsu'ong raised his chin with tremendous dignity and spoke to the night sky:

**"Oe lolu skxawng nìngay."**
*"I truly was a moron."*

**"Set nga lu lafyon,"** said Kaymi, dry as stone.
*"Now you are wise."*

Atanvi, still violet, said into her knees: **"P-po plolltxe nìngay."**
*"H-he spoke truly."*

And that broke the circle. Even Rewon laughed.

---

Later, when the bowl had gone around too many times and the stories had grown quieter, Tsa'rawm stopped laughing. He looked across the fire at Ketxe for a long moment, the way you look at something you lost and found again in a different shape.

**"Ma Ketxe… irayo. Ngal oeti zolong."**
*"Ketxe… thank you. You saved me."*

Ketxe went very still. She had disappeared, once, to keep Varang away from him, and she had never been sure he understood.

**"Pelun set?"**
*"Why now?"*

**"Kxawm swoat nìhawng noläk oel."**
*"Perhaps I drank too much."*

**"Rä'ä rou!"** said Rewon, far too late.
*"Do not get drunk!"*

Ketxe laughed, a short, wet sound. Tsa'rawm's hand had rested on Rewon's knee the whole time; Tsu'ong's tail was curled around Ketxe's ankle. Rewon and Tsu'ong looked at each other across the fire, and there was no jealousy in it, only two people who had married into a very old story.

**"Oel tsat omum,"** said Rewon.
*"I know (that)."*

**"Oel tsat omum nìteng,"** said Tsu'ong.
*"I know (that) too."*

---

Syayvi had been quiet a long time, turning the water gourd in her hands. When she finally spoke, it was so low that only the ones beside her heard.

**"Oe ke lu tute."**
*"I am not a person."*

**"Kehe!"** said Nì'syu at once, fiercely.

But it was Tsu'lan who looked at her. Tsu'lan, who had wandered from clan to clan after the Sky People burned his home, said the same words he had once thrown at Luka like a blade, and this time he held them out like an open hand:

**"Oe lu nga."**
*"I am you."*

Syayvi looked at him. Something in her shoulders let go.

Nì'syu rested her forehead against her mate's. **"Ngari txe'lan mawey livu."**
*"May your heart be calm."*

---

Kaymi, who did not get sentimental, had gotten sentimental. She had her head in Sänrri's lap and was looking up at her as if she were the moon.

**"Ma Sänrri, nga lu plltxeyu anawfwe."**
*"Sänrri, you are a fluent speaker."*

**"Ke pxan,"** said Sänrri, as she always did.
*"I am not worthy."*

**"Pxan!"** Kaymi insisted, poking her chin.
*"Worthy!"*

Sänrri smiled, slowly, and said it the way only she says things, with the topic first, as if setting it down on a table:

**"Fìtxonìri, oe 'efu nitram."**
*"As for tonight, I feel happy."*

---

**"Ma Txewì, rol!"** Syulang begged.
*"Txewì, sing!"*

**"K-k-kehe…"**

But Syulang was already humming, and Txewan's stutter has never once survived a melody. Her voice came in under Syulang's like the waterfall under everything, no words, only a tune, a *tìng lawr*, carrying a rhythm of the Falls that nobody had taught her. Tsu'lan beat it out on his knee. Atanvi, still faintly violet, joined in. Even Tsu'ong closed his eyes.

In front of all of them, the touched helicoradian sang.

Ketxe looked around the circle: the Nine, the mates, the children asleep below, the mist going red above the fire.

**"Kea 'u lu sìltsan to fpom,"** she said quietly, to no one.
*"Nothing is better than peace."*

---

By the time the song ended, Tsa'rawm was asleep, snoring with his mouth open and his tail draped over Tsu'lan. Syulang patted his head.

**"Tam tam."**
*"There, there."*

Rewon let out the oldest sigh at the Falls.

**"Fìskxawngìri tsap'alute sängi oe."**
*"I apologize for this moron."*

Nobody had apologized for anything all night. It was the first apology, and it was the right one.

---

*Language notes.* *Wolem wä Atanvi*: *wä* "against" normally causes lenition, but *A* does not change. *Snolaytx* is the specific verb for losing a game or a duel, and with nothing named as lost the subject stays plain (canon: *Ayoe snolaytx fìtrr*, "We lost today"). *Oel tsat omum* keeps its object because "I know" refers to something specific; *Oe noläk* without an object would be generic. *Nitram* is felt, not been: *oe 'efu nitram*. Sänrri puts the topic first, the way a Japanese speaker sets down は.
