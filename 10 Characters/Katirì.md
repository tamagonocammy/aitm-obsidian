> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Family:** Sister of [[Waynhì]], mate of [[Tswesya]], mother of [[Oarä]]
> **Personality Snapshot:** Kind and outgoing. Introduced to music young by sister [[Waynhì]]. Passion for percussion connecting with Eywa. Uses rhythm to teach spiritual heritage. Works with [[Syulang]] to help [[Txewan]] overcome anxiety. Despite accommodating nature, has assertive side - playful tsundere towards sister, [[Tswesya]], or [[Tìfyaw]] when antics go too far. Complements Tswesya's reserved nature with outgoing energy. Rhythms inspire connection and appreciation of Eywa.

## Profile

**Personality:** Katirì te Hafyon Tsìeya'ite learned rhythm before she learned words. Her sister Waynhì introduced her to music young, and it became her language: the way she connects with Eywa, the way she teaches younger Na'vi about their spiritual heritage, the way she makes sense of a world that can feel overwhelming in its complexity. Percussion is her gift. Not just the technical skill, though that's considerable, but the way her drums seem to sync with heartbeats, the way her rhythms create space for people to breathe, to move, to *feel*. She uses music to ground others, and in doing so, grounds herself. Warm and luminous, Katirì brings balance wherever she goes. She's kind without being a pushover, nurturing without being overbearing. Her natural impulse is to support, to create safe spaces, to help people grow. She works alongside Syulang to help Txewan overcome social anxiety, not by forcing exposure but by building environments where Txewan can practice being seen without fear. During rehearsals with the Woven Harmony, Katirì provides the foundation. Her steady rhythms become the heartbeat that allows Txewan's melodies to soar, Tswesya's instrumentation to shine, Syulang's harmonies to weave through it all. She's the one who notices when Txewan's hands start trembling, who adjusts the tempo without drawing attention to it, who knows exactly when to pause and when to push forward. The Woven Harmony is where Katirì's truest self emerges. This circle of chosen family (herself, her mate Tswesya, her best friend Syulang, and the anxious composer they've all adopted, Txewan) exists outside the structures of exile or warfare that define so many clan bonds. Here, Katirì's outgoing energy complements the four of them so perfectly and thoroughly that even if they don’t know, they notice her. She draws Tswesya out of their shell without demanding they change. She gives Syulang someone who matches her enthusiasm without competing with her warmth. She reminds Txewan, through presence more than words, that being vulnerable and being weak are different things entirely. But Katirì has her limits, and she's learned to assert them. Her sister Waynhì, Tswesya, Tìfyaw: when their antics go too far, when tradition becomes rigidity or when laziness becomes irresponsibility, Katirì's playful tsundere streak emerges. Sharp words delivered with affection, eye-rolls that somehow still convey care. She loves them, but she won't be taken for granted. Her bond with Tswesya is particularly meaningful, a partnership grounded in mutual respect and shared passion, where her outgoing nature balances their reserve and their quiet depth balances her sometimes overwhelming energy. They fit. The work of fitting continues, but the foundation holds. Their daughter Oarä is a wildchild who treats the most dangerous peaks of Sheltering Falls like a playground, and Katirì vacillates between pride and terror watching her disappear into the wilderness. She tries to instill caution, tries to teach her that bravery and recklessness are different things, but Oarä has inherited too much of Tswesya's independent spirit and Katirì's own stubborn determination. So instead, Katirì teaches her rhythm, watches her tap out complex beats on rocks and hollow logs, and trusts that the music they share will always call her home. Katirì's rhythms don't just entertain. They inspire connection, harmony, and deeper appreciation of Eywa. When she teaches, young Na'vi learn that music and spirituality speak the same language, that Eywa's voice comes through in beats and silences as much as in words. And in the quiet moments, when the Woven Harmony gathers after the clan has gone to sleep, when her drums echo through the mist-shrouded chambers of Sheltering Falls, Katirì feels it. The heartbeat of her chosen family. The rhythm of home.
