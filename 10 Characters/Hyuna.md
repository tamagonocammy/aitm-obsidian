## Profile

Hyuna was the radiant, terrifying heart of the ANAKT Fireteam: a paradox of brilliant light and lethal shadow. Her Recombinant resurrection preserved her most fundamental essence: a vast, blinding love for the idea of life, and a protective drive so fierce it curdled into a pure, ecstatic joy for combat. Unlike her comrades, haunted by ghosts or consumed by obsession, Hyuna fought with a dancer's grace and a composer's passion. She did not see battlefields; she saw stages. A worthy opponent was not an enemy, but a partner in a sublime, violent dialogue, a chance to test the magnificent limits of the form Dr. Phan had built for her, a form that proudly integrated the prosthetic leg from her first life into a weapon of elegant, crushing kicks. Her smile, bright and unwavering even in the heat of battle, was her banner. She wore the Mangkwan clan’s ashen paint not as a tribal mark, but as a warrior's signature, a bold declaration of her presence. This radiant exterior, however, hid a cavernous, wounded core. Her one fatal orbit, her "only weakness," was Luka. He was the man who had killed her twin brother, Hyunwoo, in the Gardens. He was the living embodiment of her greatest failure to protect, and yet, the gravitational center of her universe. Her feelings for him were a tangled, poisoned knot, a suffocating mix of survivor's guilt, a desperate need for his approval, and a twisted love she could neither sever nor fully embrace. To fight beside him was to constantly press on a bruise, a pain that felt like the only thing that made her real. Her final duel during the **Siege of the Falls** was her transcendence. Matched against Kaymi for hours, she found a moment of shocking purity. Stripped of the RDA's agenda, Luka's manipulations, and the ghost of her brother, the fight became everything she ever truly wanted: an honest, glorious, and mutual test of spirit and skill. When Kaymi's final blow landed, Hyuna did not fall with regret. She met her end with a breathless, exhilarated laugh, a final, genuine compliment to her opponent's technique on her lips. At that moment, the Astikasya did not see a Recombinant blasphemy. They saw a warrior's soul, unbroken and true. In a gesture of supreme respect, they granted her the honor reserved for their own most valiant: a hero's burial. They understood that while her war was wrong, her heart, in its own fractured way, had fought with absolute conviction. Her story ended not as a tragedy of the hollow, but as the redemption of the warrior who loved the fight itself more than the cause.
