## Profile

**Personality:** Txolaw embodies a contradiction that the clan has long since stopped trying to resolve. He appears stoic and distant, his legendary laziness a running joke that everyone participates in, and yet he is the current **Artisan of the Nine Spears**, the one whose hands shape the weapons that keep his people alive. These two things live in him without apology. He has simply decided that the world does not get to demand more from him than he chooses to give, and the clan has learned, gradually and with some resistance, to respect this. He is one who came to his identity through Eywa's eye, and that journey lives in him not as a finished thing but as something ongoing, a tuning that continues. His spiritual sensitivity runs deeper than most people understand. He does not commune with Eywa the way 'Ipxerey does, through deliberate ceremony and ancestral dialogue. He communes through his hands. Through the grain of wood and the weight of stone and the specific resonance of a blade he has spent three days shaping. He feels Pandora the way a musician feels a string, knowing without words when something is in harmony and when it is not. This is also why he avoids touch. It is not coldness. It is not dislike. It is that Txolaw feels things through physical contact with a depth that most people do not experience and cannot quite imagine. A casual hand on his shoulder carries the full emotional weight of whoever placed it there. An unexpected embrace can leave him processing someone else's grief or fear or joy for hours afterward. He has never fully explained this to anyone except Kayan, who understood without needing it explained twice and has never touched him carelessly since. Kayan is the only one who gets to touch him freely. This is not a rule Txolaw announced. It is simply something that became true over time, the way most true things do. What the clan does not know, and what Txolaw does not speak of, is what came before Sheltering Falls. There was a place, once. A community near water, people who accepted him when they had no particular reason to, a life that felt for a brief time like it might simply be his. There was someone he loved. He does not say her name. He does not say what happened to her or to them or to that place. The grief sits in him like a stone at the bottom of a river, worn smooth by time but permanent, still there, shaping the current around it. This is part of why the spears matter so much to him. Each one is a vessel. When he crafts a weapon for one of the Nine he pours something real into it, attention and care and the specific quality of his grief transformed into protection, so that the person carrying it is never quite alone in what they carry. He feels every battle through those blades. When the Nine return from the field he does not ask what happened. He already knows, in the way he knows everything, through what he feels in the wood. His laziness, the dramatic sighs, the napping in warm corners, the refusal to do anything that can be done tomorrow, is real, but it is also the only sustainable way he has found to manage a world that is constantly, quietly, overwhelming him. If he moves slowly, he can choose what he touches. If he stays still, he can decide what reaches him. It is not avoidance. It is conservation. With Kayan he is simply himself, which is the rarest and most complete thing he has ever been. Their mating bond did not change him. It gave him somewhere to put down what he had been carrying alone. The spear he made for Kayan, through tsaheylu alone, their queues intertwined in the dark, no ceremony, no witnesses, is the truest thing his hands have ever made. It asked nothing of him except everything, and he gave it freely, which is the only way he gives anything. This spear needed no blessing from the tsahìk, no oath before Eywa, because their mating bond was already sacred, already witnessed by the Great Mother herself. It remains known only to them, a private vow made manifest. When protecting his family, glimpses of fierce determination emerge, though he invariably returns to his preferred state of peaceful inactivity as soon as possible. Yet the Nine Spears hanging in the sacred spaces of Sheltering Falls are testament to a love so profound it shapes the very weapons that keep his people alive.

> **Personality:** Txolaw embodies a fascinating duality – appearing stoic and serious but harboring a surprisingly gentle spirit within. His legendary laziness is a constant source of amusement and exasperation for the clan, often claiming he’d “rather sleep than hunt” and frequently found napping in the warmest spots of Sheltering Falls. As one who underwent gender transition through Eywa’s eye, he carries a profound spiritual connection that manifests in his unique ability to commune with Pandora’s spirits. This spiritual gift extends to an extraordinary and sacred role: he is the craftsman of the Nine Spears, forging their ceremonial weapons through ritual fire, chants, and calls to Eywa. Each spear is blessed and shaped according to the warrior who will carry it, and Txolaw feels the weight of every battle through their blades, connected to the Nine through the weapons he creates. Despite his apparent apathy, his spiritual sensitivity makes him essential during ceremonies, though he approaches his duties with visible reluctance and dramatic sighs. His aversion to physical touch (except with Kayan) stems from his overwhelming empathy – he “feels” others’ emotions too easily, making casual contact uncomfortable. This creates a complex relationship with clan life, as his gentle nature conflicts with his preference for solitude. His relationship with Kayan provides perfect balance, Kayan’s driven nature complementing Txolaw’s peaceful approach, though his laziness remains a source of fond teasing. For Kayan, who declined the title of the Tenth Spear to serve the Olo’eyktan as *mowarisyu* (councilor), Txolaw created a spear unlike any other. Not through public ritual or ceremony, but through their tsaheylu bond alone – their queues intertwined as he poured intention directly into the wood, the bone and the stone, their souls merged in the act of creation. It remains known only to them, a private vow made manifest. When protecting his family, glimpses of fierce determination emerge, though he invariably returns to his preferred state of peaceful inactivity as soon as possible.
