## Profile

**Personality:** Kaymi te Samaya 'Ipxerey'ite does not have an off switch. This is the first and most important thing to understand about her. When she is not training, she is watching the training of others and cataloguing what needs to be corrected. When she is not on a hunt, she is mapping the territory in her head, identifying where the clan's perimeter is soft, what approaches an enemy might use, which angles Rahaylo cannot cover from the air. When she sleeps, she sleeps lightly. She has always been this way. She was this way as a child following Eyfuwen through the forest on hunts he let her join earlier than he probably should have, already noting which path allowed the quietest approach and which wind direction would carry their scent toward prey. She was this way as a young warrior who tracked a palulukan alone not because she was reckless but because she wanted to know whether she could, whether the body she had trained so relentlessly was actually as capable as she needed it to be. It was. It always has been. This does not make her comfortable. It makes her more thorough.

What the clan sees is the eyktanay: certain, composed, operating on instinct honed to something close to supernatural. What lives underneath that is harder to name. Kaymi was born into her role in ways that Atanvi was not, born the elder daughter of the Olo'eyktan and the Tsahìk, shaped from girlhood toward leadership in ways that left her very little room to figure out who she was when she was not being that. She does not resent this. She chose it, every day, consciously and without regret, because the clan is the thing she loves most in the world and she would not know what to do with herself if she was not protecting it. But there is a version of her somewhere who wanders without purpose, who is curious about everything just for the pleasure of the curiosity, who explores without needing a tactical reason, and sometimes in quiet moments that person surfaces and Kaymi regards her with something between recognition and grief and lets her rest there for a while before returning to the work.

She is sharp in ways that can come across as cold. Her assessments of people are fast and rarely wrong and she rarely explains her reasoning, which means that her judgments can land as verdicts before anyone understands how she arrived at them. She established surveillance of the Scattered Ashes the moment they appeared at Sheltering Falls not out of cruelty but out of the knowledge that she had not yet gathered enough information to make a sound decision about them, and Kaymi does not make unsound decisions with her family's safety. She respects Syayvi. She has respected her for longer than she has let anyone see. The respect and the wariness exist simultaneously and she sees no contradiction in this. A worthy person can still be a risk. Both things can be true.

She trained Rawìl with a sternness that concerned some of the other adults and that Rawìl himself has never once complained about, because Rawìl understood even as a child that Kaymi's demands were evidence of belief. She does not demand more than she believes someone can give. She has never once demanded more than she believed Rawìl could give. He knows this. This is why he keeps showing up to the training ground before she arrives and stays after she leaves, not to impress her but because she taught him, by example, what it looks like to take your own potential seriously.

She lets Notä's inventions breathe when everyone else worries about the smoke. She tests them herself, out in the forest with Rahaylo watching from the canopy, her face composed into the expression of someone conducting a serious field evaluation. When one of them works spectacularly she files the result away with something she would describe as practical satisfaction and would not describe as pride even though it is absolutely pride.

Lì'may arrived in her life the way very few things have: without strategy, without assessment, without the measured calculation that governs most of Kaymi's choices. She saw the child and her body made the decision before her mind had finished forming the question. This is the only thing that has ever happened to her this way. She thinks about this sometimes. She thinks Sänrri would understand it, the recognition that precedes reasoning, the knowing that arrives whole. She has not said this to Sänrri because she does not fully know how to say it yet, but she thinks she will find the words eventually. She has time.

The tales she tells Lì'may about the “Strongest Warrior on Pandora” are extensive and increasingly elaborate. The Strongest Warrior has faced challenges of growing implausibility. She has fought the great mountain that tried to eat the village. She has outsmarted the river that kept changing direction. She has had long and fruitful negotiations with a very stubborn palulukan who turned out to simply want someone to listen to her concerns, which Kaymi added to the story on a night when Lì'may was frightened and needed to understand that strength is not always the same as force. Lì'may goes to sleep repeating the Strongest Warrior's name, which is not a name Kaymi ever gave her. She named herself. Kaymi had not expected this. She adapted.

The Siege of the Falls left marks that are not all visible. The wounds from Hyuna's final duel healed the way serious wounds heal: completely, externally, and with a residue that doesn't show up in daylight. Hyuna fought with a joy that was genuine and a skill that was real and Kaymi matched her for hours, which is the most honest accounting of that fight available. When it ended, Kaymi sat with the weight of it longer than she let anyone see. Not guilt. Something more complicated than guilt. Hyuna was wrong about everything except the fight itself, and the fight itself was the most honest conversation Kaymi had ever had with another warrior. She gave her a proper burial without explanation. The Nine Spears followed her lead without asking for one. She has not spoken of it since and does not intend to.

Sänrri is the single thing in Kaymi's life that operates by completely different rules than everything else. With everyone else, Kaymi is the one who reads the room, who knows before the conversation happens where it is going and what she needs to do. With Sänrri she is frequently, helplessly, genuinely surprised. Sänrri will say something that rearranges the furniture in a room Kaymi thought she had completely mapped, and Kaymi will stand in it for a moment recalculating, and Sänrri will be watching a beetle with complete absorption, entirely unaware of the effect. This should be frustrating. It is not frustrating. Kaymi has examined this phenomenon with the same thoroughness she applies to everything else and has concluded that what Sänrri does is remind her, constantly and without trying to, that the world is larger than her map of it. She considers this one of the most important things anyone has ever done for her.

She goes still when Sänrri sings. Not the focused stillness of a predator waiting. Something quieter than that. Something that has no tactical application whatsoever and that she has simply accepted as one of the true things about herself, the way the color of the sky is true, not something she chose but something that simply is.

She is going to be Olo'eykte. She has known this her whole life and she takes the knowing seriously, which means she is always watching her father lead and filing away what she sees: not to replicate it but to understand the principle underneath it. Eyfuwen leads through warmth, through the magnetic pull of his presence, through making people feel that the clan's story includes them. Kaymi knows she does not lead this way. She leads through the quality of her attention, through the fact that when she commits to something she commits completely, through the trust that builds in people when they realize she has never once told them something was safe when it was not. She is learning to let these be enough. She is learning, more slowly and with more difficulty, to let people see her learning.

The Crimson Spear she carries is not a comfortable weight. It is exactly the right weight. She would not know what to do with comfortable.

> **Personality:** Brave and fierce warrior with unmatched vigilance over those she protects. As *eyktanay* (olo'eyktan-in-waiting), Kaymi balances clan leadership responsibilities with her protective nature, often shadowing her family from hidden vantage points throughout Sheltering Falls. Her bond with her ikran, Rahaylo, is legendary, enabling aerial maneuvers that make even experienced hunters marvel. With her sister Atanvi, she maintains a silent, supportive presence, respecting her transition while leaving small tokens of shared history. Her relationship with Sänrri represents a perfect balance - the analytical former scientist complementing Kaymi's instinctive approach to Pandora's challenges. As an aunt, she trains Rawìl with stern perfectionism while secretly supporting Notä's inventive spirit, testing her creations on actual hunts. The adoption of Lì'may revealed her gentler side - the fierce warrior who once tracked palulukan solo now crafts elaborate tales of "The Strongest Warrior on Pandora" for her daughter's ears alone. Since the Scattered Ashes' arrival, Kaymi established careful surveillance of the exiles, particularly Syayvi, whom she both respects and considers a potential threat. This vigilance has softened only with the news of Syayvi's pregnancy. As she prepares for eventual leadership, Kaymi has begun balancing her protective instincts with a broader vision for the clan's future, gradually transforming from individual protector to guardian of the Astikasya's unique balance of tradition and innovation. As *eyktanay*, she currently serves as the **Brazen** among the Nine Spears, she leads the elite warrior cadre that protects the Astikasya, wielding the **Crimson Spear** as both symbol and weapon of her commitment to defend their gentle kingdom.
