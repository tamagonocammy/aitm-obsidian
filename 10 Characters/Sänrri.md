> **Personality Snapshot:** Former dreamwalker who fully embraced Na'vi spirituality. Autistic traits include collecting leaves/rocks, powerful singing voice. Bridges human knowledge with Na'vi wisdom. Completely abandoned scientific worldview for pure spiritual faith.

## Profile

**Personality:** Sänrri te Tsawtral Hikari'ite is the heartbeat of the Astikasya told in the most unlikely voice. She is small and often quiet and will sometimes stop mid-sentence because a particularly interesting rock has caught her attention on the path. She has been known to miss the beginning of her own ceremonies because Zìsya found something in the forest and Sänrri needed to investigate it too. She collects things obsessively and without apology: leaves pressed between woven strips, stones of a specific satisfying weight, shells with surfaces that feel exactly right beneath her fingertips. Lì'may helps her now, solemn and intent, bringing specimens for Sänrri's approval with the gravity of someone conducting sacred work. This is, in Sänrri's view, precisely correct. It is sacred work. Everything is, if you pay enough attention.

 She was not always this certain. The person she was before she was Sänrri spent years feeling like she had arrived in the wrong body in the wrong world speaking the wrong language, close enough to everything that mattered to see it clearly and just far enough away to never quite touch it. That distance was her defining experience for a long time: watching belonging happen to other people, not understanding what she kept doing wrong, writing poems about the gap between what she felt and what she could make anyone understand. She was told she was strange. She was told she was difficult. She internalized both with the thoroughness of someone who genuinely believes the people describing her must know better than she does.

She does not believe that anymore. She learned not to believe it here, on Pandora, in the moment her queue first touched the roots of Eywa and something vast and warm and entirely real said, without words, that she had been understood all along. That moment did not fix everything. She still blames herself when things go wrong around her. She still stutters when she is nervous and goes nonverbal in crowds and has to leave ceremonies briefly sometimes because the sensory weight of it all becomes too much. But she knows, now, that these things are not evidence of wrongness. They are just the shape of how she moves through the world, and Eywa made the world to hold every shape.

What she became in the years after that first communion is something the clan struggles to put into words that feel adequate. She leads ceremonies with a voice so large it seems impossible it comes from someone her size, filling the mist-shrouded chambers of Sheltering Falls until the sound has weight, until people feel it in their chests before they process it with their ears. She composes songs the way she once wrote poetry: from the inside out, from the exact center of what something feels like, and the result lands in listeners with an intimacy that can be startling. Clan members have cried at ceremonies they have attended dozens of times before, simply because Sänrri found a new angle into the same song and the new angle was the one that went straight through them.

Under 'Ipxerey's guidance she has grown into her role as tsakarem with a seriousness that surprises people who first meet her in the middle of a hunt for a perfect-feeling stone. She is thorough. She is patient in ways that seem impossible given how quickly she becomes fascinated by things. She sits with people in their grief without trying to move them out of it, which is rarer than it sounds. She knows how to hold space that does not have a shape yet. This is her deepest gift: she has always been able to sit inside something unresolved and not panic, to wait for it to tell her what it is. Eywa, it turns out, communicates the same way.

She knows what it is to not belong anywhere and then to choose a place so completely that the choice becomes who you are. This is why the clan's lost and wandering find their way to her specifically, because she does not perform welcome. She simply is welcoming, in the same way a root is strong, not because it is trying to be, but because it grew that way. The Cohort found her when they were still learning what home meant in this body in this world. Sänrri did not attempt to explain it. She sat with them in the not-knowing, which is worth more.

 Her speaking voice is still soft and still sometimes stumbles, and she still does not always make eye contact when she is thinking hard. Her singing voice is something else entirely and always has been, a total contradiction of her speaking register, enormous and sure and capable of carrying a silence into sound so smoothly that listeners cannot identify the moment the room changed. Kaymi has heard her sing thousands of times and still goes quiet in a particular way when it happens, a stillness she would describe as paying attention and which Sänrri privately recognizes as something closer to awe, which she finds both touching and extremely funny.

Kaymi is the love of her life in the form of a person who would also die for her on any given afternoon without being asked, which Sänrri considers both a gift and a mild ongoing concern. They balance each other in the way that two completely different instruments can balance in a song: not by being similar, but by each providing exactly what the other cannot. Kaymi moves on instinct and does not second-guess herself. Sänrri second-guesses everything, including her second-guessing, and has made peace with this as simply the price of the kind of attention she pays. Together they are formidable. Apart, briefly, they are both slightly worse at everything, which is something they have both noticed and neither has said aloud.

Lì'may was not a decision Sänrri made carefully. She saw the child and knew, the way she knows when a stone is right, not through analysis but through some immediate and total recognition that she has long since learned to trust. Lì'may treats the world with the same combination of fearlessness and complete earnestness that Sänrri herself moves through ceremonies, which Kaymi finds deeply amusing. Sänrri watches her daughter declare herself the strongest warrior on Pandora and carefully does not let her face do the thing it wants to do, which is break open with pride so enormous it has no dignified expression.

Her relationship with Rängay is the great disciplining force of her spiritual life. Rängay holds the tradition like a standard and never lowers it, and Sänrri has not always met it, and Rängay has not always found graceful ways to tell her so, and they have come through all of that into something neither of them would call easy and both of them would call essential. Sänrri leads the ceremonies. Rängay corrects her when she is wrong. Neither of them would change this arrangement. What Sänrri knows, and has never said to Rängay because she is not sure Rängay would know what to do with it, is that being held to that standard is one of the primary ways she knows she belongs.

 She takes night flights with Zìsya when she needs to think clearly. Pandora at night, from above, is the closest thing in this world to what she once sought in the dark of a closed room filled with stars: the sensation of being very small inside something very large that is not threatening, only vast, only full. Zìsya, her ikran, is the only creature Sänrri has ever known who shares her specific relationship with the texture of silence, companionable and content. They go out into the dark together and come back with whatever needed to settle having settled. Kaymi no longer worries when Sänrri does not return until the bioluminescence is dimming toward dawn. She used to. She has learned that Sänrri always comes back, because Pandora is where she belongs, and the people in it are who she belongs with, and she is not lost anymore.

She does not think of herself as the heart of the clan. She would deflect the suggestion with something self-deprecating and then get distracted by a beetle. But the clan knows what it has in her. 'Ipxerey knew it first. Kaymi knows it with her whole body. The Cohort knows it in the particular way that people who were found by someone know the shape of the hand that found them. Lì'may knows it the way small children know essential things, without words, as a given, as gravity.

Sänrri te Tsawtral Hikari'ite. Her name means starlight. She picked it, alone, on the night she first looked up at Pandora's sky as something that had truly chosen to stay. It is the most accurate thing about her. Not a sun, not a fire, not a blaze. A steady light in the dark. There whether you look for it or not. Older than any of the stories told about it. Still there.

> **Personality:** In her life as Na'vi, Sänrri evolved from her former self as Dr. Tomori Takamatsu, retaining core traits while growing into new strengths. Once a quiet, introverted scientist who struggled with self-confidence and social interactions, she transformed after defecting from the RDA Sci-Ops to join the Na'vi, following the path of Jake Sully, the Sixth Toruk Makto. Since childhood, she had never quite felt “human” - a disconnection that finally found resolution when she fully connected with Eywa. As a human, she found comfort in collecting specific items - band-aids with animal designs, slippery objects, perfectly-sized things, and natural specimens. She would seek solace in planetariums and aquariums when anxious, a habit that translated into taking night flights with her protective ikran, Zìsya. Though her speaking voice was soft and often accompanied by a stutter, she possessed a remarkably powerful singing voice and expressed herself through poetry and musical compositions. Now as Sänrri, she has grown into a more confident and flirtatious personality while maintaining her gentle nature. Her autistic traits express themselves through her continued love of collecting, now focused on leaves, rocks, and natural specimens which she weaves into her clothes. Upon experiencing true communion with Eywa, she completely abandoned her scientific worldview, embracing pure spiritual faith and intuitive connection to Pandora’s living network in her role as tsakarem (tsahìk-in-waiting) under 'Ipxerey’s guidance. Her powerful voice, once used for analytical poetry, now leads clan ceremonies with joyful songs celebrating Eywa and her chosen family. She is deeply protective of her family, especially her mate Kaymi and their adopted daughter Lì'may, channeling her former tendency to blame herself into a fierce dedication to those she loves.
