> **Personality Snapshot:** Gentle and maternal. Early life marked by tragedy - birth clan scattered by conflict. Raised alone during long hunts, leading to abandonment fears. During imprisonment, unconsciously mimicked Mangkwan ceremonies before catching herself - horrified by own adaptability. Struggles with fluctuating identity.

## Profile

**Personality:** Pxayu built herself into a monument against abandonment. Her gentleness, her nurturing, her perfect adaptability are all arguments against a truth seared into her childhood: you were left because you were too much to love. When her birth clan scattered, that message became proof. So she learned to un-learn her own needs. She became what people wanted before they knew they wanted it. Finding Sänrri's cohort felt like finding home. Here were women also searching, also broken. Pxayu saw her purpose clearly: be the anchor, be the nurturer, be so essential they cannot leave. Her bond with Apayì became everything. In Apayì's witness, she saw someone who wouldn't shatter when she showed her need. Finally, she could believe: you can stay if you make yourself necessary enough. But beneath her calm exterior, tsunamis move. Her emotional world runs on abandonment calculus. A missed glance is evidence of imminent departure. A quiet day is the prelude to being forgotten. She doesn't lose herself in others so much as dissolve her boundaries as an offering, building a cage of perfect care designed to make her indispensable. When triggered, she splinters. The person she loves becomes a betrayer. The cohort that felt permanent becomes fragile and moments from shattering. She cycles between desperate clinging and terrified withdrawal. Her training under 'Ipxerey became less about spirituality and more about excavating her core wound. The Tsahìk recognized what Pxayu truly was: not broken, but brilliantly constructed as a survival mechanism that had stopped working. Apayì became her witness through this slow, agonizing process. Every time the old terror rose that she was too much, Apayì would simply see it. Not fix it, not reassure her away from it, but acknowledge it: I see that you're scared. I'm still here. Her capture by the Mangkwan clan confirmed every fear. Separated from her cohort, torn from Lawarey, Pxayu's survival mechanisms activated with devastating precision. She mimicked the corrupted ceremonies, adapted perfectly to hell itself, because that's what she'd been built to do. The horror wasn't that she was corrupted. It was that her own machinery for preventing abandonment worked flawlessly in a place designed to destroy identity. It proved what she'd always believed: there was no core Pxayu, only empty rooms she built for others to occupy. It was Apayì's voice that kept her tethered. A laugh remembered, a glance across the darkness, the knowledge that someone knew her completely enough that even broken and shape-shifted into heresy, she was still worth saving. Apayì's witness didn't ask her to stop adapting. It saw the crying child beneath the architecture and loved her anyway. Now, returned to Sheltering Falls, Pxayu is learning to build on a completely new foundation, and the work is daily and brutal and sacred. Some mornings she wakes convinced Apayì has left. Some days she performs perfect motherhood for Lawarey, terrified that showing actual need will drive her away. Some moments she catches herself mirroring Sänrri and freezes, wondering if anything authentic is left. But she's learning that Lawarey's love isn't a prize for perfect mothering. It's given to the crybaby she hides. She's learning that saying "I'm terrified you'll leave" is more powerful than any perfect performance. She's learning that Sänrri doesn't need her to be stable. She needs her to be real. Her healing is the slow, terrifying work of believing that her cohort stays for the noise of her, not the silence she constructs. They choose her neediness. They choose the parts that are messy and desperate. They choose the crybaby. Pxayu is learning to build a home where the foundation isn't "you need me so you can't leave," but something more terrifying and beautiful: "I need you, and that's okay." Her presence is no longer an argument against abandonment. It's a quiet, defiant act of faith: building something beautiful with someone, knowing it could all be taken away, and choosing to build it anyway. She still fails sometimes. She collapses into old patterns, performs perfection until she shatters, mistakes kindness for pity. But she's learning to hold her wounds gently, to see her desperate need for belonging not as pathology but as capacity for love. And in the quiet moments, when Lawarey rests against her and Apayì's hand finds hers, Pxayu dares to believe something impossible: *I am not the abandoned one. I am home. And I'm allowed to stay.*
