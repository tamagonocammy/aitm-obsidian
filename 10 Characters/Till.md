## Profile

**Personality:** Till was not a berserker. He was a walking contradiction of sacred love and profane rage, a lullaby forever looping inside an engine built for slaughter, each note a wound that never closed. His neural backup performed a cruel miracle: it perfectly preserved the single most tender memory of his human life: a fragment of a song his mother, Io, sang to him as a baby, her voice warm and infinite. Then the same backup grafted that pristine memory onto the searing trauma of her loss, his father's abuse, and the violent, dependent love he felt for Ivan. The RDA created something impossible: perfect preservation alongside perfect devastation, forever intertwined. The result was a consciousness perpetually short-circuiting between two extremes. The gentle hum of that forgotten lullaby became a constant, maddening tinnitus in his Na'vi skull: a ghost he could never console and could never silence. It was the sound of a love that no longer had a home, and it made him insane in ways that went beyond madness. He didn't just hear it; he felt it, vibrating through his neural pathways like a wound that kept reopening. He fought not with strategy, but with desperate, kinetic urgency, a primal need to make the outer world scream as loud as his inner one did. Every explosion was an attempt to drown out the hum. Every brutal charge was a plea for a feeling strong enough to override the memory. He was chaos incarnate because his mind was a perpetual civil war between the infant being lullabied to sleep and the man who'd been broken by the universe. He smeared the Mangkwan clan’s ash not as a uniform, but like a child finger-painting in a fit, carelessly and desperately, screaming "**LOOK AT ME**" to a universe that had made him a ghost. His fixation on Ivan was a doomed search for an anchor, for the one person whose own obsessive order might somehow counteract the chaos in his head. Ivan understood strategy, understood control, understood the weight of holding things together. If Till could orbit him perfectly enough, maybe the lullaby would stop. Maybe the noise would make sense. When Ivan was killed, that last potential circuit breaker blew. His final rampage through the Astikasya workshops was not a tactical move or an order being followed. It was the lullaby's crescendo, reaching a fever pitch that no amount of destruction could actually silence. He was trying to annihilate the song by becoming one with total, annihilating noise, destroying the clan's heart just as his own had been destroyed. Every workshop he burned was a prayer. Every tool he shattered was a scream. When Tsa'rawm and Ketxe cornered him in the flooded workshops, he wasn't facing soldiers or enemies of the RDA. He was facing the living embodiment of the familial love and protection his memory taunted him with: Tsa'rawm with his blood-siblings and mate and children, Ketxe who had broken free from chains to choose her own love. They were everything Till could never be. The brutal, spiritual mutilation of his death wasn't just vengeance for the clan: it was mercy. Kuru severed by the Scarred Spear, Till's consciousness scattering into the aquifer, and in that final moment of dissolution, the chainsaw's roar drowned out the hum. For the first time in years, there was absolute silence. The Thornbriar was finally, blessedly, still. In the sudden quiet, the lullaby stopped. And Till, at last, could rest.
