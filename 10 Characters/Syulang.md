## Profile

**Personality:** Syulang te Ayfomä Kxuryo'ite has never met a gathering she couldn't improve or a conflict she couldn't gently defuse. She moves through the clan like sunlight through mist: warm, pervasive, impossible to ignore. She organizes ceremonies, makes sure the elders have comfortable seating, checks that the children aren't getting into too much trouble, and ensures everyone feels valued and seen. She's the shortest of the parent generation, a fact that delights the younglings and occasionally frustrates her, but what she lacks in height she more than makes up for in sheer force of care. The clan still talks about how she delivered Mìyo and Mìrì naturally, without remedies or assistance, her stubbornness and optimism carrying her through. It became a story they tell to remind themselves that sometimes strength looks like endurance, like trusting your body even when it hurts. But beneath that sunny exterior, Syulang wrestles with her own quiet anxieties. She sees the unique talents around her (Txewan's compositions that make people weep, Katirì's rhythms that connect souls to Eywa, Tswesya's instruments that are works of art in themselves) and sometimes she wonders what she brings besides organization and enthusiasm. The voice is small, easily drowned out by her natural optimism, but it exists. And so she channels that uncertainty into becoming the best supporter she can be, the one who makes space for others to shine. She organizes the Woven Harmony's rehearsals not because she leads them, but because she genuinely loves seeing her chosen family come together. She smooths over Tswesya and Katirì's occasional creative disagreements with patience and humor. She sits with Txewan during anxious spirals, not trying to fix them, just being present. She turns every practice session into something more: a space where vulnerability is safe, where mistakes are learned, where the four of them are held by something bigger than any one of them could create alone. The Harmony is Syulang's heart made manifest. They laugh together, bicker gently, share comfortable silences, push each other to be better. Syulang thrives in this space. Here, her scattered energy becomes enthusiasm. Her tendency to talk too much becomes the bridge that helps Tswesya and Txewan feel less alone in their quietness. Her optimism, which could feel exhausting in larger settings, becomes exactly what the circle needs when creative frustration sets in. She works alongside Katirì to help Txewan overcome her social anxiety, and alongside Atanvi and Tsa'rawm when more serious intervention is needed. Her emotional intelligence is her greatest gift. She reads people instinctively, knows when to step in and when to step back, understands that sometimes the most loving thing you can do is simply show up. She mediates conflicts not by imposing solutions but by helping people see the good in each other, by creating space for reconciliation. Her gatherings and celebrations aren't frivolous; they're the threads that weave the clan together, reminders that even in the shadow of war and loss, there is still joy worth protecting. And at home, with Txewan and their children, Syulang allows herself to be imperfect. To admit she's tired. To let her mate's quiet strength hold her for once. To watch the twins finish each other's sentences and baby Netezu reach for sounds with his small hands, and feel, despite everything, that this (this messy, beautiful, chosen family) is more than enough.
