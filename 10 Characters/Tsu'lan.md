## Profile

**Personality:** Tsu'lan is the easiest person in the clan to underestimate in exactly one specific way: because he is so genuinely, visibly warm, because his laugh is so loud and his friendships are so real and his delight in physical challenge is so uncomplicated and infectious, it is easy to mistake the warmth for the whole of him. It is not the whole of him. Underneath the warmth there is something immovable, which is how the spear got its name, and which explains why he is the only one who can properly wield it. The immovability is not stubbornness. It is closer to the quality of deep roots, the kind that do not shift regardless of weather, that simply continue being what they are through everything that tries to change them.

 His settlement was destroyed when he was young. He does not carry this as a wound he keeps reopening. He carries it as knowledge: that things you love can be taken, that nothing is guaranteed, that the only thing that answers this fact is showing up. He wandered between clans for seasons after, learning combat from everyone who would teach him, taking what was useful and building something of his own from the pieces. The fighting style that resulted is genuinely distinctive, combinations that no single clan tradition would produce, power and fluidity working in ways that should contradict each other and do not. Young warriors trained under him are recognizable for their stance, which prioritizes protecting someone else above protecting themselves. He teaches this explicitly. He also demonstrates it constantly and without thinking.

His friendship with Tsa'rawm is the kind that requires no maintenance because its foundation is so complete. They established it in a viperwolf fight, which is on brand for both of them, and it has held through everything since without needing to be discussed or tended. They communicate in a language of physical presence, of showing up, of the specific trust between people who have been back-to-back and know exactly what the other is capable of in the worst possible circumstances. Tsa'rawm says goodbye by saying come back. This is more honest than goodbye and both of them know it.

He carries a gift for the creatures Pandora keeps at its most dangerous edges, something in his *tsaheylu* that reaches the parts of an animal's mind that are not rage but only caution raised to a fever pitch. He brings them down to calm. He does not fully understand how he does this and does not try to. Some things are simply true about a person and the understanding is not the point.

During the Siege he faced Luka in the Cave of Truth and he let him go. He has carried this decision every day since. Not with guilt exactly, but with the specific weight of knowing that a choice he made had consequences he could not fully calculate and that those consequences were real and ongoing. Luka's last words before disappearing into the Western Frontier were a warning, not a taunt, and Tsu'lan received them as such, which is perhaps the most Tsu'lan possible response to being warned by someone who had just tried very hard to kill him.

 After the Siege, he made a vow. He told no one he was making it. He did not need to. The vow was between him and Eywa and the weight of what had been done to his clan and the knowledge that Luka had said there are many of us and meant it.

 When he left to hunt, he checked his weapons in a particular way and left in a particular posture and the Nine Spears saw it. Kaymi saw it first. She could not stop him and she knew it and she let him go, which is one of the most difficult things she has done and one of the most important. Tsa'rawm said come back. Rawìl stood with him the night before and said nothing useful and everything necessary, which is exactly what was needed. He went alone. He did not stay alone.

 Yukeyo was waiting at the Bone Sanctuary because she is Yukeyo and she knew. She asked one question before agreeing to follow: is this justice or is this grief. He was quiet for a moment and then said both, because both were true and both were honest, and she nodded and packed. Both she could follow. The Flying Troupe heard the exchange and did not discuss it and were ready the next morning because they had heard who Tsu'lan was asking for and what he was going to do and they chose to be part of it without being asked. Paysìn knew where Luka was and had known for two days and waited until Tsu'lan was ready before saying so. This is the specific math of the found family that Luka could never calculate. There is no column for it.

 The Hunt rode nine. Nine ikran across the night sky, bioluminescence against the gas giant's amber, blue and green, wings on cold air and silence. Luka looked up and saw them and he ran, because he had calculated everything and the calculation said run. The calculation was correct about the running and wrong about the outcome. Tsu'lan does not make the same mistake twice.

 He came home with a fulfilled vow in one hand and a new weight in the other, because Luka's final words were the same as his words after the Siege, a warning delivered with his last breath: there are many of us. The Hunt ends but the war does not. This is the thing Tsu'lan carried back across the Western Frontier alongside everything else, the knowledge that what he did was necessary and complete and also not enough, because the thing they are fighting is larger than Luka and always was.

He brought Tsyokanì with him, the Ghost of the Northern Ravines, who had been a ghost long enough. This was not planned. It was the natural result of a spar in which something was established between them that could not be unestablished, a recognition of the specific grammar of what Tsu'lan was doing that Tsyokanì understood without needing it explained. Tsu'lan looked back at him at the edge of the ravines and put nothing demanding in the look and waited. It was enough. When Tsyokanì said he was finished there, Tsu'lan said *srane* and meant it and they came home together.

 He is learning, in the time since the Hunt, that a fulfilled vow does not resolve the weight that made it necessary. That justice and grief can both be true at the same time and that being right about both does not lighten either. He is learning this the same way he learns most things, by continuing to move, by continuing to show up, by continuing to be the specific thing he is regardless of what tries to change him.

 **The Immovable Spear** has his name on it because it is exactly accurate. The spear does not move. Neither does he. This is not a boast. It is simply what he is.

> [!quote]- Earlier version — AITM_06_Other_Characters_and_Lore
> **Personality:** Energetic, compassionate, and with an unshakeable sense of justice. Tsu'lan is known for his extraordinary physical strength and superhuman endurance, traits that make him a formidable hunter and warrior. However, his true strength lies in his generous heart and ability to connect with others. As a Na'vi, he has a deep connection with Eywa, but his approach is more intuitive than spiritual. Originally from a small outlying settlement that was destroyed in a territorial conflict with the RDA, Tsu'lan wandered between clans for several seasons before finding a home with the Astikasya. His journeys across Pandora gave him unique combat knowledge, having learned fighting techniques from multiple clans and adapted them into a distinctive style that combines power with unexpected fluidity. He now serves as the clan's foremost combat instructor, teaching the younger generations how to defend themselves and their territory. Tsu'lan firmly believes in protecting the weak and fighting against any threat to his people or Pandora. His friendship with Tsa'rawm is legendary, sharing a bond forged in countless hunts and adventures. Their first meeting during a dire hunt gone wrong – where they fought back-to-back against a pack of viperwolves to protect an injured clan member – cemented a lifelong brotherhood that rivals even formal blood-oath bonds. Despite his serious nature in battle, Tsu'lan has a playful sense of humor and enjoys physical challenges and friendly competitions, often organizing impromptu tests of strength that serve as both entertainment and training for the clan's warriors. He's especially skilled in tsaheylu with more aggressive Pandoran creatures, showing a unique ability to calm even the fiercest beasts. This rare gift has led to him forming bonds with animals others consider too dangerous to approach, including a particularly aggressive species of forest thanator that the clan now uses for perimeter defense. The exact nature of this unusual affinity remains mysterious, though some elders whisper it stems from an encounter with a rare plant deep in uncharted territory during his wandering years. Although not from the Sarentu clan, Tsu'lan has a natural gift for uniting people, often acting as a bridge between different families within the Astikasya and occasionally serving as an informal ambassador to neighboring clans. His complex relationship with the Scattered Ashes stems from having briefly traveled with them before they fully separated from the main clan. He understands their grievances while maintaining his loyalty to the Astikasya, making him one of the few who can speak openly with both groups without arousing suspicion. His teaching methods blend traditional Na'vi techniques with innovations of his own creation, emphasizing adaptability over rigid form. Young warriors trained by Tsu'lan are recognizable by their distinctive fighting stance that prioritizes protecting others above personal glory. His loyalty and willingness to sacrifice for others have made him a beloved and respected figure among the Astikasya Na'vi, with many young warriors seeking to emulate not just his combat prowess but his unfailing kindness. After his travels and his tireless work for the clan, he now serves as **the Righteous** among the Nine Spears, bearing **the Immovable Spear** that only he can properly wield, his unwavering sense of justice making him the protector of the weak and defender of the Astikasya's values.
