## Profile

**Personality:** There are two voices that live inside Txewan te Ngotxì Mitsìyo'ite, and they are always at war. The first is the stutter: sharp, involuntary, a betrayal of her body when the clan gathers and all those eyes turn toward her. It fractures her sentences, makes her shrink inward, makes her wish she could disappear into the mist like the phantom they named her. The second voice is the one that emerges when she sings. It does not stutter. It does not falter. It rises clear and unbidden, weaving the stories and sorrows of her people into melodies that make even the sternest elders pause and listen. This is the voice that earned her **the Singing Spear**, the one wrapped in songcords that hum when she moves, the one that coordinates warriors through sound and shadow during battle. This is the voice that made her one of the Nine, despite carrying the weight of feeling perpetually inadequate next to warriors who seem so certain of themselves. But long before the spear, before the title, before she was ever called **the Phantom**, there was the **Woven Harmony.** Txewan, Syulang, Katirì, and Tswesya: four souls bound not by exile or survival, but by the quiet, radical act of choosing each other. They are a refuge built in trust, a small circle where music flows as naturally as breath and silence is just as sacred. It was within this circle that Txewan first learned her voice (both of them) mattered. That her catastrophic spirals, her worst-case-scenario mind that spins and spins until she can barely breathe, could be held gently by people who understood. That Tswesya's dry humor could make her laugh mid-panic. That Katirì's steady rhythms could ground her when the world felt too loud. That Syulang's warmth could remind her she was loved even when she felt like a burden. They rehearse in quiet moments, tucked away in corners of Sheltering Falls where the bioluminescent vines glow soft and the sound of waterfalls drowns out the rest of the world. Txewan composes, her fingers trembling slightly as she works through a new melody, and Tswesya listens with that unreadable expression that somehow still feels like approval. Katirì's drums provide the heartbeat, the foundation, while Syulang hums along, her voice weaving effortlessly with Txewan's in a way that makes the anxious composer believe (just for a moment) that harmony is possible. These are the moments where Txewan becomes just **Txewì**, the nickname Tswesya gave her, the one that feels like home. She is deeply devoted to her family: Syulang, her beloved wife, whose cheerful presence keeps her tethered when anxiety threatens to pull her under; her bond siblings Atanvi and Tsa'rawm, who helped her become brave enough to transition through the Eye of Eywa alongside Atanvi, an experience that reshaped her understanding of herself; and her children. Mìyo and Mìrì, the twins whose matching voices make her heart ache with pride, and baby Netezu, whose quiet fascination with sound already mirrors her own. Motherhood terrified her. Still does, sometimes. The fear that she will never be enough, that her anxiety will somehow damage them, haunts her in the dark hours. But Syulang is patient. The Harmony is patient. And slowly, Txewan is learning to be patient with herself. Her music chronicles the clan's emotional life: the victories, the losses, the small joys that might otherwise be forgotten. She composes constantly, her mind always half-lost in melody, and when she takes a breath from creating she often looks to the stars, talking to Sänrri about the vastness of space and Eywa's infinite presence within it. When nervous, she fidgets. Kuru flailing in the wind, tail swishing and lashing out, fingers tapping out rhythms only she can hear. And sometimes, late at night when the clan sleeps and the mist curls thick around Sheltering Falls, she hums. Not for anyone else. Just for herself. Just to remember that her voice, in all its forms, still exists.
