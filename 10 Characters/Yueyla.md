## Profile

**Personality:** Yueyla has been the last of something for long enough that she has stopped expecting it to feel different. She was born Zeswa — plains blood, direhorse-raised, shaped by wind and open sky and the particular pride of a people who project their voices outward because the land demands it — and she remains Zeswa in her bones even now, even here, even when the clan that made her is reduced to the space between her ribs where grief lives. The Mayfam family name she still carries is not sentiment, but precision. It is the most accurate description of what she is: the last daughter of Eysi, of a lineage the RDA unmade with the particular indifferent thoroughness of a machine that does not know what it is destroying and would not care if it did.

  

She came up as a warrior the way Zeswa warriors do — on the back of a pa'li before she could properly hold a spear or a bow, in formations built for open ground, learning to read wind and grass and the behavior of prey across distances that would make a forest-born Na'vi dizzy. She was good at it. She was, if she is being honest about herself in the quiet moments she rarely permits, exceptional at it, and she knew it then with the uncomplicated confidence of someone who had not yet learned how much can be taken from a person. She has learned now. Several times over. She carries the education in the hollow of her right eye socket, covered always by the cured leather patch she has worn so long it has shaped itself to her face — a record of the first partner she could not save, the first in a line that grew long enough to become its own kind of identity. She has been the survivor so many times that she stopped understanding herself any other way.

  

What the eye means in Na'vi terms is the thing she does not discuss and has not discussed with anyone except Kayan, who already knew before she could have told him, because Kayan is the kind of person who understands certain weights without being told their name. The missing eye means she cannot bond fully on her right side. The tsaheylu, when she makes it, opens only halfway — a partial circuit, a song with one voice missing. She can bond. She does bond. But there is an asymmetry to it that she feels every time, a reminder stitched into the most sacred act of Na'vi life that she gave something away and did not get it back. She does not frame this as a loss. She frames it as payment, and she believes the payment was fair, and she has made her peace with it in the way she makes peace with most things: by not looking at it directly and continuing to function.

  

Her relationship with Kayan is the oldest living thing in her life. They grew up in the orbit of each other through the Great Games and the alliances that bind Zeswa to the wider Western Frontier, and what passed between them then was the specific warmth of two serious young warriors who recognized each other as serious, which is rarer than it sounds and matters more than people who have always had it understand. She watched him become the man he is now with the uncomplicated pride of someone who was there for the early drafts. She came to Sheltering Falls in the drifting, rootless season after her clan was gone because she had no direction that meant more than another, and Kayan's name was the only thing she had that was still attached to a living person. She did not ask to stay. She simply did not leave. The Astikasya, with the particular grace of people who understand what it is to be without a home, did not ask her to explain herself. Eyfuwen looked at her once with the eyes of someone who has made a study of grief and said nothing, and Yueyla understood that as the most generous thing anyone had offered her in years.

  

She is not easy company. She is warm in the way that something which has been very cold for a long time is warm when it finally encounters heat: genuinely, but with an edge that reminds you of what preceded it. She drinks zangke with a dedication that the clan's elders find concerning and that she considers reasonable given the circumstances. She jokes in the dry, plains-flat manner of the Zeswa — deadpan, oblique, timed perfectly, never explaining the joke because if you need the explanation you are not the intended audience. She is unexpectedly good with young warriors, the ones who are old enough to understand what real danger means and young enough to still be afraid of it, because she does not pretend the fear away and she does not perform bravery, and they can tell. She tells them: *live long enough to regret something.* It is the most honest advice she has. It is the advice no one gave her when she was their age. She does not know if it is wisdom or just the thing she tells herself, but the distinction has begun to feel like a question for a calmer season.

  

She has not bonded with an ikran since her last one died in the RDA engagement that finished her family name as a plural thing during the destruction of the Celebration Arches. She is aware this is a problem. She is working up to it. The Astikasya have, with characteristic tact, stopped asking directly and started simply making sure she knows where the ikran roost.
