## Profile

**Personality:** Sua was a ghost haunting a weapon, her heart and mind perfectly preserved by a technology that understood nothing of her soul. The RDA's neural backup captured her essence with cruel precision: her cold indifference forged by a neglectful guardian who taught her that isolation was safer than connection, her deep-seated fear of being hurt that made her push everyone away except two people: her younger brother Ivan and her partner Mizi, and the singular, all-consuming devotion that defined her entire existence. She loved completely or not at all. There was no middle ground, no safety in moderation. As a Recombinant, this translated into lethality of a uniquely intimate kind. Her innate evasiveness and desire to be unseen became a supernatural gift for infiltration and silent execution. She moved through spaces like she was apologizing for existing in them, her footsteps barely disturbing the air. She refused all Mangkwan ash paint, not just for stealth, but because it was an affiliation, a connection to a group, that her guarded nature inherently rejected. Paint was claiming. Paint was belonging. And Sua had learned long ago that belonging only meant eventual abandonment. She did not kill from a distance. She killed face-to-face, with bladed weapons, moving close enough in her final moments to search her victim's eyes. It was as if in severing a life, she was desperately seeking a recognition or understanding that her own fractured existence denied her. During the Siege, Sua moved through the passages of Sheltering Falls like a phantom, hunting the children she'd been assigned to eliminate. She moved without hesitation, without mercy, executing her orders with the cold precision of someone who'd learned that emotion was a liability. This was what she was built for. This was the only thing she was good at. Until she found them. Syulang emerged from the mist with her twins and her baby boy, and for just a moment, Sua faltered. Not out of compassion, but out of recognition. Here was a family unit. Here was the connection she missed. Here was everything Sua had sacrificed by choosing isolation. For that moment of hesitation, she threatened them. Her blade came up, her voice cold as winter ice, and Syulang's maternal terror was palpable. A brief, desperate clash ensued as the caretaker tried to protect her children, trying to shield them from the thing that had come to take them away. But then Txewan came. The fight that erupted between Sua and Txewan in those mist-shrouded corridors was a conversation between two reclusive souls who spoke the same silent language of anxiety and careful observation. Two people who'd learned to disappear, to move through the world unseen, to understand that visibility was a threat. The Clematis and the Phantom met in near-absolute silence, their duel almost quiet in its brutality. Two warriors moved with minimal wasted motion, their blades singing not in triumphant arcs but in precise, calculating strikes. Each movement was surgical, economical, designed not for spectacle but for absolute, efficient lethality. Sua moved like water, searching for the patterns in Txewan's defense. Txewan moved like smoke, refusing to be pinned down, refusing to let Sua find solid ground. In that mist-shrouded passage, they recognized something in each other that no one else could name. For just a moment, before the killing stroke came, there was understanding. There was the acknowledgment of two broken things that had learned to survive by being invisible, and now faced each other in perfect clarity. When Txewan's strike found its mark, Sua's death was swift. But the secret of her end, the identity of her killer and the quiet understanding that passed between them in that final moment, became a stone in Txewan's heart. A burden of mercy and guilt carried in whispered, broken Na'vi apologies to empty air. Txewan would never tell. Some silences are sacred. Mizi would never know that her beloved had faced another person who understood her completely in the instant before ending. She would only know that Sua was gone. Her death shattered two worlds simultaneously. Ivan, the younger brother she learned to vanish to protect, was left with only a silent, screaming absence in the neural link. The connection that had anchored him fractured, leaving him untethered and desperate for anything loud enough to drown out the void where her presence should be. The loss of Sua sent him spiraling into obsession and rage, his strategic mind fracturing because the one person who made him feel less alone was simply gone. Mizi, the woman who was her universe and her only safe harbor, was unmoored. The person she'd devoted herself to completely, the only one she'd allowed close enough to touch, was simply gone. Her duet was now forever a solo performance, a song forever incomplete. Sua, the quiet girl who loved observing the stars, who loved Mizi with a fierceness that terrified her, who had spent her entire existence learning to disappear so completely that even in death she would leave only silence behind, became the first ghost of the Siege. A silent casualty whose absence screams louder than any battle cry, haunting every life she touched. She exists now in the spaces between words, in the things people don't say, in the guilt that Txewan carries like a prayer, in the void that swallowed Ivan's stability, in the harmony that Mizi sings alone to the mist, in the children that Syulang was able to protect because Sua hesitated for just one moment too long. The Clematis grew in darkness, clung to those closest to her, and was cut down in silence by someone who understood her completely. And the world is diminished for her passing, even as it is saved by her death.
