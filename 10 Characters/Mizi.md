> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Callsign Meaning:** Empathy surviving death
> **Status:** Defected after learning Sua's death during Siege. Last seen wandering Spires alone, unable to join either side. Too far from Earth, rejected by Mangkwan as failure, dismissed by RDA as compromised, persona non grata to Astikasya. Exists in spaces between, helping children regardless of species. Resistance and Astikasya keep tabs. Sometimes voice heard in mists - second voice, softer/fainter, joins in harmony.
> **Personality:** Gardens anomaly - empathy conditioning never fully erased. RDA called it "processing error" in neural backup. Truth: some things survive death. Recombinant resurrection brought all human grief intact, all capacity for care/connection making her team's heart and greatest vulnerability. Fights not from enjoyment or programming but terror of losing more people. Manic protectiveness - doesn't just defend positions, shields. Siege assignment: nursery containment. Facing [[Atanvi]] changed something - saw another fighting from love. [[Atanvi]] speaking gently about children's names, favorite foods, fears made Mizi see them as people. Wore Mangkwan paint reluctantly (necessary uniform). Fight with [[Atanvi]] burned her, covering matching tattoos she shared with [[Sua]] in life.

## Profile

**Personality:** Mizi was a soul that survived the crucible only to be forged into a wound. Her neural backup performed a cruel alchemy, preserving the very essence that made her Mizi: her profound, self-sacrificing empathy, her bright love for Sua, and the deep-seated belief that she was a survivor who brought only pain to those she cherished. The RDA dismissed this as a "processing error," but they were wrong. It was the core truth of her being, replicated perfectly, which made it infinitely worse. As a Recombinant, this translated into fragile, furious protectiveness. She fought not for a cause or mission objective, but to shield the last remnants of her "squad," to keep alive the one person who made her existence feel less hollow. Her love for Sua was a tether she clung to with desperate strength, the only thing anchoring her consciousness to something resembling purpose. Everything she did was to protect that bond, to ensure Sua survived, to prove that she could be the protector her guilt insisted she'd failed to be. Her fate was sealed in the mist-shrouded passages of Sheltering Falls. Mizi was moving through the corridors, trying to reach the nursery, trying to complete the mission even as everything inside her screamed that the battle was already lost. The neural link was fractured, Hyuna's presence suddenly absent, and she could feel the cascading failures rippling through their team consciousness like cracks spreading through ice. That's when she found her. Sua was lying in the narrow corridor that led to the children's sanctuary, her body sprawled across the wet stone like discarded poetry. There was a knife wound, precise and devastating, right where her heart should be, as well as her kuru being cut. And her eyes were open, looking at nothing, seeing nothing, finally at peace after a lifetime of searching for connection in all the wrong places. For a moment, Mizi's entire system stopped. The neural link didn't need to confirm anything. She could see it. Her Sua. Her love. Her tether to something resembling humanity. Dead. The scream that came out of her was not a sound a Recombinant should be able to produce. It was primal, raw, the sound of something fundamental breaking inside her consciousness. She knelt beside Sua's body, her hands shaking, trying to find some sign of life, some proof that this wasn't real. But Sua was gone. And she'd died in the corridor that led to the children. She'd died protecting them, even in the end. Then Till's final rampage reached them. A secondary explosion, meant for Tsa'rawm and Ketxe in the workshops below, tore through the mountainside with indiscriminate fury. The blast radius was catastrophic. Stone and fire and shrapnel erupted upward, consuming the mist, consuming everything in its path. Mizi was caught at the epicenter, her body shielding Sua's corpse from the worst of it, as if even in death she could still protect her. The fire seared her skin with agony that threatened to shut down her entire neural system. The tattoos that mirrored Sua's, the marks that bound them together even in resurrection, were obliterated. Not erased neatly, but destroyed with brutal imprecision, leaving only burned, twisted flesh where identity used to be. A physical branding of her stolen past, her connection to Sua literally written in ash. She dragged herself up from the explosion, her vision blurred by pain and grief, and that's when Atanvi found her. The stuttering warrior, concerned for the children beyond, reaching out to help the Recombinant soldier despite everything. For Mizi, cradling the body of the woman she'd loved, watching Sua's blood mix with her own on the scorched stone, watching Atanvi extend her hand in kindness to the thing that was supposed to kill her, it was too much. The cognitive dissonance was unbearable. This wasn't mercy. This was a mockery of everything she'd failed to protect. Survival instinct and a lifetime of learned betrayal merged into something primal and violent. She lunged to kill the witness to her absolute vulnerability, to destroy the kindness that made her feel things no Recombinant should be able to feel. But her movements were uncoordinated, her systems screaming in pain, her consciousness fractured between grief and survival. Her foot slipped on the wet, blast-blackened rock. For just a moment, her momentum betrayed her. Atanvi, reflexively defending herself, pivoted, and suddenly Mizi was falling into the void, tumbling through the mist-choked chasm below. She survived not through grace or redemption or the hand of Eywa, but through blind, irrational luck and the fact that her Recombinant body was built to endure things that would destroy the average Na'vi. She crashed through bioluminescent vines, struck stone, fell further into the depths until she landed in water so cold it nearly stopped her heart. Broken, burned, and hollowed out by the confirmed death of Sua, she dragged herself from the depths through sheer instinct and the stubborn refusal of a body designed not to quit. Her defection was not a choice. It was the final state of a being with nothing left to lose. The RDA and the Mangkwan clan would execute her for failing to complete the mission. The Astikasya would execute her for what she'd been built to do. There was no side to choose because there was no side that wanted her. So she became a ghost of the Spires, belonging to nothing and no one. Her only remaining echo of empathy, the only part of herself that still functioned, became a silent, atoning vigil. She guides lost children home, regardless of species, regardless of whether they belong to the Astikasya or the humans or anywhere at all. It's not redemption. It's the only thing her fractured consciousness knows how to do with the love that's left. In the deepest mists, in the moments when she allows herself to exist rather than merely survive, she sometimes hums the melody of a duet from another life. A song she and Sua used to sing in the Gardens, before the RDA taught them what resurrection meant. The song is incomplete, halting, full of pain and memory and things that can never be recovered. Those who have heard it swear a second, fainter harmony weaves with hers in response. A second voice, softer but unmistakably present, joining in a harmony that spans the distance between life and death, between the living and the ghost. Some say it's imagination, grief made manifest. Some say it's Eywa herself, responding to a love too profound to be truly severed. Some say it's the forest recognizing kindred spirits, two broken things that refused to let fire and fall and death completely separate them. Mizi knows what it is. She knows that somewhere in the spaces between consciousness and the void, Sua still reaches back. That the connection they forged in life survived resurrection, and survives even now, a fragile thread that neither death nor defection nor distance can completely break. It's not enough. It will never be enough. But it's what remains when everything else is ashes. In the mists of the Spires, Mizi walks as penance and protection both. She will guide the lost home. She will shoulder the weight of surviving when Sua did not. And in the quiet moments, in the deepest fog, she will sing to the woman she loved and lost, waiting for that softer harmony to answer back. Always waiting. Always listening. Always, impossibly, still tethered to the irreplaceable half of her soul.
