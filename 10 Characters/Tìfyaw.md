## Profile

**Personality:** Tìfyaw is a contradiction that resolves itself the moment she picks up an instrument. In every other context, she is chaos given a face and a queue braid: scatterbrained, perpetually short on anything tradeable, constitutionally incapable of arriving anywhere at the expected time, and deeply, legendarily fond of fermented drink. She will borrow things and return them three seasons later without apology. She will show up to a ceremony with plant resin on her hands and a suspicious flush on her face and offer no explanation. She will start three sentences at once and finish none of them. The children of the clan find her tipsy antics delightful. Waynhì does not.

Then she sits down and plays. The atmosphere changes before her hands even move. Her eyes focus. Her whole body settles into something unhurried and exact. The loose, laughing presence vanishes and something much older and more serious takes its place. Every technique in her playing is self-invented: her style blends traditional Na'vi sounds with rhythms she worked out alone over years of private experimentation, using a pick shaped like a traditional bachi that no one else uses quite the way she does. The music that comes out is technically flawless and emotionally enormous. People forget to breathe. This is the real Tìfyaw, and the chaos around her is not a contradiction of it. It is the shell the music lives inside.

She was born into the Zeswa clan, and she showed extraordinary musical talent young. The problem was that early talent in someone otherwise withdrawn and awkward created a strange isolation: people wanted to hear her play but not spend time with her. She was treated as an instrument rather than a person, which is a particular kind of loneliness. She had a reclusive, gloomy nature well into her youth, and she carried it like dead weight. She discovered that fermented drink burned away the part of her that cared what people thought and left behind someone freer and louder and genuinely present in a crowd. She named that state the Happiness Spiral and has defended it enthusiastically ever since. What she will only admit to people she truly trusts, and only sideways, laughing at herself first, is that without it she feels herself going quiet in ways that scare her. Waynhì understands this completely, and this knowledge lives between them without either of them naming it aloud.

Her instrument has a name. It is a very long name. It is a ridiculous name. She is completely serious about it. The children know the name by heart.

Her friendship with Tswesya is one of the foundational constants of her life. They share a kindred spirit built around causing problems for their respective partners, borrowing things from people, and showing up uninvited to Katirì and Waynhì's spaces for various purposes that never quite hold up to scrutiny. They both reliably suffer the consequences of this behavior and reliably learn nothing from it. It is one of the purest friendships in the clan.

Her mentorship of Txewì is quieter and more serious than anything else she does. She recognized something in Txewì that she has never been able to fully name, a version of the person she was before she found her way out, and she has been showing up for her ever since. She does not give speeches. She attends every performance. She appears with poignant, perfectly timed advice that no one expected from her given the circumstances, and then immediately does something to undermine the gravitas. She told Txewì once not to stress over her future too much, which was the most useful thing anyone had said to Txewì in years, and then borrowed two things from her before leaving. She spotted Txewì's talent when no one else had articulated it clearly, and she has been its most reliable witness.

The gender-affirming communion with Eywa that she suggested to Txewì and Atanvi was not a revelation she presented with ceremony. She mentioned it once, practically, when it became relevant, because she had done it herself years before either of them were old enough to ask. She does not make speeches about this either. It is simply part of her history with Eywa, a private conversation that changed the shape of her life, and she passes that door open quietly for anyone who needs it.

Her relationship with Waynhì is the great anchor of her adult life, though she would phrase this differently and with more jokes. Waynhì is exasperated by her approximately seventy percent of the time. The other thirty percent, she looks at Tìfyaw with an expression she does not allow anyone to see. Tìfyaw knows this and exploits it with complete sincerity. The truth underneath the teasing is that Waynhì has always been there, steady and solid and furious, and Tìfyaw has always needed exactly that. She was the one who first brought them together as a band, as a family, and she has never forgotten that the structure of her life was built because Waynhì chose to stay in it.

Her bioluminescence pulses with rhythm when she plays, not a steady glow but something that moves with the music, as if the sound is passing through her skin on its way out. Her queue braid is always tied with a strip of woven material that Waynhì made for her. She does not go anywhere without it. The mandala marking she keeps refreshed on the back of her right hand is her own design, painted and repainted over years with near-religious dedication. Spider lily patterns appear in her body paint, always applied loosely, always slightly asymmetrical, never centered, never matching side to side. She does not plan this. It just always comes out that way.

> **Personality:** Tìfyaw is a charismatic and extroverted Na'vi, renowned for her ability to play traditional instruments with a unique and modern style. She's a musical innovator, blending ancient Na'vi sounds with rhythms inspired by human music she's heard. Her contagious energy and talent have made her a popular figure at clan celebrations. Despite her apparent confidence, Tìfyaw internally struggles with the pressure of maintaining her image and sometimes seeks solitary moments to reconnect with Eywa through her music. She mentors Txewan in music and life in general, and was the one who gave Txewan and Atanvi the idea to commune with Eywa to change their gender, something she did herself years ago. She's quite fond of alcohol, which annoys her partner, Waynhì, but amuses the children. This fondness for drinking often leads to comical situations, with Waynhì trying to keep her in check during clan gatherings, while the younger Na'vi find her tipsy antics highly entertaining.
