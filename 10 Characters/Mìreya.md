## Profile

**Personality:** Mìreya built something beautiful for one person and committed it to everyone. This is the truest summary of who she is, though she would object to it on the grounds that it makes her sound more heroic than deliberate, and she was very deliberate. She knew what she was building. She chose to make it bigger than it needed to be for the same reason she does most things, which is that doing something halfway has always felt to her like an insult to the work.

She is not easy. She is not easy in the way that people who have been used as objects are not easy, in the way that someone who grew up watching love weaponized learns to be suspicious of love being offered freely, in the way that years of being treated as a prize or a piece or a strategy rather than a person will make a person prickly and controlled and very slow to trust. She knows this about herself. She does not apologize for it. She simply tries, every day, to make sure that the armor is protecting something real and not just existing for its own sake.

Her mother's botanical knowledge lives in her hands. She has never fully sorted out how she feels about this, that the most intimate connection she has to a parent who died before she could be truly known by them is through the soil under her fingernails and the particular patience required to coax something green out of nothing. The greenhouses she maintains throughout Sheltering Falls are not sentimental in their function. They are practical and thoroughly documented and contribute meaningfully to the clan's medicine and food supply. They are also, in ways she does not discuss, a form of continuous conversation with someone she never got enough time with. She tends them alone in the early morning before the rest of the clan is moving and she does not consider this grief. She considers it care. She has come to understand that these are not opposites.

Her father is estranged and that is all she has to say about him and she has said it precisely once, to Atanvi, and will not revisit it. The clan has understood and respected this, partly because Mìreya's tone when she said it left very little room for follow-up questions, and partly because they are a clan that understands having parts of your history that you carry alone.

She declined the role of tsakarem when it was offered not from lack of qualification but from honest self-assessment: the spiritual path requires a particular kind of openness to Eywa's guidance that requires not needing to control the outcome, and Mìreya needs to control outcomes. She is aware of this. She has made peace with it as a feature of who she is rather than a failing, and she has found that the work she actually does, the strategy, the medicine, the quiet architecture of how the clan sustains itself, benefits from exactly this quality rather than being hindered by it. She is more useful here than she would have been there. This is a more comfortable truth than it used to be.

What Atanvi did for her, specifically, was refuse to accept the performance of not needing anyone as the final word. She kept showing up. She kept treating Mìreya as if the person underneath the control was worth waiting for, which turned out to be the one approach Mìreya did not have a defense against, because it was so fundamentally not what she expected from anyone that she did not know how to dismiss it. She let her in. She is still, sometimes, surprised that she let her in. She is never, not once, sorry about it.

Rawìl is her greatest strategic achievement and her greatest source of pride and she is absolutely aware that she is not supposed to think of her child as a strategic achievement, but Rawìl understands this about her and finds it funny rather than cold, which tells her something important about the person he is becoming. Notä is chaos in a body and Mìreya loves her the way you love something that contains its own weather system: with equal parts exhaustion and absolute wonder. She tests Notä's inventions when she thinks no one is watching. She is usually wrong about no one watching.

She considers Syulang and Rewon her sisters in every way that counts. Syulang because she is warmth that does not ask anything from you in return for existing, which Mìreya needed more than she knew how to ask for, and Rewon because she is precision and quiet dignity and the particular respect of someone who sees you clearly and keeps choosing to stand beside you anyway.

> **Personality:** Serious and professional with an unwavering determination to assert her independence, Mìreya maintains a carefully controlled exterior that masks a deeply tender and vulnerable core, channeling her brilliant strategic mind into her work as a talented tiretu and botanist who constantly seeks innovative ways to use Pandora's flora to benefit her family and clan. Her knowledge of plant medicine rivals that of the most experienced healers, a skill she developed partly to honor her late mother's botanical expertise, which she continues through maintaining extensive greenhouses throughout the settlement. Beneath her composed facade lies someone who struggled with trust and connection before finding her place among the Astikasya, her sharp intellect and natural leadership abilities making her an indispensable strategist alongside Rewon, though she tends to make quick decisions based on what she believes is best for others, sometimes without fully considering their feelings. This protective instinct extends especially to her wife Atanvi, whom she adores completely and views as her greatest source of strength and support, while in private revealing a remarkably affectionate and emotionally needy side that only her closest family witnesses. She has cultivated deep bonds with Syulang and Rewon, whom she considers sisters, maintains a particularly strong connection with her eldest son Rawìl, and despite being offered roles as tsakarem alongside Atanvi's potential eyktanay position, she respectfully declined, preferring to work behind the scenes while subtly positioning Rawìl for future leadership responsibilities. Her complicated relationship with her estranged father, whom she refuses to acknowledge, has left her fiercely independent but deeply committed to being the parent she never had, while her exceptional command of English, second only to Sänrri in the clan, and her quick wit make her both respected and beloved, though she's often lovingly teased by family members for her intensity and tendency to become surprisingly protective and clingy when truly comfortable.
