## Profile

**Personality:** Eyfuwen te Samaya Tìrol'itan is the kind of person who makes a room feel larger when he enters it. Not through force of presence in the way warriors have force of presence, not through command or gravity or the weight of authority, but through something more ordinary and more essential than any of those things: he is genuinely, completely glad to see you. Every time. Whether you are his eldest daughter or a youngling he met twice or a traveler who arrived at Sheltering Falls hungry and uncertain, you will receive the same thing from Eyfuwen, which is his full attention and the warm, uncomplicated impression that he was waiting for you to arrive.

He was not always Olo'eyktan. He earned it the way people earn things that suit them: not through wanting it but through being exactly who he already was, persistently, until the clan understood that was what they had been looking for. He is a mediator by nature and by practice, with the particular skill of someone who spent years coordinating complicated projects between people who disagreed about everything except the end goal. He finds the place where people agree before they know they agree, pulls it into the light, and builds from there. This is not manipulation. It is patient attention, which is different, and he would want you to understand the difference. 'Ipxerey has occasionally noted, with the dry precision she applies to most things, that Eyfuwen's approach and her former approach were not so unlike in their mechanics. Eyfuwen finds this observation funny. 'Ipxerey does not disagree with his finding it funny.

He is a storyteller in the deepest sense, meaning that he does not just tell stories at ceremonies. He thinks in stories. He organizes experience into narrative instinctively, which means that when something terrible happens to someone he loves he is already, somewhere in the back of his mind, finding the thread of it, the shape of what it might become when time has had its way. This does not mean he bypasses grief. He grieves with full presence, loudly when it is appropriate to be loud and quietly when quiet is what is needed, and he does not rush himself or anyone else through it. But he has always known, somewhere beneath the grief, that the story is not over, and this knowledge is not denial. It is a form of faith.

He carries one story he does not tell publicly, which is the story of a time before. He knows what it is to be interrupted at the wrong moment, to have the work feel more urgent than the person in front of you and to learn, too late, how wrong that hierarchy was. He knows what it is to carry the memory of a birthday that did not go the way it should have, a child who went looking for something because the adults were busy. He did not get to fix that in the life before this one. In this life he has fixed it, every day, deliberately and with full awareness of what he is doing. He does not miss his daughters' ceremonies. He does not miss their hunts when he is invited. He does not take the call when the people he loves are in front of him. This is not because he is perfect. It is because he remembers exactly what the cost of getting it wrong looks like, and he has decided that he will not pay it again.

This is the source of something that occasionally frustrates the more pragmatic members of the clan: Eyfuwen is hard to pull into urgency. He believes, with conviction born from loss and from the long work of rebuilding, that most things which feel urgent are not more urgent than the person standing in front of you. He has been wrong about this precisely twice. Both times he corrected course immediately and accepted the correction without making it about himself, which is one of the reasons the clan trusts him.

He is exceptionally, sometimes embarrassingly proud of his daughters. Kaymi's leadership fills him with a specific, almost overwhelming satisfaction, the kind that does not express itself in grand declarations but in the way he watches her work, quiet and attentive, occasionally catching 'Ipxerey's eye across the fire and exchanging the particular look of two people who cannot believe they made something this remarkable. With Atanvi he is more openly tender, something she accepts with the gracious awkwardness of someone who loves being loved but is not always sure what to do with the largeness of it. He has always known both of them completely, which is its own kind of gift, the knowledge that has no gaps or projections, only the actual people his daughters are.

Rawìl and Notä he loves with the particular indulgence of a grandfather who has already done the serious parenting and now gets to simply enjoy. He tells Rawìl the long versions of the clan's origin stories, the ones with all the detours and tangents, because he can see in the boy the same quality he sees in Kaymi, the mind that is always building maps, and he believes Rawìl's map of the clan should have texture and detail and the full weight of where it came from. He lets Notä explain her inventions to him at whatever length she chooses, asks precise and interested questions, and has only twice had to move quickly to avoid the results of something she was very confident about. He considers this a good record.

Lì'may calls him **Neyä**, which is not a Na'vi word and which Lì'may invented entirely on her own, and which Eyfuwen uses as his name with her without comment, because some things a person simply accepts when they are offered, especially when they are offered like that.

His relationship with 'Ipxerey is the love story of his life and he is completely aware of this, which means he tells her so more often than she strictly needs to hear it and she responds with varying degrees of patience depending on what she is in the middle of. He understands her in ways that took years to build and that required him to be a better listener than he was naturally inclined to be, because 'Ipxerey does not say the whole of a thing out loud and never has, and learning to hear what she means underneath what she says is the work of a lifetime. He considers it worthwhile work. He would do it again.

He sings. This is not a notable fact about him in the way that Sänrri's voice is notable, it is simply part of how he moves through the world, the way some people talk to themselves and some people tap rhythms and some people are never quite still. He hums while he works, sings to his ikran Torya during long flights, occasionally breaks into full song at celebrations in the way of someone who genuinely forgets to be self-conscious about it. Torya, who is old and has known him for most of Eyfuwen's adult life, has been observed singing back in her own fashion. Eyfuwen maintains that this is exactly right.

The Siege of the Falls aged him in ways that are visible if you know where to look. Not the fighting, he was not a warrior and did not pretend to be one during the Siege, but the days of not knowing who was coming back and from which direction and in what condition. He held the clan's center during those days, which meant he was the person others found when they needed somewhere to put their fear, and he held all of it without letting it collapse him, and when it was over and the cost was counted he went somewhere quiet for a long time before he came back to the fires. 'Ipxerey went with him. They do not speak of what was said there. What is visible afterward is that he carries the clan's grief differently now, more carefully, with the awareness of someone who has learned the exact weight of it.

He still organizes the celebrations. He still tells the long versions of the stories. He still makes every room feel larger. The grief and the joy are not in competition with each other. He has always known this, but now he knows it more.

> **Personality:** Charismatic, jovial, and open-hearted, Eyfuwen possesses a magnetic presence that attracts everyone around him, embodying the warmth and steadfast devotion that defined his original role as a loving father and dedicated family man. As the clan's Olo'eyktan, he's a natural storyteller able to captivate his audience with stories that interweave tradition and humor, his resonant laughter serving as a beacon of joy throughout the settlement during difficult times. Deeply connected to Eywa, he sees life as a precious gift to be celebrated and shared, approaching his leadership with the same protective instincts and selfless dedication that once drove him to sacrifice everything for his family's safety. His skills as a mediator reflect his background as a development manager who understood the importance of balancing different perspectives and finding solutions that honor both innovation and tradition, using his charisma and wisdom to resolve conflicts with grace and compassion. As a father, he's extraordinarily loving and encouraging, always pushing Atanvi and Kaymi to pursue their dreams while maintaining the kind of patient, nurturing presence that turns even ordinary moments into celebrations. His relationship with 'Ipxerey is one of deep respect and mutual love, complementing each other perfectly in their clan leadership roles, with his practical wisdom balancing her spiritual insight in ways that strengthen the entire community. Eyfuwen is renowned for organizing large feasts and celebrations, believing that unity is best forged through shared joy and understanding that bringing people together around food and stories creates bonds that transcend individual differences. Despite his naturally extroverted nature and gift for bringing out the best in others, he also values moments of quiet reflection, often seeking Eywa's guidance in the tranquility of the forest where his contemplative side emerges, revealing the thoughtful strategist beneath his jovial exterior who carries the weight of leadership with both humility and unwavering determination to protect those he loves.
