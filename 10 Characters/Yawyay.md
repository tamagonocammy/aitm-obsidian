## Profile

**Personality:** Yawyay, originally from the coastal clan Ta'unui, presents herself as friendly and good-natured, with a mischievous streak that surfaces in her playful teasing and habit of giving nicknames to her fellow Ashen members. Beneath her cheerful exterior, however, lies a soul shaped by devastating loss - her village was burned by Colonel Quaritch during the events of the second Pandoran War and the hunt for Jake Sully and his family, leaving her with a burning need for vengeance that eventually drove her to make a fateful decision. Unlike the others who were captured by the Mangkwan clan, Yawyay willingly sought them out, believing their rumored collaboration with human forces might provide her the means to strike back at Quaritch personally. However, witnessing Varang's corrupted spiritual practices and the true extent of their collaboration with the very forces that destroyed her home shattered her worldview completely. What she thought would be righteous vengeance revealed itself as the same destructive cycle that had claimed her family and village. Though she maintains her playful front and sharp intuition that others often underestimate, Yawyay now carries profound shame about her choices, particularly how her desire for revenge led her to align with those who perverted everything sacred to the Na'vi. During the Siege, Yawyay fought with reckless abandon born not from her usual mischievous energy but from desperate need to prove her loyalty wasn’t conditional, that choosing the Astikasya over the Mangkwan’s false promises of vengeance was the right decision, finally directing her “righteous fury” at actual threats rather than empty revenge. Her unpredictable fighting style, once a compensatory mask for deeper shame, became a genuine tactical advantage as she moved between targets with gleeful chaos that kept the Mangkwan raiders off-balance. Fighting in the final coordinated strike against Luka alongside the Nine Spears and her fellow Ashes, Yawyay experienced clarity she’d never known: she wasn’t fighting to avenge her burned village or prove herself worthy of belonging, she was fighting because these people were her new family, and that choice made all the difference. The Siege transformed her playful front from defensive mechanism into authentic expression, her shame not erased but transmuted into purpose, proving that even someone who willingly sought corruption could choose light when it mattered most. Her dynamic with Katirì as a smug, light-hearted rivalry masks deeper guilt about the path that brought her to the Scattered Ashes. Having grown up in a remote village far from the central territories, she compensates for perceived provincial origins through exaggerated confidence and careful attention to appearing sophisticated in her mannerisms, though this now also serves to hide her shame about having been willing to work with Quaritch's allies. Her loyalty to the Scattered Ashes before the Siege was upon a knife's edge - while she found genuine belonging with Syayvi and the others, she often voices criticism against their leader's decisions, partly from her own guilt about the compromises she was willing to make for revenge, though this attitude mellowed out after the Siege, where she took the decision to move back to her home clan alongside Tìmeyo as her new mate, swearing she would find her lost clan in the archipelagos near her home. Her drumming skills during ceremonies reveal glimpses of her true self, moments where her calculated persona falls away to show both her profound connection to traditional rhythms and the deep wounds that almost led her down a path of spiritual betrayal.
