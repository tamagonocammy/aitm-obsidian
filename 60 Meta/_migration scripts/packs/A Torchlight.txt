===== CHARACTER: Sänrri =====
> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Family:** Former human (Dr. Tomori Takamatsu), mate of [[Kaymi]], adoptive mother of [[Lì'may]]
> **Personality Snapshot:** Former dreamwalker who fully embraced Na'vi spirituality. Autistic traits include collecting leaves/rocks, powerful singing voice. Bridges human knowledge with Na'vi wisdom. Completely abandoned scientific worldview for pure spiritual faith.

## Profile

**Personality:** Sänrri te Tsawtral Tanhì'ite is the heartbeat of the Astikasya told in the most unlikely voice. She is small and often quiet and will sometimes stop mid-sentence because a particularly interesting rock has caught her attention on the path. She has been known to miss the beginning of her own ceremonies because Zìsya found something in the forest and Sänrri needed to investigate it too. She collects things obsessively and without apology: leaves pressed between woven strips, stones of a specific satisfying weight, shells with surfaces that feel exactly right beneath her fingertips. Lì'may helps her now, solemn and intent, bringing specimens for Sänrri's approval with the gravity of someone conducting sacred work. This is, in Sänrri's view, precisely correct. It is sacred work. Everything is, if you pay enough attention.

 She was not always this certain. The person she was before she was Sänrri spent years feeling like she had arrived in the wrong body in the wrong world speaking the wrong language, close enough to everything that mattered to see it clearly and just far enough away to never quite touch it. That distance was her defining experience for a long time: watching belonging happen to other people, not understanding what she kept doing wrong, writing poems about the gap between what she felt and what she could make anyone understand. She was told she was strange. She was told she was difficult. She internalized both with the thoroughness of someone who genuinely believes the people describing her must know better than she does.

She does not believe that anymore. She learned not to believe it here, on Pandora, in the moment her queue first touched the roots of Eywa and something vast and warm and entirely real said, without words, that she had been understood all along. That moment did not fix everything. She still blames herself when things go wrong around her. She still stutters when she is nervous and goes nonverbal in crowds and has to leave ceremonies briefly sometimes because the sensory weight of it all becomes too much. But she knows, now, that these things are not evidence of wrongness. They are just the shape of how she moves through the world, and Eywa made the world to hold every shape.

What she became in the years after that first communion is something the clan struggles to put into words that feel adequate. She leads ceremonies with a voice so large it seems impossible it comes from someone her size, filling the mist-shrouded chambers of Sheltering Falls until the sound has weight, until people feel it in their chests before they process it with their ears. She composes songs the way she once wrote poetry: from the inside out, from the exact center of what something feels like, and the result lands in listeners with an intimacy that can be startling. Clan members have cried at ceremonies they have attended dozens of times before, simply because Sänrri found a new angle into the same song and the new angle was the one that went straight through them.

Under 'Ipxerey's guidance she has grown into her role as tsakarem with a seriousness that surprises people who first meet her in the middle of a hunt for a perfect-feeling stone. She is thorough. She is patient in ways that seem impossible given how quickly she becomes fascinated by things. She sits with people in their grief without trying to move them out of it, which is rarer than it sounds. She knows how to hold space that does not have a shape yet. This is her deepest gift: she has always been able to sit inside something unresolved and not panic, to wait for it to tell her what it is. Eywa, it turns out, communicates the same way.

She knows what it is to not belong anywhere and then to choose a place so completely that the choice becomes who you are. This is why the clan's lost and wandering find their way to her specifically, because she does not perform welcome. She simply is welcoming, in the same way a root is strong, not because it is trying to be, but because it grew that way. The Cohort found her when they were still learning what home meant in this body in this world. Sänrri did not attempt to explain it. She sat with them in the not-knowing, which is worth more.

 Her speaking voice is still soft and still sometimes stumbles, and she still does not always make eye contact when she is thinking hard. Her singing voice is something else entirely and always has been, a total contradiction of her speaking register, enormous and sure and capable of carrying a silence into sound so smoothly that listeners cannot identify the moment the room changed. Kaymi has heard her sing thousands of times and still goes quiet in a particular way when it happens, a stillness she would describe as paying attention and which Sänrri privately recognizes as something closer to awe, which she finds both touching and extremely funny.

Kaymi is the love of her life in the form of a person who would also die for her on any given afternoon without being asked, which Sänrri considers both a gift and a mild ongoing concern. They balance each other in the way that two completely different instruments can balance in a song: not by being similar, but by each providing exactly what the other cannot. Kaymi moves on instinct and does not second-guess herself. Sänrri second-guesses everything, including her second-guessing, and has made peace with this as simply the price of the kind of attention she pays. Together they are formidable. Apart, briefly, they are both slightly worse at everything, which is something they have both noticed and neither has said aloud.

Lì'may was not a decision Sänrri made carefully. She saw the child and knew, the way she knows when a stone is right, not through analysis but through some immediate and total recognition that she has long since learned to trust. Lì'may treats the world with the same combination of fearlessness and complete earnestness that Sänrri herself moves through ceremonies, which Kaymi finds deeply amusing. Sänrri watches her daughter declare herself the strongest warrior on Pandora and carefully does not let her face do the thing it wants to do, which is break open with pride so enormous it has no dignified expression.

Her relationship with Rängay is the great disciplining force of her spiritual life. Rängay holds the tradition like a standard and never lowers it, and Sänrri has not always met it, and Rängay has not always found graceful ways to tell her so, and they have come through all of that into something neither of them would call easy and both of them would call essential. Sänrri leads the ceremonies. Rängay corrects her when she is wrong. Neither of them would change this arrangement. What Sänrri knows, and has never said to Rängay because she is not sure Rängay would know what to do with it, is that being held to that standard is one of the primary ways she knows she belongs.

 She takes night flights with Zìsya when she needs to think clearly. Pandora at night, from above, is the closest thing in this world to what she once sought in the dark of a closed room filled with stars: the sensation of being very small inside something very large that is not threatening, only vast, only full. Zìsya, her ikran, is the only creature Sänrri has ever known who shares her specific relationship with the texture of silence, companionable and content. They go out into the dark together and come back with whatever needed to settle having settled. Kaymi no longer worries when Sänrri does not return until the bioluminescence is dimming toward dawn. She used to. She has learned that Sänrri always comes back, because Pandora is where she belongs, and the people in it are who she belongs with, and she is not lost anymore.

She does not think of herself as the heart of the clan. She would deflect the suggestion with something self-deprecating and then get distracted by a beetle. But the clan knows what it has in her. > **Personality Snapshot:** Fierce warrior and vigilant protector. Legendary bond with ikran enables impossible aerial maneuvers. Balances protective instincts with vision for clan's future. Killed [[Hyuna]] during the Siege but was seriously wounded.

## Profile

**Personality:** Kaymi te Samaya 'Ipxerey'ite does not have an off switch. This is the first and most important thing to understand about her. When she is not training, she is watching the training of others and cataloguing what needs to be corrected. When she is not on a hunt, she is mapping the territory in her head, identifying where the clan's perimeter is soft, what approaches an enemy might use, which angles Rahaylo cannot cover from the air. When she sleeps, she sleeps lightly. She has always been this way. She was this way as a child following Eyfuwen through the forest on hunts he let her join earlier than he probably should have, already noting which path allowed the quietest approach and which wind direction would carry their scent toward prey. She was this way as a young warrior who tracked a palulukan alone not because she was reckless but because she wanted to know whether she could, whether the body she had trained so relentlessly was actually as capable as she needed it to be. It was. It always has been. This does not make her comfortable. It makes her more thorough.

What the clan sees is the eyktanay: certain, composed, operating on instinct honed to something close to supernatural. What lives underneath that is harder to name. Kaymi was born into her role in ways that Atanvi was not, born the elder daughter of the Olo'eyktan and the Tsahìk, shaped from girlhood toward leadership in ways that left her very little room to figure out who she was when she was not being that. She does not resent this. She chose it, every day, consciously and without regret, because the clan is the thing she loves most in the world and she would not know what to do with herself if she was not protecting it. But there is a version of her somewhere who wanders without purpose, who is curious about everything just for the pleasure of the curiosity, who explores without needing a tactical reason, and sometimes in quiet moments that person surfaces and Kaymi regards her with something between recognition and grief and lets her rest there for a while before returning to the work.

She is sharp in ways that can come across as cold. Her assessments of people are fast and rarely wrong and she rarely explains her reasoning, which means that her judgments can land as verdicts before anyone understands how she arrived at them. She established surveillance of the Scattered Ashes the moment they appeared at Sheltering Falls not out of cruelty but out of the knowledge that she had not yet gathered enough information to make a sound decision about them, and Kaymi does not make unsound decisions with her family's safety. She respects Syayvi. She has respected her for longer than she has let anyone see. The respect and the wariness exist simultaneously and she sees no contradiction in this. A worthy person can still be a risk. Both things can be true.

She trained Rawìl with a sternness that concerned some of the other adults and that Rawìl himself has never once complained about, because Rawìl understood even as a child that Kaymi's demands were evidence of belief. She does not demand more than she believes someone can give. She has never once demanded more than she believed Rawìl could give. He knows this. This is why he keeps showing up to the training ground before she arrives and stays after she leaves, not to impress her but because she taught him, by example, what it looks like to take your own potential seriously.

She lets Notä's inventions breathe when everyone else worries about the smoke. She tests them herself, out in the forest with Rahaylo watching from the canopy, her face composed into the expression of someone conducting a serious field evaluation. When one of them works spectacularly she files the result away with something she would describe as practical satisfaction and would not describe as pride even though it is absolutely pride.

Lì'may arrived in her life the way very few things have: without strategy, without assessment, without the measured calculation that governs most of Kaymi's choices. She saw the child and her body made the decision before her mind had finished forming the question. This is the only thing that has ever happened to her this way. She thinks about this sometimes. She thinks Sänrri would understand it, the recognition that precedes reasoning, the knowing that arrives whole. She has not said this to Sänrri because she does not fully know how to say it yet, but she thinks she will find the words eventually. She has time.

The tales she tells Lì'may about the “Strongest Warrior on Pandora” are extensive and increasingly elaborate. The Strongest Warrior has faced challenges of growing implausibility. She has fought the great mountain that tried to eat the village. She has outsmarted the river that kept changing direction. She has had long and fruitful negotiations with a very stubborn palulukan who turned out to simply want someone to listen to her concerns, which Kaymi added to the story on a night when Lì'may was frightened and needed to understand that strength is not always the same as force. ===== CHARACTER: 'Ipxerey =====
> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Family:** Mate of [[Eyfuwen]], mother of [[Kaymi]] & [[Atanvi]], grandmother of [[Rawìl]], [[Notä]] & [[Lì'may]]
> **Personality Snapshot:** Wise spiritual leader who transformed from vengeful strategist to compassionate teacher. Can communicate with ancestors through Tree of Souls. Her journey from darkness to light makes her uniquely qualified to guide others. Maintains keen strategic mind now used for building rather than destroying.

## Profile

**Personality:** 'Ipxerey became who she is now through a process she would describe, if pressed, as necessary and that she would not wish on anyone. She was not always the tsahìk whose voice carries the weight of genuine wisdom. She was not always someone whose guidance could be trusted to serve the person being guided rather than the guide's own ends. She was someone who loved her family with the whole of herself and then watched everything burn and then made the burning mean something by spending the next years of her life turning it into a weapon. The weapon was precise and patient and aimed at a specific target and she was very good at using it. She was also, in the process, doing to her daughters what had been done to her family: making them instruments of her purpose rather than people with their own.

She knows this. She has always known this, in the way that people who are very intelligent and very focused know the thing they are doing wrong and do it anyway because the goal feels more real than the cost. What she did not fully know, until Eywa made it undeniable, was that the cost was the point. You do not get to use people you love as pieces and also keep them whole. You have to choose, and she had chosen, and the choosing had consequences she had not let herself see clearly until she could no longer look away.

Her redemption is not a clean arc. She would be suspicious of anyone who described it as one. What happened was more like the slow process of a tree growing back after fire: it looks dead for a long time, and then one day something green appears, and then another day more green, and it is never quite the same tree that burned, but it is alive, and alive is not nothing. She learned to put what she knew, the strategic precision, the cold intelligence, the ability to see exactly how a system works and where to apply pressure, into the service of the people in front of her rather than the objective behind them. This is harder than it sounds. She still catches herself planning instead of listening. She still, occasionally, sees the board before she sees the person. The difference now is that she stops, and corrects, and asks the question she should have been asking all along: what does this person actually need.

Her ability to communicate with those who have passed through Eywa's network is not something she takes lightly or uses casually. It is the most sacred thing she does, and she does it with a reverence that surprises people who knew her before, who remember her as someone for whom strategy came before reverence. What she found, in the roots of the Tree of Souls, was that the people she had lost were not gone. They were changed. And what they carried into the network was not the shape of what she had wanted from them but the shape of who they actually were, which was sometimes different, and which she was learning, slowly and with difficulty, to love on their own terms.

Every time. ## Profile

**Personality:** Anxious and perpetually convinced that everything will go wrong (usually correctly), Kobeni brings her legendary bad luck and survival instincts to the Resistance as one of their most valuable intelligence assets. Despite being possibly the worst soldier in RDA Sec-Ops history, her paranoid attention to detail and intimate knowledge of corporate security protocols has made her instrumental in planning successful operations against her former employers. Her defection came after the Astikasya raid on Mining Outpost Delta-7, where her attempt to hide behind air filters led to an encounter with Sänrri - the first person to speak Japanese to her on Pandora, creating an instant connection that sealed her decision to abandon the RDA. Though she never became a skilled fighter, her strategic mind and ability to predict problems before they happen has earned her respect among the Resistance members. She maintains her characteristic nervous energy and tendency to overshare when anxious, but has found genuine belonging among people who value her honesty and paranoid insights. Her transformation from expendable corporate soldier to key Resistance operative proves that sometimes being "useless" in the wrong system makes you essential in the right one.

===== RELATIONSHIP LORE (sections) =====
### Kaymi & Sänrri
Protective force and vulnerable brilliance. Kaymi is the kind of warrior who does not need to justify her love, it manifests as action, as presence, as an ikran waiting outside a ceremony so that Sänrri does not walk out alone. Sänrri was a former dreamwalker who arrived fragile and brilliant and terrified of not belonging, and Kaymi chose her with the same ferocity she brings to combat. There is something in Sänrri that Kaymi recognizes as worth guarding at all costs. There is something in Kaymi that Sänrri found before she found herself. Their relationship with Lì'may is its own arc — the found daughter who made them a family before they fully realized they already were one.

### 'Ipxerey → Sänrri
The tsahìk and her tsakarem. 'Ipxerey recognized in Sänrri the specific quality of a soul that had crossed a great divide — a dreamwalker who had given up everything human to become something true. She did not make Sänrri's path easy. She made it possible. Sänrri is now the tsakarem, 'Ipxerey's heir in spiritual leadership. The relationship has evolved from guidance into partnership.

### Rängay & Kaymi — The Pact
This needs its own section because it is not a simple rivalry and it is not a simple resolution. Rängay and Kaymi clashed over Sänrri from almost the beginning. Rängay is a disciplined ritualist who earned her own identity through spiritual practice; she found Kaymi's instinctive, overwhelming approach to protection borderline heretical. Kaymi is a warrior who does not justify her love in words — she acts. For a long time each found the other's method inadequate. Their unspoken pact formed over time: Rängay's secret approval of the Kaymi/Sänrri union was the ultimate concession. She trusted the warrior to protect the songbird in ways ritual alone could not. The terms of the pact, never spoken aloud: *guard her with your life, or I will spend mine undoing you.* Post-Siege, this has deepened into something neither would name but both act on. In a crisis they operate as one without speaking. They still disagree on almost everything about the clan's spiritual health and still have sharp, frequent debates about it. The respect is enormous and completely unacknowledged.

### The Lost Cohort
Sänrri, Rängay, Pxayu, Apayì, Pamyaw. Formed around Sänrri's spiritual training under 'Ipxerey. Each arrived with a different wound and a different reason. They held each other through the Mangkwan captivity. They survived as a unit. Post-Siege, the Cohort's bonds are tested and deepened — trauma that would have scattered a group of strangers has instead braided them tighter. Pamyaw's version of belonging is appearing and disappearing as she pleases and being certain she will be understood when she returns.

> [!info] Synopsis written 2026-11-12 from the notes in this vault. Nothing in it is new canon; gaps are listed under Still open.

The Lost Cohort is the circle that formed around Sänrri while she trained as tsakarem under 'Ipxerey, after she arrived on Pandora as the dreamwalker Dr. Tomori Takamatsu *(The Dreamwalker Years)*. Each member came with a different wound and a different reason. The Mangkwan captured Sänrri, the Cohort and Txafwe and held them for months *(Rulings Log)*. They came home with Sänrri carrying the warning "They are coming" *(Scaled and Icy)*.
### Who they are
- [[Sänrri]]: the circle's center; tsakarem; the Relationship Lore counts her as a member, but her own note does not carry the group tag.
- [[Rängay]]: "Spiritual Musician"; stern ritualist; mate of [[Txafwe]], mother of [[Nìla]] and [[Mawpay]]; once at odds with Kaymi over Sänrri.
- [[Pxayu]]: "Nurturer"; mate and co-parent with Apayì, mother of [[Lawarey]]; afraid of being abandoned.
- [[Apayì]]: "Social Perceptive"; co-parent of Lawarey with Pxayu; posed as Mangkwan to get a message to [[Tsu'lan]].
- [[Pamyaw]]: "Wandering Spirit"; no mate or family given; joined because Sänrri "made things interesting".
### What defines them
They were formed around Sänrri's spiritual training under 'Ipxerey, and each member's profile calls them the "Original Sänrri Cohort" *(Relationship Lore, member profiles)*. They survived the captivity as a unit and, after the Siege, their bonds were "tested and deepened" *(Relationship Lore)*. In captivity they watched sacred rhythms twisted into heresy *(Scaled and Icy)*. Pxayu caught herself mimicking Mangkwan ceremonies, Pamyaw lost her instruments, and Apayì kept spirits up *(member profiles)*. Pamyaw's way of belonging is to come and go and trust she will be understood *(Relationship Lore)*.
