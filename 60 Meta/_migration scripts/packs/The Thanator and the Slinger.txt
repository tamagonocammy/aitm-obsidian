===== CHARACTER: Tsu'ong =====
> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Family:** Mate of [[Ketxe]], father of [[Eantu]] & [[Tulìpey]]
> **Personality Snapshot:** Transformed from arrogant warrior to wise counselor. Once challenged [[Atanvi]] for [[Mìreya]] (humiliated in defeat). Father's death and seeking uturu reshaped worldview. Advocates wisdom over violence. Brought Tipani combat techniques. Journey from arrogance to humility earned Nine Spears position. Pride cooled by experience became unbreakable wisdom.

## Profile

*Personality:* The thing most people do not know about Tsu'ong te Korhe Txep'itan is that the boy who came before the arrogant warrior was genuinely kind. Not soft. Not weak. Kind in the specific way of someone who could look at a stranger and immediately want them to feel welcome, who could extend warmth before he had any reason to. The arrogance came later, built by a father who treated him as a tool and a system that rewarded him for treating everything else the same way. By the time he walked into the Astikasya's territory and challenged Atanvi for Mìreya's hand as if Mìreya were a prize and Atanvi an obstacle, he had almost entirely buried the person he was born as under the person he had been shaped into. Almost.

  

Atanvi defeated him. This is the first fact in the story of who Tsu'ong became, and it is worth sitting with rather than moving past quickly. He did not lose to a superior strategy. He did not lose because conditions were unfavorable. He lost because Atanvi was better, and because the certainty he had built his entire identity on turned out to be a structure with no foundation. The humiliation was real. So was something else: the crack it made in the structure was the first honest thing that had happened to him in years, and somewhere underneath all of that pride he recognized it as such, which is the only reason the story did not end there with him simply rebuilding the wall higher.

  

His father's death is the wound that does not have a clean name. He killed him by accident, in a battle where neither of them knew who the other was, in the dark and the chaos of things that were already out of control. He is not innocent. He is also not guilty in the way that a deliberate act would be guilty. He carries both of these things simultaneously, which is harder than carrying either one alone. After it happened he was hollow for a long time. Not the arrogant hollow that had filled the years before, but something rawer and more genuine: a person stripped of everything he had used to avoid feeling the full weight of his own life. He spent time in that state. He watched someone die in his arms who was not supposed to die. He came back from it not by deciding the weight was acceptable but by deciding he had to carry it anyway because putting it down was not an option he was willing to choose.

  

He returned to his people. He sought uturu at Sheltering Falls from the very clan he had once treated as an obstacle between himself and what he wanted. He came with Ketxe and their children Eantu and Tulìpey, and he came without arrogance, which meant he came with nothing but the request itself. The Falls accepted them. This is still something he does not fully believe he deserved, and the disbelief keeps him honest in ways that the certainty never did.

  

His relationship with the Samaya family, and specifically with Atanvi and Mìreya, is one of the things he is most careful about. It began in shame and has traveled far from there, but the distance does not erase the starting point and he does not try to make it. What he has with them now is something closer to genuine admiration, the specific respect of someone who understands what it cost to become what they are because he paid a different version of the same cost. He does not speak of this often. It is simply present in the way he moves around them, in the particular care he takes to be useful rather than impressive.

  

Tulìpey falling for Rawìl is something he has made peace with in layers, over time. The first layer was the simple surprise of it. The second was something more complicated and also more honest, the recognition that the same qualities in Rawìl that he had once resented in Atanvi are the qualities that make Rawìl good for his daughter. He reached the third layer on his own, which is the quiet satisfaction of two families that once stood opposed building something between them that neither generation planned. He considers this one of the better outcomes available to a person who starts a story doing the wrong thing.

  

His Tipani combat techniques have not left him. What has left him is the willingness to reach for them first. He views violence now the way a person views a tool they know is dangerous: with respect for what it can do, with precision about when it is necessary, with the hard-won understanding that the most dangerous person is the one who fights by choice rather than compulsion. He is the second kind of person now. He has not always been. The distinction matters to him enormously.

  

The **Tempered Spear** he carries was given its name before anyone knew exactly what it would mean to him. Now he carries it as the most accurate description available. Wood that has been through fire does not simply survive it. It comes out changed in the specific way that the fire could not destroy: harder where it was soft, more reliable than before, marked in ways that are visible if you look closely. He looks closely. He is looking all the time now, at himself and at the people around him, in the particular way of someone who spent too long not looking and will not make that mistake again.

  

His relationship with Ketxe is the ground he stands on. Two people who became someone they were ashamed of, who chose to be better together, who do not require each other to be fully healed before being fully present. He would not describe their bond as simple. He would not describe anything about who he is now as simple. Simple was what he was before the fire. What came out of it is something that will hold.

  

During the **Siege of the Falls**, he fought not for the first time, and not for pride, and not to prove anything. He fought because the Falls had given his family sanctuary and the people trying to take it were going to have to go through him first. His Tipani techniques, which were once service for his own ego, became service for something that deserved them. This is what the **Siege** meant for him: not a redemption arc reaching its conclusion, but a single honest moment of being exactly what he had been trying to become. He does not think of it as proof. He thinks of it as practice. He intends to keep practicing.

===== CHARACTER: Ketxe =====
> [!abstract] At a glance

## Profile

**Personality:** Ketxe's true nature is layered and complex, belying her initially perceived gentleness. Born to the renowned **Tawkami clan**, known for their mastery of botanical alchemy, Ketxe diverged from her peers' healing focus to pursue a more... explosive path. While others in her birth clan cultivated medicines and salves, she became fascinated with the volatile compounds hidden within Pandora's flora, discovering which seed pods could create concussive blasts, which saps burned with colors that could blind, and which spores could create devastating chain reactions. Her "disappearance" from both her clan and from Tsa'rawm, her first love, was not the accident most believed. The truth, which she guards closely, is far darker: she was captured and controlled by Varang and the Mangkwan clan, her explosive expertise twisted into a weapon for their corrupted cause. Under Varang's manipulation, Ketxe conducted operations that still haunt her, her gentle nature warped by someone who saw her only as a tool. Her escape came at great cost: she fled into the wilderness, severing all connections to avoid putting anyone she loved in danger, carrying the shame of what she'd been forced to become. It was in this self-imposed exile that she found Tsu'ong, another soul broken by pride and loss. Together, they became each other's redemption: two people choosing to heal rather than destroy, to build rather than burn. Their bond is forged in mutual understanding: both know what it means to be someone you're ashamed of, and both chose to become better. When they sought uturu at Sheltering Falls with their children Eantu and Tulìpey, Ketxe had to face the ghosts of her past, including Tsa'rawm, the man whose heart she'd broken to protect him from Varang's reach, and the clan that had every reason to reject her. Her past connection with Tsa'rawm creates underlying tension, particularly with Rewon and Sänume of the Haykxa family, though time and honest conversation have begun to heal those wounds. During their brief time together, she taught him some of her explosive techniques, knowledge he's sworn never to reveal, a secret that binds them in complicated ways even now. Her relationship with the Haykxa family remains careful, built on mutual respect and the shared understanding that the past cannot be changed, only learned from. Her mysterious interest in old RDA explosives adds an intriguing element to her character, sparking both curiosity and concern. She studies human demolitions not to copy but to improve upon them using Pandora's natural chemistry, believing the Sky People's crude methods pale compared to what Eywa's own garden can produce. Her gentle demeanor masks a mind constantly calculating blast radii and chemical reactions, seeing potential weapons where others see beauty, a duality that once made her valuable to Varang and now makes her essential to the Astikasya's defense. During the Siege of the Falls, Ketxe unleashed her full potential in defense of the home that had given her family sanctuary. Her explosive traps decimated Mangkwan forces, her knowledge of volatile compounds turning the battlefield into a maze of controlled chaos. She faced soldiers she once knew, setting aside guilt to protect the children playing in Sheltering Falls' mists, including her own. In recognition of her breaking free from Varang's control, her choice to use her dangerous gifts for protection rather than destruction, and her unwavering commitment to the clan that accepted her despite everything, she was honored as **the Unbound** among the Nine Spears, wielding the **Unleashed Spear**: a weapon that represents freedom from chains and the choice to fight on her own terms. Despite her past and her reserved nature, Ketxe's love for her children and her mate drives her to seek harmony within the broader community, showcasing the softer side beneath her enigmatic exterior. She channels her dangerous knowledge into protective measures, secretly establishing explosive defenses around the clan's perimeter that only she can navigate safely, her own twisted way of showing love. Her journey from controlled weapon to free warrior serves as proof that even those used as tools of destruction can choose to become guardians of life. Young warriors who train with Ketxe learn not just the chemistry of explosives, but the philosophy of controlled power, that true strength lies in choosing when to unleash devastation and when to hold back, that the most dangerous person is the one who fights by choice rather than compulsion. She remains vigilant, knowing that Varang's reach is long and her memory longer. But now, surrounded by family and protected by the Nine Spears, Ketxe finally allows herself to believe in safety. She is no longer the weapon someone else wields, she is Ketxe te Korhe Kilve'ite, the Unbound, and she fights for love.

## Profile

**Personality:** Tulìpey presents a fascinating blend of tomboy toughness and hidden sensitivity, inheriting her father's fierce pride and her mother's calculated nature. Normally crass and direct like her twin brother, she transforms into a flustered, lovey-dovey mess around Rawìl, her longtime crush turned partner - though this softness belies a sharp mind for strategy and clan politics that often catches others off guard. This duality in her personality often amuses and confuses those around her, though she carries herself with an underlying dignity that betrays her complex upbringing. Despite - or perhaps because of - the history between their families, she approaches her relationship with Rawìl with both fierce determination and tender vulnerability. Her extensive knowledge of clan politics and traditions belies her young age, making her a valuable asset in inter-clan relations - a skill born from years of managing her own family's complicated dynamics. She's particularly protective of Notä, forming a strong bond with her despite their different backgrounds, often acting as a bridge between the younger girl's innovative spirit and more traditional clan expectations. While her father's warrior blood runs strong in her veins, she channels it differently - using precision and knowledge as her weapons of choice, particularly in her study of healing arts under Sänrri's guidance. This tutelage under the current tsakarem is no mere apprenticeship; she is being formally groomed as Sänrri's successor, absorbing the spiritual arts and traditions to eventually step into the role of **tsakarem** (tsahìk-in-waiting) once Sänrri is raised to **tsahìk**. Her skills in weaving and potion-making suggest a future role that bridges traditional warrior culture with more diverse clan needs, maintaining her family's fierce pride while seeking new ways to honor and evolve their traditions, much like her relationship with Rawìl represents a hope for unity between their families.

===== 30 World/The Mark of Earth House.md =====
*Worldbuilding entry for AITM. Companion piece to the Resistance (Earth House) roster in AITM_05_Factions_Profiles.md. Written pre-Siege.*

---

## Overview

Before the Siege of the Falls, Earth House adopted a visual identifier to distinguish themselves from RDA and Mangkwan forces at a glance, without requiring language or close proximity to confirm allegiance. The mark has two parts, worn together:

- **A blackened right hand** — grease, paint, or soot, marking allegiance to Nika Nanaura-Fardin, "Silver Hand"
- **Cyan** worn visibly somewhere on the body or gear — cloth, tape, paint, dye — marking allegiance to Earth House as a whole

The mark was not born from wartime panic. It grew out of an already-trusted alliance, adopted deliberately, and presented to the Astikasya clan as a considered choice rather than an emergency measure.

---

## Why These Two Marks

**The blackened hand** references Nika's own prosthetic, lost during her escape from the RDA and worn since as a badge of honor. The Na'vi already called her Silver Hand in recognition of what the loss cost her and what it bought — recognition as "a friend of the People." A blackened hand on any Earth House member is a small, wearable tribute: *I follow her, I believe in what that hand cost her.*

**Cyan** was chosen because it reads clean against every color already claimed by conflict on Pandora. It does not resemble Mangkwan's red, black, and white ash paint. It does not attempt to imitate anything sacred to the Na'vi. It sits outside all existing visual vocabularies of allegiance — which is precisely what made it legible as something new, entirely Earth House's own.

Together, the two marks require no shared language to read. They only require being seen and remembered.

===== RELATIONSHIP LORE (sections) =====
### Tsu'ong & Ketxe
Two people who found redemption in each other. Tsu'ong was arrogant and had to be humiliated before he could become wise. Ketxe was captured and weaponized and had to escape before she could become herself. They did not fall into each other's arms — they recognized each other as people who had survived becoming something they did not want to be. Their bond is quieter than most, built on mutual respect for the distance each traveled. Ketxe's first love was Tsa'rawm; she disappeared to protect him from Varang's reach, and that sacrifice still lives in their history, unspoken but present.

### Notä & Eantu
Volatile and creative. They are not enemies but they are never entirely at peace, and their conflict has an energy that mirrors the old tension between Tsu'ong (Eantu's father) and Atanvi (Notä's mother). Something about that lineage keeps generating the same charged dynamic across generations.

### Atanvi & Mìreya
The bedrock couple. Mìreya is the person who reminds Atanvi of her worth when she forgets it, which is often. Atanvi is the steady heart that gave Mìreya somewhere to put all the love she had always been afraid to show. They met in circumstances that put them on opposite sides of tension — Tsu'ong once challenged Atanvi for Mìreya's attention and was humiliated — and what emerged was a bond built on choosing each other repeatedly, deliberately, across years. Mìreya maintains a controlled exterior with almost everyone; Atanvi is one of the few people in whose presence she lets it go entirely. Their children Rawìl and Notä are the living proof of what they built.
