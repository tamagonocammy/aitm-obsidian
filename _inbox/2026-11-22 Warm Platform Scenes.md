# Warm Platform scenes (proposal)

> [!warning] Proposal, not canon
> Two draft scenes for the custom described in [[2026-11-22 Warming the Falls]]. The warm platform, the herb-sorting board, the covers, the stew rules and every small gesture are invented for the drafts. Rawìl, Eantu, Tulìpey and Notä are original characters by @lindholm_lizzie. Characters: [[Sänrri]], [[Sänume]], [[Tsa'rawm]], [[Kayan]], [[Lamaytxa]] in the first scene; the whole clan in the second, including [[Kaymi]], [[Lì'may]], [[Eyfuwen]], [['Ipxerey]], [[Mitsìyo]], [[Tse'hayu]], [[Netezu]], [[Mìrì]], [[Mìyo]], [[Anake]], [[Kxayì]], [[Notä]], [[Mawpay]], [[Nìla]], [[Oarä]], [[Lawarey]], [[Rawìl]], [[Eantu]] and [[Tulìpey]].

## The Warm Platform

*Sänrri is already in her Na'vi body here, because the vault does not date her arrival.*

Sänume carried the stone pouch against her stomach with both arms, and the nantang walked behind her in a line, each nose level with the tail ahead of it. Mist had settled on the bridge ropes overnight and every rope dripped. Sänrri followed with her shoulders up around her ear-tufts, holding a fistful of wet lichen she had stopped looking at three bridges ago.

"You are cold," Sänume said, without turning around.

"I am collecting this."

"You are collecting it very slowly, and your tail is wrapped around your own leg."

Sänrri looked down. It was. She unwound it, and it wound itself back.

The Haykxa platform sat in a hollow of the cliff, a floor of woodshaped roots with a ring of river stones sunk into its middle. Tsa'rawm lay on his stomach beside the ring and fed it stones from the pouch one at a time, bone tongs in his left hand. Kayan sat behind him with a half-braided cord and her eyes on the doorway. Lamaytxa lay on her back across the warmest side with her feet toward the stones and one arm over her face.

"Sit," Tsa'rawm said. He said it loudly. He said most things loudly. "Feet by the stones, not on them. Lamaytxa did that once."

"It was a trial of the stones," Lamaytxa said from under her arm. "The stones, I regret to report, were hot."

Sänrri sat. The heat came up through the soles of her feet and into her shins, and her ear-tufts lifted off her skull by a finger's width. She held her hands over the ring. Her feet and hands were warm and the rest of her sat in the draft from the doorway, with the mist coming in low along the floor.

"It is very good," she said. "The heat goes up and away, though. It is like..." She stopped.

"Like what?" Sänume had sat down beside her, close, looking at the wall. The nantang arranged themselves around the edge of the platform like a fence.

"A kotatsu." The word sounded strange in the room, so she said it again, slower. "In my land we had a low table with a heater fixed underneath. A heavy blanket hung down over the edges all the way around, so the heat stayed in. You sat on the floor, put your legs under the table, and the blanket closed around your waist. People ate there. People slept there. People lost whole afternoons there."

Tsa'rawm rolled onto his side. "The table keeps the heat?"

"The blanket keeps the heat. The table holds up the blanket and gives you somewhere to put your cup."

"We have a platform," Tsa'rawm said. "We have blankets. We have a table, almost." He pointed with the tongs at Sänume's herb-sorting board, a slab of woodshaped pine on four short legs, leaning against the wall under a drift of dried leaves. "We have never put them together."

"You are shouting," Sänume said.

"I am thinking in a loud way."

Lamaytxa took her arm from her face. "Pray, what manner of beast is this kotatsu, that it traps the heat and devours no one?"

"It is not a beast."

"A pity."

It took the five of them a short time to build. Sänume swept the leaves off the board with the back of her arm, saying that every one of them had been sorted and somebody would pay. Tsa'rawm set the board on its legs over the ring. Kayan brought the heavy sleeping hide from the stack by the wall, and Sänrri spread it across the board and drew it down to the floor on all four sides, smoothing the corners with her left hand. Tsa'rawm added two more stones and tapped the pouch for luck.

They slid their legs under. The hide made a long dry sound, five pairs of legs and one tail apiece going in together, and the edges settled against their waists.

"Oh," Kayan said. Her eyes had filled. "Oh, that is not fair."

"Why are you crying?" Sänume said.

"My feet are happy."

Nobody said anything for a while. Sänume pulled a bundle of roots onto the board and began sorting them with her left hand, announcing that she would finish this and then check the drying racks. Outside, the plants along the bridge rail began to glow, a line of pale green going away into the mist. Tsa'rawm said that in a moment he would walk the bridge guards. He lay back on his elbows. He did not walk the bridge guards.

The first nantang put its muzzle under the edge of the hide beside Sänume's knee.

"No," Sänume said. "This is a clan matter."

The muzzle went in as far as the eyes. A second one followed it, and then the rest came across the platform in a line, each one turning twice before it lay down, until the hide stood up in lumps and the board lifted a little where the backs pressed against its underside. A chin came to rest on Sänume's knee.

"I did not say you could."

The chin stayed. Her ear-tufts went down, and she went on sorting roots with her free hand, slowly, one root at a time.

"I could rise whenever I wished," Lamaytxa said. She had been lying with her eyes shut for some time. "I merely wish not to." She tried to sit up. A tail pinned hers to the floor, and a nantang was asleep on the other end of it. She lay back down. "I have chosen to remain."

Tsa'rawm's voice came from somewhere near the ceiling. "Sänrri. I would carry you across the Lake for this."

"I would like that very much," Sänrri said, "but you would have to get up."

Nobody laughed, because nobody had the energy. Sänrri tried to count the tails under the hide, reached nine, lost the count to a twitch, and started again. At the doorway the relief for the bridge watch stood with a lantern leaf in her hand and looked at the mound in the hollow. The mound rose and fell. One ear-tuft stuck out from the edge of the hide and flicked once. The relief set the lantern leaf down beside the door and went to tell the watch that they would be a while.

## The Coziest Night on the Spires

*Some cold weeks later, once the board and cover have spread from the Haykxa platform to the whole clan. Lì'may is already Sänrri's daughter, so this cannot be Sänrri's first days.*

The relief for the bridge watch had been told to wait a while, and she had waited, and then she had gone to find out why the watch was still on the bridge. She carried her lantern leaf low and went across the Falls by the outer ropes, because the inner bridges had gone quiet in a way she did not trust.

The Ngotxì platform glowed at its edges. Mitsìyo had woven the cover for it herself, a hide-and-fiber sheet wide enough to roof a family, and it hung to the floor in even folds that nobody else could have made even. She sat against the wall with Netezu asleep in the crook of her arm and one hand still working a loose thread. Tse'hayu knelt at the edge with a ladle, passing bowls under the cover to hands the relief could not see.

"Eat it now," he said. "It has a time, and the time is now."

"Tse'hayu," Mitsìyo said. "Sit."

"I am checking the stew."

"Sit."

He sat. Under the cover Txewan had started the evening song in a voice that kept dropping lower, and Mìyo was saying that the wind outside had a face. Mìrì said it did not. Mìrì knocked her bowl over reaching to prove it, and a soft shuffle went around under the hide as two sets of tails moved out of reach of each other's hands. Neither twin liked being touched there. Neither of them left.

The relief went on. On the Samaya platform Eyfuwen had been telling a story about a hunt that grew larger each time he reached the part with the thanator, and his voice had slowed until the thanator was only a rumor and then not mentioned. 'Ipxerey sat beside him, upright, ear-tufts level, the only person in the hollow whose eyes were open. She turned her head when the lantern leaf passed.

"Tomorrow is the ceremony in the Cave of Truth," the relief said, quietly, because she felt someone should.

"The ceremony will begin when I stand," 'Ipxerey said, "and I am not standing." She closed her eyes.

Lì'may stood on top of the board with her fists on her hips. She had a gap in her front teeth and a blanket corner clamped in them.

"I am the strongest warrior on Pandora," she announced around the blanket. "I am staying."

"You are on the table," Sänrri said, from beneath it.

"I am winning."

"You are winning very well." Sänrri reached up with her left hand and lifted her by the middle, and Lì'may let herself be lifted, folded herself under the edge, and landed in her lap like something poured. "Sa'nu," she said, and bit the cover once more for the record, and her eyes closed in the same breath.

A shape was lowering itself down the rope from the rafter above. Kaymi had been watching from there for some time. She stopped halfway, looked at the daughter and the mate and the board, and said nothing. She came down the rest of the way and slid her legs under the hide without a word, and the relief saw one hand come out and rest on Sänrri's knee.

The Haykxa platform was the last one, and it had grown. Notä had lashed two extra boards to the first with cord and a lever she would not explain, and a flat roof of hide ran over all three. Under it lay every older child in the Falls who had been told to go to their own platform. Each of them had decided separately that this was the platform with the original.

Lawarey counted heads in the lantern light. She did it out loud and stopped at fourteen, which was right, and she looked pleased and then looked at the nantang and counted those too. Sänume sat in the middle with her roots abandoned and a muzzle on each knee. Tsa'rawm lay full length with his one good eye closed, a nantang across his chest and Lamaytxa's tail across his ankles. Kayan was crying quietly over somebody. Nobody asked who.

"It is the stones that make the heat," Mawpay said, to the roof. "The cover only holds it in."

"Technically yes," said Sänrri, who had come across from the Samaya platform to see the work. She said it the way she would have said it to an elder.

"I am correct, then."

"You are correct. Irayo."

Mawpay lay very still for a moment. Then she sank another hand's width under the hide.

Kxayì announced that she was leaving. She slid in deeper. She announced it again, from lower, and Notä said, "You said that eleven times," and Kxayì said, "Twelve," and neither moved away from the other. Nìla lay between them and listened to both and said nothing. Oarä had a piece of cord in her fingers and a knot half-tied. She had tasted the edge of the cover, and the knot was for that. At the far end Eantu lay with his feet sticking out into the cold, bristling at the boy two bodies over, who was counting the ways out of the hollow, as always, and had found none. That was Rawìl. Tulìpey, between them, pretended to be asleep and did it badly. Rawìl looked at the feet and pulled the edge of the hide up over them. He did not say anything. Eantu said nothing back. The feet did not come out again.

Anake went by on his knees with a pot, feeding whoever had not eaten, and then he lay down with the pot on his stomach and ate the last of it himself. Lawarey crawled across the board to Sänrri and put something small and flat into her hand. It was a stone, gray, worn smooth on both sides.

"It means you did the thing," Lawarey said.

"Which thing?"

"The thing." She went back to her place and turned her back on everyone, and she was asleep before the stone had cooled in Sänrri's palm.

Sänrri sat for a while holding it. Under the roof of hides ten children and five adults breathed together in a long soft rhythm, and the boards creaked as the pile of nantang shifted and resettled. Outside, the plants along the bridge rail had gone bright, and the mist moved between them without hurry.

The relief stood in the doorway with the lantern leaf for some time. Then she crossed to the nearest empty corner, set the leaf down on the floor where it could glow, and slid under the edge of the cover with her bow still on her back.
