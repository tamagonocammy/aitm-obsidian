# AITM — Reef Na'vi Reference
### *Lì'fya Wiona* — how the reef clans speak, and how Yawyay sounds at the Falls

> **Scope.** Reef Na'vi (RN; Frommer calls it *LW*, Lì'fya Wiona) is the dialect of the reef clans from *Avatar: The Way of Water*. Forest Na'vi (FN; *LN*, Lì'fya Na'rìngä) is the dialect from the first film and the one the Astikasya speak. In AITM the only native RN speaker is **Yawyay** (Ta'unui clan). This doc covers the confirmed differences, how to spell RN on the page, Yawyay's version of AITM names, reef vocabulary, and the full list of words with *ù*.
>
> **Sources.** Paul Frommer (signing as Pawl) on Na'viteri: "Reef Na'vi part 1: Phonetics and Phonology" (Jan 2023), "Clarifications and Announcements" (Jan 2023, comments), "Reef Na'vi part 2: Morphology, Syntax, Lexicon… and more" (May 2023), "The complete u / ù list for Reef Na'vi" (Jul 2023). Reykunyu's dialect rules and dictionary data (downloaded 2026-10-28). Built for AITM.

## 1. The big picture

Frommer describes the two dialects as fully mutually understandable, like British and American English: the same sentence, said differently. Neither one is "standard." Almost all the differences are pronunciation; grammar and vocabulary differ only in a few preferences.

---

## 2. Pronunciation

**Ejectives become voiced stops.** At the start of a syllable, *px tx kx* become *b d g*: *txon* → *don* "night," *holpxay* → *holbay* "number," *kxitx* → *gitx* "death," *txampay* → *dampay* "sea." Not after *f* or *s*: *skxawng* stays *skxawng*, *fpxäkìm* keeps its ejective. When two ejectives touch, the second one's change pulls the first along (Frommer's examples: *adge*, *egdu*). Lenition happens first, then the RN change: *txon* "night" → *don*, but plural *ton* stays *ton*.

**The glottal stop smooths away between different vowels, inside a word.** *fra'u* → *frau* "everything," *Lo'ak* → *Loak*, *tsa'u* → *tsau*. Between identical vowels it is optional, and the doubled vowel stays (*rää* or *rä'ä*; *apxaa*, *meeveng* stay as they are). A glottal stop at the start of a word is kept: *'Ipxerey* keeps its first one.

**A second u vowel: ù.** FN merged two vowels into *u*; RN keeps them apart: tense *u* [u] and lax *ù* [ʊ], like the vowels of English "boot" and "book." This is lexical, so it has to be looked up: *tsun* "heel" vs *tsùn* "can," *hum* → *hùm* "leave," *tulkun* → *tùlkun*. Most *u*'s stay *u*. The full list is in section 7.

**Unstressed ä often becomes e.** Frommer says "often" and leaves the conditions open until he has "heard more of the Reef people's speech": *ätxäle* → *edäle*, but *rää* keeps its *ä*. Reykunyu applies the change everywhere; treat it as a tendency, not a law.

**sy and tsy.** RN says *sy* as "sh" and *tsy* as "ch": *syaw* [ʃaw] "call," *tsyal* [t͡ʃal] "wing," *syawm* [ʃawm] "know." The spelling does not change.

---

## 3. Grammar and word choice

**Patientive: RN prefers -ti.** Both dialects have -it/-ti/-t; FN leans toward -it after consonants, RN toward -ti. FN *Oel fwew fìtutanit*, RN more often *Oel fwew fìtutanti* "I am looking for this man."

**The topic can come last.** In FN the topical (-ri/-ìri) must start its clause: *Fìstxeliri alor irayo* "thank you for this beautiful gift." RN also allows it at the end: *Irayo fìstxeliri alor.*

**RN often drops *lu*.** *Fìtutan a rììrmì ftu na'rìng* "this man in the reflection is from the forest." Frommer has not described when this is allowed, so use it sparingly and only in simple "X is from/at Y" sentences.

**Preferred words.** "Know": RN says ***syawm*** ("shawm"); FN says *omum* (both exist in both dialects). "Diligently, with effort": RN prefers ***val***; FN *kawl*.

## 4. Spelling RN in AITM

Frommer says writing *b d g* is optional; it is mainly used to show the difference. *ù* should be written; *sy*/*tsy* keep their FN spelling. For AITM, writing **Yawyay's dialogue in RN spelling** (b/d/g, dropped glottal stops, ù) makes her voice visible on the page the way her accent is audible at the Falls. Everyone else stays in FN spelling. If an RN *n* and *g* meet across syllables, a hyphen or dot keeps them from reading as *ng* (*tìkan-gan*).

The project lexicon (`claude/AITM_Navi_Lexicon_Reykunyu.md`) shows the RN spelling of every word that differs (`RN:` in the entry).

| Haykxa | **Hayga** | ejective → voiced |
| Ngotxì | **Ngodì** | ejective → voiced |
| Ta'unui | **Taunui** | glottal stop drops (her own clan's name) |

Names whose spelling stays but whose **sound** changes because *sy* is "sh": **Syulang** [ˈʃu.laŋ] "Shulang," **Syayvi** "Shayvi," **Tswesya** "Tswesha," **Nì'syu** "Nì'shu," and the clan itself, **Astikasya** "Astikasha."

Two things this table cannot settle: whether any name has an *ù* (names are not in the ù list, so that is a story choice), and whether unstressed *ä* in names like *Notä* or *Oarä* softens toward *e* (it depends on stress, which the roster does not record yet).

So at the Falls, Yawyay calls the Tsahìk ***'Iberey***, yells for ***Gayì*** and ***Dewan***, and lives among the ***Astikasha***. Everyone has long since stopped correcting her.
