# AITM — Notes from Na'viteri
### What Paul Frommer's blog adds to the AITM Na'vi work (checked October 29, 2026)

> Na'viteri (naviteri.org) is Frommer's own blog, where new words and grammar rulings are first published. These notes cover the posts that bear on tonight's work and on the clan's daily life. Every word below is also in Reykunyu.

---

## 1. Rulings that touch our open questions

**Causatives: who goes in which case.** When ‹eyk› ("make someone do") goes on a *transitive* verb, the one who causes takes the agentive and the one made to act moves to the **dative**: *Fìpamtseol oeru neykew futa srew* — "This music makes me want to dance" (lit. causes-to-me wanting that I dance). For intransitive verbs the causee stays the object. **Settled:** the Annotated Dictionary has canon examples of *sleyku* (the causative of *slu*) with exactly this shape, *oeti nitram sleyku* "makes me happy" (*Fula tsayun oeng pivängkxo ye'rìn oeti nitram sleyku nìtxan* — "It makes me very happy that we will be able to chat soon"). So *Fìsponol … oeti tsamsä'o sleykolu* ("this island made me a weapon") is canon-shaped Na'vi. ‹eyk› cannot go on modal verbs (*tsun*, *new* used as a modal…). *(“Five little discussions,” 2020)*

**After *fwa* / *futa*, use the bare verb.** Frommer now treats the plain verb as normal in a *fwa/futa* clause: *Sunu oer fwa srew* "I like to dance," and *srivew* there is ungrammatical. The subjunctive ‹iv› stays required after *fte* ("in order to") and with modals (*tsun sivar*). Our *Moel spaw futa moel tsun tsat sivar…* follows this: the ‹iv› is there because of *tsun*, not because of *futa*. *(Same post)*

**Sno = "her own."** *Sno* points back to someone in the same clause, like English "his/her own": *Ateyol fpìl futa Ralul sneyä tsmuket ve'kì* — "Ateyo thinks that Ralu hates *his own* sister" (Ralu's), while *peyä* there would mean Ateyo's. Frommer notes the rule may be more complicated in practice. *(Same post)*

**Still open.** Nothing on Na'viteri settles how *muntxa si* marks the partner (Tsu'ong's *Oehu muntxa si*), how case endings attach to a full name with *te*, or a canon example of *frato*. These stay flagged.

## 2. Canon usage examples (Plumps' Annotated Dictionary, via Reykunyu)

**A transitive verb with no object takes a plain subject when the statement is generic:** the Na'vi book's own example is *oel tse'a pot* "I see it" vs *oe tse'a* "I see." So *Oe noläk nìtxan* "I drank a lot" is fine, but "I know" about a specific thing should carry its object: ***Oel tsat omum*** "I know (that)," not a bare *Oel omum*. Every canon *omum* example has an object.

**Happy is felt, not been.** *Nitram* is "used with *'efu*": ***oe 'efu nitram*** "I feel happy," not *oe lu nitram*. (*Po ke 'olefu nitram* — "She had not been happy.") Only for people.

**Txewan, in canon Na'vi.** The dictionary's example for *loreyu 'awnampi* could have been written about her: ***Lu por mokri amiklor, slä loreyu 'awnampi lu. Ke tsun rivol eo sute.*** — "She has a beautiful voice, but she is extremely shy. She cannot sing in front of people."

**Drinking.** *swoa* "intoxicating drink," *rou* "be/get drunk," *tsngal* "cup": ***Ngeyä tsngal lumpe lu mek? Näk nì'ul ko!*** — "Why is your cup empty? Drink up!" ***Kxawm swoat nìhawng noläk oel*** — "Perhaps I drank too much." ***Rä'ä rou!*** — "Do not get drunk!" *Ke pxan* is the canon reply to a compliment ("don't mention it").

**Losing.** *snaytx* "lose (a game)" takes a plain subject when nothing is named as lost: *Ayoe snolaytx fìtrr* — "We lost today." **Fighting.** *wem* "fight": with someone = *hu*, against = *wä* (also suffixed: *Omatikayawä*).

## 3. Confirmations

**Names ending in -ì** inflect like any vowel-final noun (Frommer's paradigm for *hapxì*: *hapxìl, hapxìt/hapxìti, hapxìr/hapxìru, hapxìyä, hapxìri*). So *Kxayìl, Mìrìyä, Katirìri* in the case sheet are right. *(“A few little discussions about grammar,” 2022)*

**Unreleased final stops.** Word-final *k, p, t* (and *k, p, t* before another consonant: *nik.re*, *txep.mì*) are unreleased in Na'vi. The Astikasya accent's "final stops dissolve into the waterfall" is that same canon feature pushed one step further, which grounds the accent doc nicely. In fast speech, an unreleased stop before a glottal stop can pop into an ejective, as in *nìk'ong* — the word inside Mitsìyo's saying *'ivong nìk'ong*. *(“Five little discussions”)*

**Reading the forest.** The Na'vi verb *inan* means "read" **in the sense of reading the forest, gaining knowledge from the senses**: *Pol na'rìngit inan nìltsan* "He reads the forest well." *Tsmìmìri wätx fol tinanit nìngay* — "They're really bad at reading animal tracks." So a Na'vi character who "reads" tracks, wind or a face is fully in line with the AITM rule that Na'vi have no writing; only reading *marks made by people* would break it. *(Na'viteri post on inan)*

## 4. The clan's daily life: canon words worth using

**Sänrri's name.** *Sänrr* was published in 2015 as "light source, lamp"; Reykunyu now glosses it "glow, an instance of glowing." Tomori's own name, 燈, means *lamp*. If that was intentional, it is perfect; if it was not, it is a gift.

**Home.** *swaynivi* "family hammock" (*soaia* + *nivi*), *snonivi* "single hammock," *prrsmung* "baby carrier" (Netezu's), *tsmisnrr* "nectar lantern," *sänrr* "lamp, glow."
**Food.** *huru* "cooking pot," *tsyey* "snack," *niktsyey* "leaf food wrap," *merki* "meat-smoking rack," *swoasey* "kava bowl," *swoasey ayll* "the large social kava bowl" (Tìfyaw's natural habitat), *yomyo lerìk* or just *rìk* "leaf plate."
**The hunt.** *tarpongu* "hunting party," *seyto* "to butcher (process a carcass)": *Awngal fìyerikit nìwin siveyto ko* — "Let's cut up this hexapede quickly," a line the Haykxa dinner was missing.
**Guests.** *Sko frrtu, frakrr mì helku ngeyä lu oeru hoan nìtxan, ma tsmuk* — "As a guest, I always feel very comfortable in your home, brother."
**Speech.** *nawfwe* "fluent," from *na hufwe* "as fast as the wind." What the clan says about Sänrri's Na'vi now.

**Etiquette.** *Lal* "old" is for things, never for people: calling a person *lal* is an insult. For the elders, use their titles or *txanlal* only for ancient stories and objects (*vur atxanlal* "an ancient story").
