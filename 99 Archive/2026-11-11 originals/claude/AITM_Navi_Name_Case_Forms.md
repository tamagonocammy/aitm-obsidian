# AITM — Na'vi Name Case Forms

*Reference for inflecting Astikasya names in Na'vi dialogue. Follows Kelutral's grammar course (kelutral.org/horen/3-1), which is the standard for AITM canon. Built October 2026.*

## The rules

Na'vi has five case forms. The **subjective** (subject of a verb with no object) and the **vocative** (*ma Atanvi*) use the bare name. The other four cases add an ending, and which ending depends on how the name ends.

| Case | Use | After a vowel | After a consonant or ll/rr | After ay / ey | After aw / ew |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agentive | Doer, with a transitive verb | -l | -ìl | -ìl | -ìl |
| Patientive | Object of a transitive verb | -t, -ti | -it, -ti | **-t**, -ti | -ti, -it |
| Dative | "to / for" someone | -ru, -r | -ur | -ru, -ur | -ru, -ur, **-r** |
| Genitive | "of / 's" | -yä (**-ä after o or u**) | -ä | -ä | -ä |
| Topical | "as for…" | -ri | -ìri | -ri | -ri |

**Patientive after -y.** After ay or ey, the -it ending shortens to -t because the y and the i run together: *'Ipxereyt*, not *'Ipxereyit*. (Kelutral's example: *keyeyt*.) The long form -ti is also correct and sounds more formal (in the film, Eytukan and Mo'at use -ti when addressing the clan).

**Long vs. short forms.** In the patientive and dative, the choice between the long and short form (-ti / -t, -ru / -r) depends on rhythm and register, not grammar. The short dative -r on a name is rare in practice, so -ru is the safer default.

**Genitive after o / u.** Names ending in o or u take plain -ä: *Netezuä*, *Mìyoä*, *Mitsìyoä*. Nouns ending in -ia take -iä (*soaiä*, "of the family").

**Pseudovowels.** Names ending in ll or rr behave like consonants: *'Awllìl*, *'Awllä*.

**Known disagreement.** The Wikibooks *Na'vi* book (2010-era) gives *kifkeyit* for the patientive after -y and limits the short -r dative to pronouns. AITM follows Kelutral on both points.

**Not covered.** Inflection of full names (*Atanvi te Samaya 'Ipxerey'ite*). Where the ending goes on a full name with *te* has not been confirmed yet.
