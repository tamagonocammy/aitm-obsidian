# AITM — The Nine Spears in Na'vi
### *Tukru avolaw yapayä* — the Nine Spears of the Mists

> Every word below was checked against Reykunyu's live dictionary and parser (October 2026). Grammar follows Kelutral. Where Na'vi has no confirmed word for an English concept, the doc says so and uses the closest real word instead of inventing one. Built for AITM.

---

## The name

**Tukru avolaw yapayä** — *the Nine Spears of the Mist*

*tukru* "spear" · *volaw* "nine" (octal *vol* "eight" + *'aw* "one": the Na'vi count in eights, so nine is literally *eight-and-one*) · *yapay* "mist, fog" in the genitive. When a number modifies a noun, the noun stays singular and the number takes the attributive *a*: *zìsìt amrr* "five years," so *tukru avolaw* "nine spears." (*Volaw* is formed by rule and does not appear as a dictionary headword; the number system is in the project's `Na'vi.pdf`, section 9.)

## The motto

**Kelku aflrr mì ho'on aysukruä**
*A gentle home within a ring of spears*

*kelku* "home" · *flrr* "gentle, mild, tender" · *mì* "in, on" (causes lenition: *ko'on* → *ho'on*) · *ko'on* "ring, closed roughly circular shape" · *tukru* in the plural (*ay+* with lenition: *t* → *s*) and the genitive. Na'vi has no word for "kingdom," so the motto says *home*, which may fit the Astikasya better anyway.

**The secret tenth.** *Tukru avomuve* — the tenth spear (*vomuve* "tenth," octal *eight-and-two*). The one Txolaw made for Kayan through tsaheylu alone, the title Kayan turned down. Nobody says it out loud.

**A shared cry.** **Oeyä tukrul txe'lanit tivakuk!** — *Let my spear strike the heart!* This sentence comes straight from the project's Na'vi book (section 5.4, word order), so it is attested Na'vi, not a construction of ours: *tukru* in the ergative, *txe'lan* "heart" in the accusative, *takuk* "strike" with the subjunctive ‹iv› (t‹iv›akuk).

## How each piece works, and what was missing

**Titles** use the most natural Na'vi pattern for "X the Y": the name plus an attributive adjective (*Kaymi atstew*, like *tute atstew* "a brave person"). Where no adjective exists, the title becomes a genitive ("of the shadow") or a short relative clause ("who does not miss").

**The Brazen → *atstew*** "brave." Na'vi has no separate word for "bold" or "brazen." *Tìtstewnga'* "courageous" also exists, but *tstew* is cleaner, and the vow uses its adverb, *nìtstew*.

**The Merciful → *anusalsteng*.** There is no word for "mercy." This is the active participle of *nalsteng* "feel empathy or compassion": n‹us›alsteng, "compassionate, feeling-with." Participles are fully productive, so this is ordinary grammar, not a coined word. *Txoa* "forgiveness" is the other near-synonym and belongs to Syayvi.

**The Phantom → *ukä*** "of the shadow." There is no word for "ghost" or "phantom." *Uk* "shadow" ties the title straight to her vow ("guide through shadow").

**The Thunderous → *rawmpxomä*** "of the thunder and lightning" (*'rrpxom* is thunder alone). The *rawm* inside *rawmpxom* and inside *Tsa'rawm* is a coincidence worth keeping.

**The Unerring → *a ke hupx*** "who does not miss." *Hupx* means "miss, fail to hit a target," and Na'vi has no single adjective for "unerring."

**The Righteous → *amuiä*** "proper, fair, right, justified." (*Ngay* "true" is the alternative.)

**The Penitent → *alusapx*** "regretting, regretful": the active participle of *lapx* "regret." There is no word for "penitent" or "redemption"; her vow seeks *txoa*, forgiveness.

**The Wise → *alafyon*** "wise." (*Txantslusam* and *hafyonga'* also mean "wise.") Her vow's *hafyon* "wisdom" is also the family name of Katirì and Waynhì.

**The Unbound → *alayro*** "free (from oppression)," exactly Ketxe's story with Varang. There is no word for "chain" or "bound."

**Crimson → *areypaytun*** "blood red" (*tun* is plain red-orange).
**Tender → *aflrr***, Reykunyu's own gloss is "gentle, mild, tender."
**Singing → *arusol***: active participle of *rol* "sing," r‹us›ol.
**Scarred → *ayskxirä*** "of wounds." **No word for "scar" exists**; *skxir* is "wound." If a word for "scar" is published, swap it in.
**Balanced → *apawnon***: passive participle of *pon* "balance," p‹awn›on, "balanced."
**Immovable → *aketsuk'ärìp***: *ketsuk-* "in-…-able" on *'ärìp* "move (something)," so "unmovable." Reykunyu's parser recognizes it as a regular derivation.
**Ashen → *anawnekx*** "burned": passive participle of *nekx* "burn, consume," matching the spear's wood burned in the Siege. (*Txeva* or *txepìva* are "ash" if a noun is preferred: *tukru txevayä* "spear of ash.")
**Tempered → no word.** Na'vi has no confirmed word for tempering or hardening. The table uses *atxa'* "hard"; *tukru txepä* "the spear of fire" is the other honest option. Flagged until a better word turns up.
**Unleashed → *alawnonu***: passive participle of *lonu* "release, let go," l‹awn›onu, "released, let loose."

**Vows** are first-person statements in the present, the way an oath sounds when it is spoken as a fact rather than a wish. Transitive verbs with no object (*eyk*, *zeyko*, *takuk*) keep the subject unmarked (*oe*); *Oel frayrrapit lewn* has an object, so *oel* takes the ergative. *Mowar si* is a si-verb, so it stays *oe*. *Tsun* "can" takes the subjunctive: '‹iv›ärìp. For a more ceremonial register, Na'vi has the formal infix ‹uy›, which could be added to any of these verbs when they are sworn at the Cave of Truth.

## Epithets in Na'vi: both orders work

Na'vi can build a title in English order (*Shanks the Red-Haired*) or Japanese order (*Akagami no Shanks*), because its two main tools can sit on either side of the name.

**Adjective + *a*.** The attributive *a* always sits between adjective and noun, so it moves with the adjective: **Kaymi atstew** "Kaymi the Brave" = **tstewa Kaymi** "Brave Kaymi." (Reykunyu notes *tstew* is used only for people.)

**The genitive, Na'vi's の.** *-ä / -yä* can come before or after the name, exactly like Japanese の: 赤髪のシャンクス lines up word for word with **tuna nikreyä Shanks** (*tun* "red," *nikre* "hair," *-yä* "of"); English order is **Shanks nikreyä atun**. So **Txewan ukä** = **ukä Txewan**, 影のトゥエワン.

**Relative clause.** **Rewon a ke hupx** = **ke hupx a Rewon** "Rewon who does not miss."

**Rank in front.** Simple apposition: **Olo'eyktan Eyfuwen**, **Tsahìk 'Ipxerey**.

**Deed titles.** *Toruk makto* "toruk rider" is a frozen phrase (noun + the verb *makto* "ride," with no *-yu*), so it works as an idiom rather than a pattern. The productive way to say "one who does X" is *-yu*: *taronyu* "hunter," *zeykoyu* "healer."

**Affection.** The diminutive **-tsyìp** works freely on names (Reykunyu: "including proper nouns"): **Lì'maytsyìp** "little Lì'may," Na'vi's answer to *-chan*.

**A possible clan custom.** Frommer describes no difference in meaning between the two orders, so the Astikasya can make it a matter of register: the Japanese order (*tstewa Kaymi*, *ukä Txewan*) in ceremony at the Cave of Truth, the English order (*Kaymi atstew*) in everyday speech and when the children shout the Nine's names in their games.

## Words of the Nine: lines from their source stories, in Na'vi

Each Spear carries an echo of the character they were adapted from. These are real lines from the source anime and manga, checked against published sources (October 2026), each rendered into Na'vi and re-checked word by word in Reykunyu. Where Pandora has no equivalent (candles, mice, bread with jam), the Na'vi version keeps the feeling and changes the image.

**Kaymi** · from Ericht Samaya (*The Witch from Mercury*): 「ロウソクみたいで綺麗だね」, "It's pretty, like candles."
**Lor lu, na txep.** *It is beautiful, like fire.*
*na* "like, as." There are no candles at the Falls, so the fire stays and the candles go. A line from a small child watching something terrible; whether Kaymi ever said it is a story choice.

**Atanvi** · from Suletta Mercury: 「逃げたら一つ、進めば二つ」, "Run away and you gain one; move forward and you gain two."
**Txo nga hivifwo, tsakrr syonti a'aw ngal tel. Txo nga rivikx nìyeyfya, tsakrr mesyonti ngal tel.**
The Samaya motto (see the main project notes on it). *Syon* means both "point" and "trait," so what you gain is a piece of who you are.

**Txewan** · from Hitori Gotoh (*Bocchi the Rock!*): 「このままじゃイヤだ！」, "I don't want it to stay like this!" (her outburst at the first live, the moment her guitar saves the show).
**Oe ke new fìfya!** *I do not want it like this!*
*new* "want" with no object keeps *oe* plain; *fìfya* "this way, like this."

**Tsa'rawm** · from Denji (*Chainsaw Man*): "At least let me dream a little."
**Oe unil sivi nì'it!** *Let me dream, a little!*
*unil si* "dream" with the subjunctive ‹iv› (s‹iv›i), which turns it into a wish; *nì'it* "a bit." Pure Tsa'rawm: a boy who grew up with nothing, asking only for the dream.

**Rewon** · from Asa Mitaka (*Chainsaw Man*): 「友達はいらない……いても意味ないから……」, "I don't need friends... there's no point in having them."
**Oel ayeylanit ke kin… Fo ke tsranten.** *I do not need friends… They do not matter.*
*'eylan* "friend," plural with lenition (*ay'eylan* → *ayeylan*) plus *-it*; *tsranten* "to matter." The line belongs to who Rewon *was*, before the thanator and Tsa'rawm. It hits hardest if someone else quotes it back to her now.

**Tsu'lan** · from Yuji Itadori (*Jujutsu Kaisen*): "I'm you." (to Mahito, after Shibuya), and "I wanna help a lot of people, so when I die, I want to be surrounded by a lot of people."
**Oe lu nga.** *I am you.*
**Oe new frapor srung sivi.** *I want to help everyone.*
The database already gives Tsu'lan his "I'm you" moment with Luka; this is how it sounds in Na'vi. *frapo* "everyone" in the dative; *srung si* is a si-verb, so *oe* stays plain.

**Syayvi** · from Sakiko Togawa (*Ave Mujica*): 「わたくしは、人で無しですわ………」, "I am... inhuman."
**Oe ke lu tute.** *I am not a person.*
For an exile once forbidden from Eywa, this is the cruelest thing she could say about herself, and the thing the Penitent's vow (*Oel fwew txoat*, "I seek forgiveness") answers.
*A check on a line people often attach to this story:* the famous "give you the rest of my life" line belongs to **Uika** (「私の人生全部あげたい、あげる」, "I want to give you my whole life… I will"), not Sakiko. In AITM that makes it **Nì'syu/'Awll's** line, said to Syayvi.

**Tsu'ong** · from Guel Jeturk (*The Witch from Mercury*, episode 3): 「俺と結婚してくれ」, "Marry me."
**Oehu muntxa si!** *Mate with me!*
*muntxa si* "to mate, marry"; *hu* "with." ⚠ Reykunyu has no usage note on how *muntxa si* marks the partner; *hu* is the natural reading, but treat it as unconfirmed. In AITM, Tsu'ong lost his duel against Atanvi for Mìreya; whether a humiliated young Tsu'ong blurted this at the wrong person is entirely Millie's call.

**Ketxe** · from Reze (*Chainsaw Man*): asked whether Denji would rather be a city mouse or a country mouse, she answers that "nothing beats peace."
**Kea 'u lu sìltsan to fpom.** *Nothing is better than peace.*
Literally "no thing is good than peace": *kea* "no" (always before the noun), *to* "than," *fpom* "peace, wellbeing." The Unbound, who was made into a weapon, choosing quiet.
