# Tìrim — The Yellow of the Astikasya

*Worldbuilding entry for AITM. Companion piece to `AITM_Earth_House_Mark.md` and `AITM_Geography_The_Lake.md`. Status: **proposal, pending Millie's confirmation.** Na'vi vocabulary used here is limited to confirmed words (rim "yellow," tìrim "the color yellow," nìrim "in yellow" — from the Na'vi grammar reference). Everything else is described in English until a translation is confirmed.*

---

## Overview

The color of the Astikasya is yellow. Not the yellow of fire, which belongs to the Mangkwan and their ash, and not a color borrowed from anyone else. It is the yellow of a healer's hands.

Before the exile, when the Astikasya were known across the forests as herbalists, their medicine was made from a root whose flesh stained everything it touched a deep, warm yellow. Every healer carried that stain. It lived in the creases of their fingers and under their nails, and it never fully washed out. Children learned to read it before they could name it: a person with yellow hands was a person who could help you. Over generations the stain stopped being an accident of the work and became the clan's sign. Healers painted it on deliberately. Then everyone did.

The color is the oldest thing the Astikasya own. Older than the Falls, older than the crossbow, older than the Nine Spears.

---

## What Was Carried Out of the Forest

The tragedy that drove the clan from their ancestral forests took nearly everything. The root did not survive the journey. It would not grow in the cold, thin soil of the Spires, and for the first seasons at Sheltering Falls there was no yellow at all. Elders still describe those years as the time when the clan had no color, as if they had been made briefly invisible to themselves.

What the clan did keep was the knowledge of the yellow: the songs that went with preparing it, the meaning of each mark, the memory of what it looked like on a healer's hands. It was held the only way Na'vi hold anything, in voices, passed from elder to child, so that the color existed in the clan long after the plant itself was gone.

## What the Falls Gave Back

The yellow returned through the mist.

On the wettest faces of the Spires, where waterfall spray hits the rock without stopping, grows a low lichen that exists nowhere else in Astikasya territory. It only thrives in permanent spray, and in the dark it gives off almost no light, so for years it went unnoticed. It was Zìsaw, newly arrived from the Aranahe and carrying their old dyeing knowledge, who first ground it and found the color inside. The pigment was a shade paler than the root's, but close enough that the oldest elders wept when they saw it on her fingers.

The clan understood it immediately as a teaching. The forest's yellow was gone, and the Falls had made a new one out of the very mist that hid them. The clan's founding belief made visible: adapt without forgetting who you are. The color changed its source but kept its meaning.

There is also a practical gift in it. Yellow carries through fog better than almost any other color. In the mist of the Spires, a band of yellow on a rider's arm is the first thing an ally sees and the last thing an enemy expects.

---

## How the Yellow Is Worn

Na'vi do not write, so the yellow is never a symbol in the human sense. It is read the way all Astikasya knowledge is read: through body art, weaving, and the stories that explain them.

**On the hands.** The oldest form. A stain across the fingers and palms, echoing the healers it came from. Worn by healers always, and by everyone during rites of care: tending the sick, preparing the dead, welcoming a newborn.

**In the braid.** A thread of yellow-dyed fiber woven into the hair, near but never across the kuru. The pattern of the weave holds meaning that can be read by those who were taught: a child's first thread, a mated pair's shared thread, a mourning thread worn loose.

**In flight.** Ikran riders wear a broad band of yellow across the forearm, so they can recognize each other through the mist at a glance. During the Siege, this is how the clan found its own people in the fog over the Lake, among Mangkwan red, black, and white ash and Earth House cyan.

**On the young.** A child receives their first yellow mark at their first laysrewtsyìp, painted on the backs of their hands by an elder. Children are told it means *you belong to the people who help.*

## The Offering to the Lake *(proposal)*

When a new Olo'eyktan or Tsahìk takes up their role, they are painted in yellow from the hands upward, the most yellow any Astikasya ever wears. The clan carries them down to the Lake, and they wade in until the water takes all of it. The color goes back to Eywa. The leader comes out of the water unpainted, and from that moment their hands belong to the clan rather than to themselves.

Someday Kaymi and Sänrri will walk into that water together.

## Real-World Echo — Notes for Millie

The Astikasya already carry an Andean/Colombian thread: the accent reference uses Bogotá Rolo Spanish as its real-world analogue and points to Quechua and Aymara for its mountain ejectives. The Lake offering above deliberately echoes, at a respectful distance, the Muisca ceremony at Lake Guatavita, where a new ruler covered in gold dust was rafted onto the sacred lake and the gold was given to the water. That ceremony is the origin of the El Dorado legend, which brought conquerors to Muisca land hunting for gold that was never theirs to take.

The parallel to Pandora is direct and painful. The RDA came to the Falls for what lies under them. But the Astikasya's yellow is pigment, not metal. It cannot be mined, traded, or stolen. It is worthless to anyone who does not know what it means. Their treasure is the one the Sky People cannot see.

This is an echo, not a copy: no gold, no raft, nothing lifted directly from Muisca sacred practice, only the shape of a leader giving color back to the water.

## TØP Overlay

The clan are Sänrri's Banditos, and the Banditos' color is yellow. In Trench, yellow is what the Banditos wear, the color Dema's Bishops cannot see. At the Falls, the RDA never learned what the yellow meant, and the mist made sure they rarely saw it at all.
