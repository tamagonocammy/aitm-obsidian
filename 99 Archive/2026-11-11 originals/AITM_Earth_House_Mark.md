# The Mark of Earth House — Origin & Meaning

*Worldbuilding entry for AITM. Companion piece to the Resistance (Earth House) roster in AITM_05_Factions_Profiles.md. Written pre-Siege.*

---

## Overview

Before the Siege of the Falls, Earth House adopted a visual identifier to distinguish themselves from RDA and Mangkwan forces at a glance, without requiring language or close proximity to confirm allegiance. The mark has two parts, worn together:

- **A blackened right hand** — grease, paint, or soot, marking allegiance to Nika Nanaura-Fardin, "Silver Hand"
- **Cyan** worn visibly somewhere on the body or gear — cloth, tape, paint, dye — marking allegiance to Earth House as a whole

The mark was not born from wartime panic. It grew out of an already-trusted alliance, adopted deliberately, and presented to the Astikasya clan as a considered choice rather than an emergency measure.

---

## Why These Two Marks

**The blackened hand** references Nika's own prosthetic, lost during her escape from the RDA and worn since as a badge of honor. The Na'vi already called her Silver Hand in recognition of what the loss cost her and what it bought — recognition as "a friend of the People." A blackened hand on any Earth House member is a small, wearable tribute: *I follow her, I believe in what that hand cost her.*

**Cyan** was chosen because it reads clean against every color already claimed by conflict on Pandora. It does not resemble Mangkwan's red, black, and white ash paint. It does not attempt to imitate anything sacred to the Na'vi. It sits outside all existing visual vocabularies of allegiance — which is precisely what made it legible as something new, entirely Earth House's own.

Together, the two marks require no shared language to read. They only require being seen and remembered.

## Origin

The idea started, as most good ones did, with Chuchu complaining.

"I'm just saying," she said, boots up on Nika's workbench, ignoring the tools she was undoubtedly displacing, "every time we go up to the Falls, some kid asks their mom if we're RDA. Every time. I don't wanna get mistaken for those bastards for the rest of my life."

"You could stop bringing a shotgun everywhere," Aliya offered mildly, not looking up from the stones she was laying out in some pattern only she understood.

"I could," Chuchu agreed, "and I won't."

"It's not really about the gun," Nika said, turning the wrench over in her prosthetic hand, thinking. It was such a familiar gesture by now that nobody in the room noticed her doing it. "It's about the Na'vi not having a fast way to know we're friendly at a glance. We can't exactly shout Na'vi across a hundred meters of jungle in a crisis."

"You could tattoo 'friend' on your forehead," Kobeni offered, and then immediately looked stricken, like she'd said too much, the way she always did.

"It's a good idea, actually," Miri said, from the corner where he was elbow-deep in a stripped power conduit and had not, as far as anyone could tell, been listening at all. He did not look up. "Not the forehead part. The visible-marker part. We need something that reads at range, low light, doesn't require language. Color's the simplest signal humans have that Na'vi eyes can pick up fast too. Something outside what Mangkwan wears. Something on the body, not just gear, because gear gets lost or borrowed and bodies don't."

"Color coordination," Ojelo said, appearing in the doorway with the specific energy of a man who had clearly been eavesdropping from the hallway for at least a minute before deciding to make it official. "Now we're talking. I've been saying this base needs a bit, we don't have a bit, everyone else has a bit."

"Nobody asked for a bit," Nuno said from behind him, not looking up from the three screens he was somehow monitoring simultaneously.

"I'm asking for it retroactively. Put it in the minutes." Ojelo dropped into the nearest chair. "But he's right. If we're gonna have a symbol, it can't just be practical, it has to feel like something. People don't wear things that don't feel like something."

"It should be simple to make and hard to fake," Lilique said, not glancing up from the supply ledger balanced on her knee, already three mental steps ahead of the conversation the way she always was. "Whatever it is, we're going to need enough of it for everyone, indefinitely, without it becoming a logistics problem. I'm just saying that now so nobody's surprised later."

"Noted," said Martin, who had come in to deliver a manifest and was now standing very still in the doorway, radiating the particular anxiety of a man caught in a room where a decision was being made faster than he could fully process it.

Nika looked down at her own hand — the metal one, the one that had cost her something and that she'd never once tried to hide. "The Na'vi already gave us a name for me," she said slowly. "Silver Hand. What if that becomes the mark. Not literally silver, nobody's got silver lying around, but the hand. Blackened. Grease, paint, whatever's on hand. If someone's under my banner, they mark the same hand."

"And something for the whole group," Aliya added, setting down her last stone like she'd just confirmed something she already suspected. "Cyan reads clean against everything Mangkwan wears. Doesn't try to look like a Na'vi color, doesn't try to imitate anything sacred to them. It's just ours. Plainly."

"I can weld strips of it onto anything metal we carry," Miri said, already halfway back into the conduit. "Till can handle the vehicles. Tape holds fine on cloth."

Till Nys, three benches over, gave a single nod without looking up from what he was doing, which from Till counted as enthusiastic agreement.

"I'll route it through supply so everyone gets theirs at the same time," Lilique said, already writing. "Nobody left out, nobody improvising with the wrong shade because we ran short."

"I can build the actual dye mix stable enough that it doesn't wash out in the first rain," Nuno offered, still not looking away from his screens. "Give me an afternoon."

"I'll test it," Ojelo said. "Somebody's gotta be the one who finds out the hard way if it stains."

"You volunteered for that," Nika said.

"I did. I'm very brave." He held up his hand, already streaked faintly cyan from some earlier, entirely unrelated project, as if to prove a point that proved nothing at all.

Martin cleared his throat from the doorway. "Should — should someone let the Astikasya know we're coming? Before we just show up wearing new things at them?"

"They'll understand when they see it," Nika said, gentler than the question probably needed. "But yes. We'll tell them ourselves. Not just show up different and expect them to guess why."

## Presentation to the Clan

It took less than a week, in the end. A black right hand, and a thread or strip of cyan somewhere visible — a wrapped wrist, a cord at the collar, a painted stripe on a rifle stock, a smear across a mechanic's knuckles that never quite scrubbed clean and eventually nobody tried to scrub clean at all.

They brought it to Sheltering Falls not as an announcement, but as a fact already true. Nika walked at the front with Chuchu and Aliya, hands blackened, cyan bright at their wrists. Kobeni trailed behind with hers a little smudged, redone four times and still not confident she'd gotten it right. Miri and Till Nys came in matching, silent, marked exactly the same way without ever once discussing it beforehand, which surprised no one who knew them. Ojelo's mark was, predictably, the most theatrical of the group, cyan wound halfway up his forearm like he'd decided one wrist wasn't a strong enough statement. Nuno's was practical and precise. Lilique's was perfect, of course, applied with the same quiet competence she brought to everything. Martin's was careful and a little too neat, like a man who had practiced the motion several times in private before trusting himself to do it where anyone could see.

Eyfuwen met them at the threshold, as he always did, with Kaymi a watchful shadow a few steps behind him and 'Ipxerey at his side. He looked at Nika's hand for a long moment — the real mark beneath the paint, the prosthetic itself — and then at the blackened hands of the others, nine of them now instead of four, and understanding moved through his face slow and warm, like sunrise through mist.

"You have chosen a mark," he said. Not a question.

"We wanted your people to know us at a glance," Nika said. "All of us. Without needing the words for it."

'Ipxerey studied them each in turn, the way she studied everything, patient and unhurried, reading more than was said. Her gaze lingered a moment on Martin, who visibly tried and failed to stand less nervously, and softened at whatever she found there.

"You have made yourselves a clan," she said finally. "A clan of Sky People. It is a strange thing, and yet it is not strange at all."

"Is that alright?" Nika asked, and for a woman who led an entire resistance, she sounded almost young asking it.

'Ipxerey's mouth curved, something between amusement and genuine warmth. "Every clan we have ever known began the same way. People choosing to be recognized as one thing, together, so that when danger comes, hands know which hands to reach for." She inclined her head toward Nika's blackened palm. "Yours already carries a story. Now it carries a people as well. All of them."

Kaymi said nothing at all, which from Kaymi was its own kind of blessing. She simply stepped forward, gripped Nika's marked hand in her own for a moment, four-fingered and warm around cold-blackened metal and skin both, and let go.

Behind her, Ojelo leaned toward Chuchu and whispered something that made her snort audibly enough that Kaymi's ear-tufts flicked toward the sound, unimpressed and entirely unsurprised.

Nobody in Earth House had a word ready for how much that single handshake meant, multiplied now across nine people who had all, in their own way, chosen to be counted. They didn't need one. That was rather the point.

## How Each Member Wears It

*Reference notes for consistency in future scenes.*

- **Nika** — blackened prosthetic hand (already metal beneath); cyan cord at the wrist of her organic arm
- **Chuchu** — blackened right hand, grease more often than paint; cyan wrapped around a gun strap
- **Aliya** — cyan thread woven into her divination stone pouch; hand marked simply, without embellishment
- **Kobeni** — hand re-done frequently out of anxious perfectionism; often slightly smudged
- **Miri** — cyan welded directly onto metal gear rather than worn on cloth; hand marked, minimal, functional
- **Martin** — careful, precise application; visibly practiced beforehand
- **Till Nys** — matches Miri's marking style exactly, unspoken and unremarked upon
- **Nuno** — practical, unobtrusive, placed for convenience rather than display
- **Ojelo** — the most visible mark of the group; cyan wound partway up the forearm
- **Lilique** — neat, efficient, applied with the same quiet competence she brings to everything
