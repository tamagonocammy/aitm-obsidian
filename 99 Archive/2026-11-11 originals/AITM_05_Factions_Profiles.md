# Avatar: Into the Mists — Chunk 5: Factions Profiles (Lost Cohort, Scattered Ashes, Resistance, ANAKT)
## The Lost Cohort


### Rängay (Taki Shiina)
**Ikran**: 'Eyeng
**Birthday**: August 9


**Personality:** Rängay's sternness is not a personality trait. It is devotion made audible. Her exacting standards and solitary nature are the marks of a soul that believes in things deeply: in sacred rhythm, in tradition, and in the truth of one's own song. Her path was not the clan's default. Like her mate Txafwe, and her fellow clanswomen Atanvi and Txewan, she came to her identity through Eywa's Eye. For Rängay, this was not just a transition but a spiritual tuning, an aligning of her outer form with the inner rhythm she had always felt singing beneath her skin. This profound, personal communion with Eywa made her connection to ritual intensely personal and fiercely protective. Her sharp corrections of those who treat ceremonies carelessly come from knowing their sacred power firsthand, from having experienced Eywa's transformative grace. She found her purpose in anchoring Sänrri's cohort. When Sänrri arrived as a former dreamwalker with a vulnerable, brilliant connection to Eywa, Rängay saw a kindred spirit: another who had crossed a great divide to find her true self. Her protectiveness was the instinct of a master musician recognizing a rare and fragile instrument. She tuned Sänrri with harsh corrections and silent approval, her way of saying without words: your song is valid, do not let them silence it. This put her on a collision course with Kaymi. Their rivalry was spiritual. Kaymi moved through the world as a force of nature, instinctive and overwhelming. Rängay, the disciplined ritualist who had chosen her own nature, moved through it as a composer. She found Kaymi's intuitive approach borderline heretical, yet could not deny its power. Her secret approval of their union was the ultimate concession: she trusted the warrior to protect the songbird in ways ritual alone never could. The unspoken pact between them was clear: ***guard her with your life, or I will spend mine undoing you***. Her family with Txafwe is her sanctuary. In him, she found another wanderer who understood the weight of choosing a home and the language of rhythm. They recognized each other as people who had rebuilt themselves, who had fought for their own truth through Eywa's grace. Their bond, built on shared transition and repaired harmony, is her quietest rebellion against solitude. With him and their children Nìla and Mowpay, her sternness melts into a fierce, quiet tenderness. In private moments, they share playful silent jokes: a balanced fruit left where he'll find it, a shell arranged just so, small languages of intimacy rebuilt after captivity nearly destroyed it. Her capture by the Mangkwan clan was an attack on her soul's core. To witness sacred rhythm perverted into heresy was to see the Eye of Eywa itself mocked. To be forced to participate in corrupted ceremonies while her mate was somewhere else, while her children waited at home not knowing if she'd return, was a specific kind of torture. Her rigidity in captivity became her lifeline: mentally chanting true ceremonies to drown out the false ones, holding the exact notes like a shield. She didn't just need to escape. She needed to return the true song to her children, to cleanse what had been defiled. Now, returned to Sheltering Falls, Rängay walks a fragile line. The trauma made her tighter, more rigid, as if perfect ritual can ward off blasphemy. She corrects others quickly, her need for purity bordering on compulsion. The younger children sometimes fear her intensity. Yet within her family's space, she is softer. She shares playful, silent moments with Txafwe that speak of intimacy slowly being rebuilt. She lets Lì'may "help" with rituals and genuinely delights in the child's earnest attempts. She watches Sänrri lead ceremonies with fierce, unspoken pride. Her dynamic with Kaymi has deepened into something neither would acknowledge but both understand. They disagree on everything about protecting Sänrri and the clan's spiritual health, their debates sharp and frequent. Yet in a crisis, they operate as one without needing to speak. The warrior and the ritualist, protecting their songbird together. Rängay, who had to fight for her own identity, sometimes perceives Kaymi's born-into-her-role confidence as a kind of privilege she herself never had. This makes their eventual respect all the more powerful. Rängay is the keeper of the sacred rhythm. Her love is a strict tempo, her protection a complex harmony. She guards the traditions that allowed her to become herself, ensuring others may find their true song within them. To be held to her standard is to be seen as capable of greatness. To be corrected by her is to be loved. In the quiet moments when Txafwe's hand finds hers or when she hears Nìla playing the old melodies she taught him, she allows herself to believe that even after the darkness, the true song still resonates.


### Pxayu (Soyo Nagasaki)
**Ikran**: Äwsun
**Birthday**: May 27


**Personality:** Pxayu built herself into a monument against abandonment. Her gentleness, her nurturing, her perfect adaptability are all arguments against a truth seared into her childhood: you were left because you were too much to love. When her birth clan scattered, that message became proof. So she learned to un-learn her own needs. She became what people wanted before they knew they wanted it. Finding Sänrri's cohort felt like finding home. Here were women also searching, also broken. Pxayu saw her purpose clearly: be the anchor, be the nurturer, be so essential they cannot leave. Her bond with Apayì became everything. In Apayì's witness, she saw someone who wouldn't shatter when she showed her need. Finally, she could believe: you can stay if you make yourself necessary enough. But beneath her calm exterior, tsunamis move. Her emotional world runs on abandonment calculus. A missed glance is evidence of imminent departure. A quiet day is the prelude to being forgotten. She doesn't lose herself in others so much as dissolve her boundaries as an offering, building a cage of perfect care designed to make her indispensable. When triggered, she splinters. The person she loves becomes a betrayer. The cohort that felt permanent becomes fragile and moments from shattering. She cycles between desperate clinging and terrified withdrawal. Her training under ‘Ipxerey became less about spirituality and more about excavating her core wound. The Tsahìk recognized what Pxayu truly was: not broken, but brilliantly constructed as a survival mechanism that had stopped working. Apayì became her witness through this slow, agonizing process. Every time the old terror rose that she was too much, Apayì would simply see it. Not fix it, not reassure her away from it, but acknowledge it: I see that you're scared. I'm still here. Her capture by the Mangkwan clan confirmed every fear. Separated from her cohort, torn from Lawarey, Pxayu's survival mechanisms activated with devastating precision. She mimicked the corrupted ceremonies, adapted perfectly to hell itself, because that's what she'd been built to do. The horror wasn't that she was corrupted. It was that her own machinery for preventing abandonment worked flawlessly in a place designed to destroy identity. It proved what she'd always believed: there was no core Pxayu, only empty rooms she built for others to occupy. It was Apayì's voice that kept her tethered. A laugh remembered, a glance across the darkness, the knowledge that someone knew her completely enough that even broken and shape-shifted into heresy, she was still worth saving. Apayì's witness didn't ask her to stop adapting. It saw the crying child beneath the architecture and loved her anyway. Now, returned to Sheltering Falls, Pxayu is learning to build on a completely new foundation, and the work is daily and brutal and sacred. Some mornings she wakes convinced Apayì has left. Some days she performs perfect motherhood for Lawarey, terrified that showing actual need will drive her away. Some moments she catches herself mirroring Sänrri and freezes, wondering if anything authentic is left. But she's learning that Lawarey's love isn't a prize for perfect mothering. It's given to the crybaby she hides. She's learning that saying "I'm terrified you'll leave" is more powerful than any perfect performance. She's learning that Sänrri doesn't need her to be stable. She needs her to be real. Her healing is the slow, terrifying work of believing that her cohort stays for the noise of her, not the silence she constructs. They choose her neediness. They choose the parts that are messy and desperate. They choose the crybaby. Pxayu is learning to build a home where the foundation isn't "you need me so you can't leave," but something more terrifying and beautiful: "I need you, and that's okay." Her presence is no longer an argument against abandonment. It's a quiet, defiant act of faith: building something beautiful with someone, knowing it could all be taken away, and choosing to build it anyway. She still fails sometimes. She collapses into old patterns, performs perfection until she shatters, mistakes kindness for pity. But she's learning to hold her wounds gently, to see her desperate need for belonging not as pathology but as capacity for love. And in the quiet moments, when Lawarey rests against her and Apayì's hand finds hers, Pxayu dares to believe something impossible: *I am not the abandoned one. I am home. And I'm allowed to stay.*


### Apayì (Anon Chihaya)
**Ikran**: Fiay
**Birthday**: September 8


**Personality:** Apayì did not find herself in the sacred rituals of the tsakarem. She found herself in the space between them—in the shared glances, the hesitant confessions, the quiet moments where performance fell away. Her initial, almost desperate vanity—the elaborate adornments, the attempts to set trends—was never about arrogance. It was the terrified sign-language of someone screaming, *"Do I belong here yet? Am I good enough to be seen with you?"* Before Sänrri's cohort, her attempts to lead were dismissed as the efforts of a skxawng. They missed the truth: she wasn't trying to lead them forward; she was trying to weave them together. In Sänrri, the former dreamwalker desperately trying to prove she belonged, Apayì saw her own reflection. Their bond became a silent pact: *I will see you, if you see me.* Under 'Ipxerey's guidance, Apayì's true role emerged not through formal training, but through revelation. She became the cohort's **Witness**—the one who mastered the sacred art of *attention*. While others sought spiritual highs or struggled with doctrine, she noticed the exact moment Pxayu's fingers would fidget with her queue, the tell of an abandonment fear spiking. She could sense Rängay's sternness hardening into brittle armor from a single tightened jaw. She understood that Pamyaw's wandering wasn't disinterest, but a soul conversing in a rhythm only it could hear. She felt Sänrri's constant quiet terror that she might never truly belong. Her elaborate decorations transformed into something sacred. She would craft a specific hairpiece for Pxayu after a hard day, leave a perfectly smooth singing stone where Pamyaw practiced, sit in silence beside Rängay when the weight of spiritual conviction threatened to crack her. Small anchors saying without words: *I see your specific heart. I see who you truly are beneath the fear.* Her vanity became devotion, her desperate need to belong transmuted into the gift of helping others feel seen. Her capture by the Mangkwan clan was the ultimate test of her purpose. In the despair of ash-stained cells, when sacred songs were perverted into heresy and Varang's corrupted rituals defiled everything they held sacred, Apayì's witness became a weapon against oblivion. Her seemingly "airheaded" observations: *"The way the ash falls in this cell is almost pretty, like grey snow,"* or *"Your voice, Rängay, it still holds the true melody beneath the fear"*, were lifelines. They reminded her sisters not of what they were suffering, but of *who they still were*. These weren't naive platitudes; they were the highest form of spiritual resistance: refusing to let despair rewrite their identities. Her most profound act came when she posed as a Mangkwan member to contact Tsu'lan, delivering the desperate plea that would ultimately save them all. It wasn't mere bravery or strategic thinking. It was the ultimate expression of her gift—using her ability to read people and perform the right face, the skill born from her lifelong fear of abandonment, to *see* her sisters' need and answer it. She became the bridge between captivity and freedom because she could perceive what salvation looked like through their eyes. Now, returned to Sheltering Falls, Apayì no longer struggles to choose between being impressive and being real. They have fused. Her elaborate decorations are love letters in physical form: each one a story, a prayer, a witnessed moment. Her playful, legendary bickering with Pxayu is the sound of a bond too strong to break, two mothers raising Lawarey together, two survivors who saw each other in the dark and refused to look away. In raising their daughter, she teaches the ultimate lesson she learned in captivity and beyond: that to be truly seen, in all your messy, terrified, wonderful specificity, is to be saved. Apayì's spirituality is not of grand visions or elaborate ceremonies. It is the profound truth that to witness another's soul—their fears, their strength, their unshakeable worth, it is the highest form of prayer to Eywa. She has become an unofficial counselor-in-training for the clan, the one the youth and the traumatized go to when they need to be *heard* without judgment. She is the bridge between the formal spirituality of the tsahìk and the messy, beautiful reality of healing hearts. Her "trends" now carry purpose: bioluminescent weeds celebrating successful hunts, bracelets that tell stories of personal struggle and survival, turning fashion into narrative therapy. Every adornment is an act of witnessing, every trend a prayer, every small gesture a reminder that in a world trying to strip away your identity, being seen is the most radical form of resistance.


### Pamyaw (Raana Kaname)
**Ikran**: Frrsay
**Birthday**: February 22


**Personality:** To call Pamyaw a wanderer is to mistake the map for the territory. She is not lost; she is simply following a song only she can hear in its entirety. If any soul within the Spires embodies the spirit of a yerik, it is her. Alert, self-possessed, moving to an internal rhythm that answers to no one but Eywa herself. Her connection to music is not a practiced skill but a fundamental sense, as vital as sight or breath. You will not find her in the communal singing circles. Instead, listen for the lone, experimental melody drifting from the sun-dappled highest canopy or echoing from the resonant belly of a waterfall cave. These are her true sanctuaries, where she translates the secret hymns of the forest into notes her clan-mates might one day understand. Her solitary practice defines her place in Sänrri's cohort. She maintains a respectful but tangible distance, attending rituals and training only when the structured harmony offers a counterpoint to her own explorations. To the rigidly traditional, this seems like disrespect. But the truth disarms criticism: her talent for the old ceremonial songs is flawless, her voice and flute-work holding a purity that even the sternest elders recognize as a gift from Eywa. She moves through clan life with the unapologetic honesty of a creature perfectly attuned to its own needs. She trades choice cuts of her hunt for sun-ripened, saccharine tawtute fruit. She politely but firmly refuses the heavily spiced stews at feasts, her palate seeking clarity over complexity. Her tastes are peculiar and unmovable, not out of stubbornness but out of an honest understanding of what actually nourishes her spirit. It was this honest curiosity that drew her to Sänrri's cohort. She appeared one day, materializing from the mist as she does, listened to Sänrri guide the others not with dogma but with intuitive feeling, and declared simply: "This is interesting." She stayed. Not out of need or desperation like the others, but because something in Sänrri's approach resonated with her own philosophy: that true spirituality comes from listening, not performing. Rängay dubbed her a "wandering spirit," a title Pamyaw accepts not as critique but as accurate epithet. Beneath this aloof exterior lies not indifference but a different kind of attention. Like the yerik she sometimes resembles, she observes everything: shifting moods, hidden tensions, unspoken truths. She gathers secrets as naturally as others gather herbs, understanding people through the spaces between their words. She shares these insights sparingly and on her own terms. A perfectly placed hum that changes a song's meaning. A rare flower left on a friend's sleeping mat. A single, blunt sentence that cuts to the heart of a matter. Her loyalty is not demonstrated through constant presence but through these perfectly timed, uncannily perceptive gifts. To the cohort, she was the mirror that reflected truth back without judgment. When Apayì spiraled into performance anxiety, Pamyaw would simply sit nearby, humming an old melody, reminding her that authenticity needed no audience. When Pxayu's abandonment fears threatened to swallow her whole, Pamyaw left a smooth stone in her path, wordlessly saying: I am still here. When Rängay carried the weight of tradition too heavily, Pamyaw's experimental variations on ancient songs showed her that honoring the old didn't mean refusing to explore it. She did not cling to Sänrri like the others. Instead, she created space for the former dreamwalker to exist without scrutiny, understanding that some souls need freedom to find themselves. This paradoxically made her one of Sänrri's most trusted companions. They rarely spoke, but they understood each other in the language of music and silence. The depth of her connection was violently revealed during the Mangkwan captivity. Stripped of her instruments, forced into the ash-choked silence of the heretics' corrupted ceremonies, she did not speak of fear or pain. She whispered, her voice hollow with a specific anguish, that it felt as if Eywa's breath had been stolen from her lungs. For Pamyaw, whose soul converses in melody, this was not mere trauma. It was spiritual suffocation. The silence was not peaceful. It was death. In captivity, she discovered something about herself: her silence could be weaponized. Her ability to observe, to read people, to understand unspoken things became a tool for survival. She noticed which guards responded to certain tones of voice, which rituals Varang cared most about, which moments of vulnerability in her captors could be exploited. Her quiet nature, so often dismissed as disinterest, became her greatest asset. She could move through the camps unseen, gathering information, sharing it with her cohort through the smallest gestures, the briefest hums that carried entire conversations. Now, returned to Sheltering Falls, Pamyaw has integrated her captivity's hard lessons into her already formidable perceptiveness. She is still the wandering spirit, still following her own internal rhythm, still appearing and disappearing into the mist as she pleases. But there is a sharpness to her now, a knowing. She understands more fully the power of her attention, the weight of her silence. She has become an unofficial scout and information gatherer for the clan, using her gift for observation to keep them safe. Yet she maintains her essential nature: she shares what she knows on her own terms, leaves clues rather than answers, trusts people to understand her in the language of music and gesture rather than words. Apayì knows how to listen for her hums. Pxayu knows to look for her gifts. Rängay has learned that her seemingly random observations often contain profound wisdom. Pamyaw is the peripheral vision of the Astikasya, the one who hears the melody in the silence and remembers the tunes that others forget. Her gift is her freedom, and her loyalty is the quiet, chosen rhythm of her return. She may never be found in the communal circles, but when the clan needs her most, she is already there, observing, understanding, offering her peculiar, essential gifts. To know Pamyaw is to understand that commitment cannot be measured by attendance but by the profound, idiosyncratic song she adds to the clan's chorus.


## The Scattered Ashes

### Syayvi (Sakiko Togawa)
**Ikran**: Tìfnu
**Birthday**: February 14


**Personality:** Syayvi, once named Tswa’avä as a mark of exile, is a charismatic yet deeply conflicted individual who leads The Scattered Ashes with a mix of strength and hidden pain. Originally part of the Astikasya clan alongside her twin sister Tìmeyo, her life changed forever when they were exiled for killing their father after his neglect led to their mother Luey’s death. Branded with a new name and forbidden from connecting with Eywa, she and her sister found temporary refuge with the Mangkwan clan, only to discover corruption and heresy towards Eywa that drove them to forge their own path under the rule of the Mangkwan’s tsahìk: Varang. Due to this, using her natural charisma and emotional intelligence helped gather others to their cause, including her devoted mate Nì’syu, and sister Tìmeyo, two other Na’vi from the Tayrangi and Ta’unui clans, Txafwe and Yawyay, and the Astikasya’s **Lost Cohort**, a group of women who were Sänrri’s closest companions during her spiritual training, they formed The Scattered Ashes (Na’vi: **Veykawniraä Txeva**) as a group of escapees from the Ash People. Now returning to the Astikasya clan years later during the Second Pandoran War, she carries herself with cold dignity that masks deeper wounds – guilt over past choices, unresolved feelings about her exile, and complex emotions about seeing Sänrri in the role of tsakarem. During the Siege of the Falls, when the ANAKT Fireteam occupied Sheltering Falls while the adults were away assisting Jake Sully and his family against the RDA and Varang herself, Syayvi proved her commitment to her adopted home beyond question. Pregnant with Nì’syu’s child, she fought with desperate ferocity alongside Tsu’lan against overwhelming odds, protecting the clan’s children and defending the sacred spaces she had once been forbidden to enter. Her coordination with the wandering warrior became legendary, two exiles fighting side by side, their combined strength a testament to redemption earned through action rather than words. When the other Spears finally returned, Syayvi stood among them not as a petitioner seeking acceptance, but as a warrior who had already bled for the clan. Her valor during those brutal hours earned her **the Ashen Spear**, carved from wood burned during the Siege itself, and recognition as the Ninth Spear, **the Penitent** who chose to protect rather than destroy. In the final confrontation against Recom Luka in the Cave of Truths, Syayvi fought alongside the other Eight Spears and her Scattered Ashes in perfect synchronization, her redemption arc reaching its culmination as she struck in unison with those who had once cast her out. The sacred pond ran red with the blood of the Strangler Fig, and Syayvi’s spear, forged from her own past failures, became an instrument of justice. Her participation in that coordinated strike, fighting to protect the very clan that had exiled her, represented the complete transformation from Tswa’avä the outcast to Syayvi, the protector. Despite often casting herself as the antagonist, Syayvi’s true strength lies in her ability to create belonging for others who feel lost, even as she struggles with her own place in the world. Her journey of redemption brings her full circle to confront not just the clan that exiled her, but the consequences of all her choices since then. Now carrying Nì’syu’s child, she faces the additional weight of bringing new life into a world she’s still trying to find her place in, her impending motherhood adding both desperate hope and paralyzing terror to her already complex inner world. Beneath her composed exterior lies a profound love for those she left behind, though she buries these emotions under layers of self-recrimination. Her leadership of The Scattered Ashes represents both her greatest achievement and her deepest struggle – creating a new family while still yearning for the one she lost, and now preparing to nurture new life while haunted by the destructive patterns of her own upbringing. The Ashen Spear she now wields serves as a constant reminder that redemption is not granted: it is earned, again and again, through the choice to protect rather than destroy.


### Tìmeyo (Mutsumi Togawa) and Kerevi (Mortis)
**Ikran**: Fe'pey
**Birthday**: January 14


**Personality:** Tìmeyo is quiet and withdrawn, carrying herself with a stoic exterior that masks profound emotional fragility. She struggles to express herself properly, her few carefully chosen words often landing wrong—perceived as blunt or insensitive despite genuine care. When stress becomes overwhelming, she doesn't break down but simply *stops*, dissociating so completely she becomes functionally nonverbal, lost somewhere deep inside where the world cannot reach her. This is when **Kerevi** emerges: a fully distinct alter within their DID system, born during the killing of their father when that trauma became too immense for one consciousness to bear alone. Where Tìmeyo is paralyzed by emotion, Kerevi is present and confident. But she's also deeply *childish* not in body but in spirit. She speaks in a patchwork of borrowed phrases collected from everyone Tìmeyo has known: Syayvi's commanding declarations, Kaymi's protective instincts, Eyfuwen's storytelling cadences, even Varang's twisted rhetoric and Quaritch's military efficiency. She mimics unconsciously, cobbling together a personality from fragments because she was born with no template for how to be. "Why are we walking?" she'll ask when a destination is fifty paces away. "We have ikran. Why wouldn't we fly?" Her logic is the simple directness of a child who cannot comprehend why adults complicate everything. This creates constant friction. She'll use Txafwe's teaching phrases back at him with childish enthusiasm, then pivot to Rängay's stern corrections mid-sentence, completely unaware that this verbal whiplash disturbs others. When frustrated, she becomes genuinely petulant—crossing her arms, tail lashing, muttering "This is stupid" like an irritated child. Her problem-solving is dangerously simplistic: "If the RDA wants this territory, we kill all of them. Problem solved." Not cruelty but just a child's if-then logic without understanding consequence or complexity. Most unsettling was her relationship with the Mangkwan’s clan heresy. Where Tìmeyo recoiled in spiritual horror, Kerevi approached Varang's corrupted rituals with a child's dangerous curiosity, not belief but fascination. "Oh, so if you twist the prayer this way, people obey. Interesting." She participated with the concentration of someone learning a new game, not understanding that some games destroy souls. She survived captivity by pattern recognition and pleasing authority figures, memorizing which prayers kept them safe without comprehending *why* it mattered. But beneath the childishness lies fierce protectiveness. "*I'm Tìmeyo's savior,*" she says with absolute conviction. Her entire existence is rooted in one purpose: be what Tìmeyo cannot. Be strong when Tìmeyo is weak, present when Tìmeyo is lost, able to *act* when Tìmeyo is paralyzed. During the **Siege of the Falls**, this dynamic reached its peak. Kerevi fronted with increasing frequency during combat, thriving in situations with clear rules: enemy approaches, you stop them. No moral ambiguity, just direct action. She fought with borrowed techniques: Kaymi's strikes, Tsu'lan's stances, even Mangkwan formations, cobbled into something uniquely effective, alongside a childish, but chilling laughter. Fighting alongside Syayvi to protect the children, both alters worked in genuine tandem: Tìmeyo's loyalty providing the *why*, Kerevi's clarity providing the *how*. The final strike against Luka forced them both to understand a fundamental truth: redemption wasn't about integration or Tìmeyo "getting better." They were both real, both necessary, both part of the same person who survived by becoming plural. The Siege didn't heal their DID, but it taught them functional coexistence as a system. Now they've reached uneasy equilibrium. Tìmeyo still carries spiritual yearning for their lost calling as tsakarem, deep connection to Syayvi, unspoken doubts about their exile. Kerevi still speaks in borrowed phrases with childish directness that frustrates everyone, still lacks social grace, still approaches problems with simplistic logic. But they're learning to work together, switching based on what situations require rather than fighting for control. Among the Scattered Ashes, everyone has learned to identify who's fronting: Tìmeyo is quiet and careful, Kerevi is loud and present. Syayvi loves both versions of her twin, speaking gently with Tìmeyo and setting clear boundaries with Kerevi, who responds well to structure. Watching Syayvi prepare for motherhood affects them differently, Tìmeyo feels complex grief about ever feeling whole enough to create life, while Kerevi feels uncomplicated excitement: "We're going to have a baby\! Can I hold it?" Despite developing her own identity and fierce autonomy, Kerevi has never tried to permanently take control. Even in Tìmeyo's darkest dissociative episodes, Kerevi dives into the internal abyss to pull her back, her childlike joy and relief when Tìmeyo resurfaces—*"I found you\! You came back\!"*—revealing the truth: they need each other. Kerevi cannot exist without Tìmeyo's core self to give her meaning. Tìmeyo cannot survive their trauma without Kerevi's protection. They are one person with DID, learning to live in a world that does not understand what that means. They are proof that survival sometimes means fracturing, that healing doesn't always mean becoming singular, that strength can look like accepting your own multiplicity. They are adapted, not broken. And in a world as harsh as theirs, adaptation is its own form of grace.


### Nì'syu/‘Awll (Uika/Hatsune Misumi)
**Ikran**: Hawnyu
**Birthday**: June 26


**Personality:** ​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​What most know as Nì'syu conceals a profound secret \- she is not the Na'vi she claims to be. Born as **'Awll,** she was forced to assume the identity of her twin sister after a tragic event claimed the original Nì'syu's life. Compelled by her family to maintain this deception due to the powerful alliance it preserved with Syayvi's lineage, she has lived a fractured existence, presenting a serene, kind exterior while harboring a hollow emptiness within. Her seemingly natural empathy masks years of careful observation and mimicry of her sister's mannerisms. Her obsession with Syayvi transcends mere devotion \- the moment they joined through tsaheylu during their mating ritual became her first experience of true connection, as Syayvi instinctively understood her hidden truth without words. This profound revelation explains her fiercely protective nature toward her mate and the child Syayvi now carries \- they represent the only authentic relationship in a life built on deception. Her time with the Mangkwan clan under Varang's corrupted regime added another layer of horror to her fractured identity \- forced to participate in heretical rituals that perverted sacred Na'vi traditions while maintaining her false persona, she watched as everything she held sacred was twisted into mockery. The collaboration with Quaritch's forces felt like the final betrayal of her true self, as she was compelled to aid the very humans who represented everything antithetical to Na'vi existence. Her fascination with celestial observation stems from childhood attempts to escape her reality, finding in the constancy of the stars the only reliable truth in her uncertain existence. The fear that flashes in her eyes when accidentally addressed by her true name reveals the depth of her vulnerability despite her carefully maintained composure. Her connection to starlight and practice of celestial divination serve dual purposes \- both genuine spiritual practice and perfect cover for her frequent need to isolate herself when the weight of her false identity becomes overwhelming. Now with Syayvi carrying their son **Sìlpey**, she faces both hope and terror \- hope that in creating new life with her mate she might finally claim authentic existence, and terror that her child might somehow become entangled in the web of deception surrounding her true identity. Those closest to her note how she shifts between profound presence and disturbing absence, her amethyst eyes sometimes reflecting a depth of loneliness that even Syayvi cannot fully reach. This duality explains why even members of the Scattered Ashes find her increasingly unsettling to truly know \- beneath her gentle smile lies not just fierce will, but a fundamental identity fracture that makes her both desperately loyal and potentially unpredictable.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​ During the Siege, while facing the ANAKT Fireteam’s and the Mangkwan’s occupation, Nì’syu fought with a desperate fury that stripped away years of careful pretense, in the chaos of protecting her mate and their unborn son, she moved as ‘Awll, her true self, no longer constrained by the false identity that had defined her existence. The life-or-death stakes revealed what tsaheylu had already shown Syayvi: that her authentic devotion transcended any deception, her fierce protectiveness more real than any carefully maintained façade. Fighting in perfect coordination with Syayvi and the other Ashes during the final battle against Luka, she experienced something profound, in combat, protecting those she loved, her true name didn’t matter because her actions spoke louder than any identity ever could. The Siege became the moment when ‘Awll and Nì’syu ceased to be separate masks and became simply herself—a warrior, a mate, a soon-to-be mother fighting for the future rather than hiding from the past.

### Txafwe (Umiri Yahata)
**Ikran**: Tximya
**Birthday**: April 7


**Personality:** Despite his striking appearance with warrior markings and the stern demeanor typical of the seafaring Tayrangi clan, Txafwe speaks with unexpected politeness and maintains a straightforward, easygoing nature that contrasts with his intimidating exterior. Born to the Tayrangi, the Ikran People of the Eastern Sea, his formative years were spent as a gifted ceremonial rhythm-keeper who led a small ensemble of song-sharers that traveled between neighboring settlements. Maybe he might finally be safe. Maybe he might finally deserve to live. The Scolopendra, who tried to solve for love with equations and strategy, was ultimately solved by it. **Recom Sua (The Clematis)**

She refused all Mangkwan ash paint, not just for stealth, but because it was an affiliation, a connection to a group, that her guarded nature inherently rejected. Paint was claiming. She killed face-to-face, with bladed weapons, moving close enough in her final moments to search her victim's eyes. It was as if in severing a life, she was desperately seeking a recognition or understanding that her own fractured existence denied her. During the Siege, Sua moved through the passages of Sheltering Falls like a phantom, hunting the children she'd been assigned to eliminate. This was the only thing she was good at. Until she found them. Syulang emerged from the mist with her twins and her baby boy, and for just a moment, Sua faltered. Not out of compassion, but out of recognition. Here was a family unit. Here was the connection she missed. Here was everything Sua had sacrificed by choosing isolation. The Clematis and the Phantom met in near-absolute silence, their duel almost quiet in its brutality. Two warriors moved with minimal wasted motion, their blades singing not in triumphant arcs but in precise, calculating strikes. The connection that had anchored him fractured, leaving him untethered and desperate for anything loud enough to drown out the void where her presence should be. The loss of Sua sent him spiraling into obsession and rage, his strategic mind fracturing because the one person who made him feel less alone was simply gone. Mizi, the woman who was her universe and her only safe harbor, was unmoored. The person she'd devoted herself to completely, the only one she'd allowed close enough to touch, was simply gone. Her duet was now forever a solo performance, a song forever incomplete. Sua, the quiet girl who loved observing the stars, who loved Mizi with a fierceness that terrified her, who had spent her entire existence learning to disappear so completely that even in death she would leave only silence behind, became the first ghost of the Siege. A silent casualty whose absence screams louder than any battle cry, haunting every life she touched. She exists now in the spaces between words, in the things people don't say, in the guilt that Txewan carries like a prayer, in the void that swallowed Ivan's stability, in the harmony that Mizi sings alone to the mist, in the children that Syulang was able to protect because Sua hesitated for just one moment too long. The Clematis grew in darkness, clung to those closest to her, and was cut down in silence by someone who understood her completely. And the world is diminished for her passing, even as it is saved by her death.

**Recom Mizi (The Witch)**

**Personality:** Mizi was a soul that survived the crucible only to be forged into a wound. Her neural backup performed a cruel alchemy, preserving the very essence that made her Mizi: her profound, self-sacrificing empathy, her bright love for Sua, and the deep-seated belief that she was a survivor who brought only pain to those she cherished. As a Recombinant, this translated into fragile, furious protectiveness. She fought not for a cause or mission objective, but to shield the last remnants of her "squad," to keep alive the one person who made her existence feel less hollow. Her love for Sua was a tether she clung to with desperate strength, the only thing anchoring her consciousness to something resembling purpose. Everything she did was to protect that bond, to ensure Sua survived, to prove that she could be the protector her guilt insisted she'd failed to be. Always, impossibly, still tethered to the irreplaceable half of her soul.

**Recom Till (The Thornbriar)**
