# The Great Games Legacy

## Part I: The Celebration

The Heart of the Plains stretched endlessly under Naranawm's silver light, dotted with the colorful banners of the Western Frontier clans. The Great Games had concluded that afternoon with spectacular victories—the Astikasya had dominated in archery competitions, while their dance-fighting demonstrations had left even the proud Zeswa warriors impressed.

Now, as the evening festivities reached their peak, the three young couples sat in a circle around their small fire, sharing stories and passing around gourds of fermented zangke. The zakru milk beverage was stronger than usual this year, the Zeswa hosts had proudly announced, aged to perfection in the deep caves of the plains.

"Irayo si, ma Eywa, for this blessed gathering," Atanvi raised her gourd with slightly unsteady hands, her usual stutter completely gone thanks to the alcohol's confidence. "For bringing our clans together in harmony."

"Srane!" Tsa'rawm laughed loudly, his boisterous energy amplified. "And for letting us show these plain-runners how real warriors fight!" He gestured wildly, nearly spilling his drink.

Mìreya rolled her eyes but smiled fondly. "You nearly fell off your direhorse during the racing competition, ma yawne."

"That was tactical dismounting!" Tsa'rawm protested, causing everyone to burst into laughter.

Rewon shook her head with amusement, her usual reserved demeanor softened by the celebration and drink. "At least you stayed on longer than that Zeswa champion who challenged you."

"The look on his face when Tsa'rawm's pa'li overtook him in the final stretch..." Atanvi giggled, a sound so rare and musical that Mìreya's ears twitched with delight.

From the neighboring fire, Txewan's gentle voice drifted over. "We should probably rest soon. Tomorrow's journey back to Sheltering Falls is long..."

"Srane, ma txe'lan," Syulang agreed with a yawn. "The little ones will be excited to hear about everything we've seen."

The two couples bid their goodnights, leaving the remaining four around the dying embers. The air grew thick with unspoken tension as meaningful glances passed between mates.

Mìreya moved closer to Atanvi, her hand finding her wife's. "The stars are particularly beautiful tonight."

"Like you," Atanvi whispered, her usual shyness replaced by bold honesty. The zangke had loosened more than just her tongue—her whole body seemed to flow like water as she leaned into Mìreya's touch.

Across the fire, Rewon and Tsa'rawm were having their own silent conversation. His usual loud energy had focused into something more intense, predatory almost, while her composed exterior showed cracks of barely contained desire.

"Perhaps," Tsa'rawm said, his voice lower than usual, "we should continue this celebration somewhere more... private."

Rewon's golden eyes flashed in the firelight. "I was thinking the same thing, ma tsamsiyu."

Without another word, both couples rose and disappeared into the darkness beyond the camp, leaving only the whisper of grass and the distant sound of ongoing festivities.

## Part II: The Discovery

Several weeks had passed since the Great Games, and life at Sheltering Falls had returned to its normal rhythm. The morning mists swirled around the clan's terraces as usual, but something felt different to both Mìreya and Rewon as they made their way toward 'Ipxerey's healing hut.

Mìreya had been experiencing strange symptoms—waves of nausea that struck without warning, unusual food cravings, and a peculiar sensitivity to certain scents. This morning, she'd barely made it out of bed before rushing to empty her stomach behind their kelku.

She rounded the corner of the path and nearly collided with Rewon, who looked equally peaked and uncomfortable.

"Rewon?" Mìreya blinked in surprise. "What brings you to see 'Ipxerey?"

"I could ask you the same," Rewon replied, studying her friend's face with concern. "You look as unwell as I feel."

"You too?" Mìreya's strategic mind immediately began connecting dots. "The nausea? The strange aversions?"

Rewon nodded grimly. "Tsa'rawm's favorite breakfast nearly made me lose what little I'd managed to eat yesterday. And certain scents..." She shuddered.

They looked at each other for a long moment, the same realization dawning on both faces.

"The Great Games," Mìreya said quietly.

"That night by the fire," Rewon confirmed.

"Oh, Eywa," they said in unison.

Inside the hut, they found 'Ipxerey grinding medicinal herbs while Sänrri organized ceremonial supplies. Both elder women looked up with knowing smiles as the younger Na'vi entered together.

"Let me guess," 'Ipxerey said with twinkling eyes, "you're both here about some mysterious illness that's been plaguing you for the past few weeks?"

Sänrri giggled, a sound that had become more frequent since her bonding with Kaymi. "The timing is rather coincidental, isn't it?"

"It's not coincidental at all," 'Ipxerey said warmly, gesturing for them to sit. "Tell me, when did these symptoms begin?"

After a thorough examination and some gentle questioning, the verdict was clear. Both women sat in stunned silence as 'Ipxerey confirmed what they'd suspected.

"Congratulations," the tsahìk said gently. "Eywa has blessed both of your families. The babies should arrive around the same time, if my calculations are correct."

Mìreya and Rewon looked at each other and burst into simultaneous laughter and tears.

"Atanvi's going to faint," Mìreya said through her tears.

"Tsa'rawm's going to shout so loud the whole clan will know before I can tell him properly," Rewon added, wiping her eyes.

"That's what makes it beautiful," Sänrri said softly. "New life born from celebration and joy."

## Part III: Fifteen Years Later

The afternoon sun filtered through the canopy as Atanvi, Mìreya, Rewon, and several other clan mothers watched their children practice archery under Kayan's stern instruction. Rawìl moved with quiet precision, each arrow finding its mark with calculated accuracy. Beside him, Anake's shots were equally skilled but delivered with more flair and commentary.

"Show-off," Rawìl muttered as Anake performed an unnecessary spin before releasing his arrow.

"Jealous," Anake shot back with a grin, landing another perfect hit.

"They're like night and day," Rewon observed, settling beside Mìreya on the woven mat. "Yet they work so well together."

"That's the way with the best partnerships," Mìreya replied, unconsciously reaching for Atanvi's hand. "Different strengths, shared heart."

Atanvi squeezed her mate's fingers gently. "Do you ever think about that night? At the Great Games?"

"Every time I look at them," Mìreya admitted. "How could two people who started life at the same celebration be so perfectly suited as friends and rivals?"

"Eywa's plan," Rewon said simply. "She knew they'd need each other."

They watched as Rawìl helped Anake adjust his stance for a more difficult shot, the quiet leader supporting his boisterous friend without being asked. When Anake succeeded, his celebration was loud enough to startle nearby birds, but Rawìl's small smile showed genuine pride.

"Sometimes I wonder what our lives would have been like if we hadn't gotten quite so... enthusiastic that night," Atanvi said with a rare giggle.

"Quieter," Mìreya said immediately.

"Definitely quieter," Rewon agreed, watching Anake attempt to show off for some of the younger children who had gathered to watch.

"Less chaotic," Atanvi added.

"Much less chaotic," Mìreya confirmed as Anake's demonstration resulted in him tangling himself in the practice rope.

They sat in comfortable silence for a moment, watching their sons help each other untangle the mess Anake had created, their laughter mixing with the other sounds of clan life around them.

"But would you change it?" Rewon asked quietly.

Atanvi looked at Rawìl, who was patiently teaching one of the younger children proper bow grip with infinite care. She thought of his fierce protection of his sister, his quiet strength, his dedication to becoming worthy of the leadership role that awaited him.

Mìreya watched Anake finally free himself from the rope and immediately start planning an even more elaborate trick shot, his optimism and humor undaunted by temporary failure. She thought of how his laughter filled their kelku, how his dreams stretched beyond the horizon, how his friendship had shaped her serious son into someone who could both lead and laugh.

Rewon observed both boys working together to set up the next challenge, turning practice into play, competition into cooperation. She thought of the countless hunts they'd returned from together, scraped and grinning, the way they pushed each other to be better, the way they'd already sworn to be brothers in all but blood.

"No," all three mothers said at once.

"Definitely not," Atanvi added, her voice full of warmth.

"It was worth it," Mìreya confirmed, watching their sons' easy partnership.

"All of it," Rewon concluded.

As if sensing their mothers' attention, both boys looked over and waved—Rawìl with his characteristic small gesture, Anake with his typical dramatic flourish. The mothers waved back, their hearts full of gratitude for that long-ago night when celebration, zangke, and starlight had combined to create something beautiful.

"Irayo si, ma Eywa," Atanvi whispered, "for the Great Games, for strong zangke, and for the courage to celebrate life."

"Srane," Mìreya and Rewon agreed together, settling in to watch their children grow into the future leaders and protectors of their clan.

The Great Games had given them more than victories and memories—they had given them the next generation, born from joy and raised in love.
