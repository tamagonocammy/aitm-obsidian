The **laysrew** ("free dance") is a traditional, ecstatic form of movement embraced by the Astikasya during celebrations, rites of passage, and moments of collective release. It is a full-body expression of energy, joy, and communion, resembling what humans might compare to a "mosh pit." Participants enter a designated space where rhythm and emotion take over-jumping, spinning, slamming, and laughing, all to the beat of hand drums and bone flutes.

  

There are no steps, no leaders. Each dancer moves in response to their own heartbeat and the collective rhythm. Laysrew is sacred but unrestrained, offering a safe outlet for wild emotion, grief, or exuberance. It is common during bonding ceremonies, victory feasts, or after long mourning periods.

  

For the younger members of the clan, a child-safe version exists: **Laysrewtsyìp**. This gentler form maintains the core values of free expression and rhythmic release but within a guided, playful environment. It is often one of the first communal rituals a child participates in, building both confidence and cultural belonging.

  

Participation in Laysrew is always optional, but few choose to stay still when the drums begin to speak.
