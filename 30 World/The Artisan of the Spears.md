The **Artisan of the Spears** is among the most sacred roles within the Astikasya, and yet it carries no seat of power, no title in council, no voice in war. This is by design. When a hunter has demonstrated exceptional skill and strength, or has committed great deeds in service to their family, their clan, and Eywa, the tsahìk herself names them worthy. Only then does the Artisan begin their work, not before, and never of their own judgment. The separation is intentional. A Spear made from ambition would be a hollow thing. A Spear made from pure witness is something else entirely.

  

The Artisan studies the warrior before the first cut of wood is made. They observe how the warrior moves, how they speak to their children, how they behave when they believe no one is watching. The ceremonial weapon that emerges is not what the Artisan imagines — it is what the warrior *already is*, made physical. This is why no two Spears are alike, and why a warrior who holds their Spear for the first time so often goes quiet.

  

In exchange for this gift of sight and craft, the Artisan renounces two things: the mantle of Spear, and the furthering of their own bloodline. They will not be defended by a Spear that bears their name, nor will they leave behind children to carry their legacy. Their legacy lives instead in every weapon they have ever made — in every oath sworn over their craft at the Tree of Souls, in every warrior who plants their Spear in the sacred circle and speaks Eywa's name. The Artisan is chosen by the tsahìk alone, for the role demands someone whose heart is without hunger for recognition, and only the tsahìk can see that clearly enough to be sure.

  

The current Artisan is **Txolaw te Haykxa Kamun'itan**, mated to the *mowarsiyu* Kayan. He accepted the role without ceremony or celebration, which 'Ipxerey later said was exactly why she chose him.
