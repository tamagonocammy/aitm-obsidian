## The Lake

South of the Spires, where the rivers of the highlands finally slow and surrender their long descent, the land opens into a great inland lake, vast enough that its far shore vanishes into haze, ringed by distant mountains that rise like watchful elders from the mist. The Astikasya call it simply **the Lake** — for there is only one, and no other name is needed to know which water is meant. Fed by Torn Thread River, Knotted River, Flowing Spirit River, and Lithing Bolt River, it gathers into itself everything the Spires send down: rain, snowmelt, silt, story. If the Cave of Truth is where the clan speaks to Eywa, the Lake is where Eywa listens back in silence, still and dark and endless, a mirror laid flat beneath the sky. On clear nights Polyphemus rises enormous and amber over its surface, and the water holds the reflection so perfectly that Na'vi flying low over it in the dark have sworn, more than once, that they were flying between two skies.

Unlike the reef-bound Metkayina, whose entire way of life is built around salt and tide and the moving sea, the Astikasya's relationship to water is a freshwater one: still rather than restless, gathering rather than churning. The Lake does not crash or reclaim; it holds. Its creatures are creatures of calm water, its islands sit close and gentle rather than scattered and storm-carved, and its shallows near the reeds of the Whispering Meadow are said to sing faintly when the wind moves through them, a sound the elders describe as the Lake humming to itself. The clan does not live directly upon its shore — the Falls remain high in the Spires, closer to the mist and the old sacred ground — but the Lake is neither distant nor foreign to them. Hunting and gathering parties travel to its edges in the warmer months, ikran riders cross its breadth to reach the far settlements of the Western Frontier, and it is spoken of the way one speaks of a grandmother: present, patient, older than memory, deserving of quiet reverence rather than daily familiarity.

There is one exception to the Lake's single, unadorned name, and it belongs to Sänrri alone. In the private language she has never fully set down — not Na'vi, not really Japanese anymore either, but something that lives in the space between the two — she calls it after a lake from the life she left behind on Earth, one shaped, the old stories said, like the body of an instrument. She has never asked permission to name it this way, and she has never announced that she does. It surfaces only in the quiet of night flights, in the moment before sleep, in the particular stillness that settles over her when Towa banks low over the water and the gas giant burns golden across its surface. Whether this name will ever be spoken aloud to another living soul is a question that belongs to her, and to whoever she someday chooses to tell.

### The Mist, Explained

The persistent mist shrouding Sheltering Falls is not incidental — it is the Lake's breath. Warm, moist air rises constantly off the Lake's broad surface (evaporation fog), and when that moist air meets the sharp rise of the Spires, it is forced upward and cools until it condenses into the mist that wraps the clan's home (orographic fog). The two effects work together: the Lake exhales, the mountains lift what it exhales, and the result is the fog the Astikasya have always lived inside. This also explains why the mist is not constant — shifts in temperature and wind change how much moisture the Lake releases and how much lift the terrain provides on a given day, which is what allows the mist to thin or clear entirely on rare occasions.

### The View from the Falls

On an ordinary day, the Lake cannot be seen from Sheltering Falls at all — the same mist that shelters the clan also hides the wider world from view. But on rare clear mornings or evenings, when the fog finally lifts, the southern horizon opens completely and the Lake becomes visible: small, silver, and impossibly far below. This does not happen on command. It is a gift of weather, not something to be climbed toward or summoned, and it carries the same rarity-as-reverence weight as a shooting star, except it can last a whole morning instead of a second. There is likely a specific watch-post or ledge at Sheltering Falls where people gather, almost involuntarily, when the mist breaks — and elders may recognize the particular quality of light that means the Lake will be visible that day before it happens.

When the mist happens to lift on a significant day — a laysrewtsyìp, a first-water rite — it is read as auspicious: Eywa clearing the veil on purpose, for that one child, for that one morning.

## Rituals & Customs of the Lake

**The First-Water Rite.** A two-part threshold tradition. Long before a child ever travels to the Lake's shore, they are carried out to the viewing ledge at the Falls as a baby or very small child and shown the Lake from far above — distant, half-mythical, seen only if the mist happens to lift that day. Only later, during the warm months, does the child actually travel to the Lake itself, where an elder formally introduces them at its edge: not a baptism, but a naming-toward — *this is the Lake, it has always been here, it will remember you now too.*

**Grief Offerings.** A quieter, secondary mourning practice distinct from the primary rites held at the Cave of Truth. Something is released into the Lake's water rather than burned or spoken aloud — fitting the clan's existing belief that waters carry memory. Some who grieve wait specifically for the mist to lift before making the journey down, as though the ability to see the Lake from the Falls that morning is itself part of the permission to go.

**The Seasonal Lakeshore Gathering.** A communal, joyful tradition tied to the warm months — possibly linked to a fish run or the flowering of the Great Waterleaf. Food, music, and celebration at the water's edge; the clan's version of letting itself be soft after long stretches of hardship.

**Night Flights.** An informal, private tradition rather than a clan-wide ceremony — couples or close bonds flying low over the Lake after dark specifically to see Polyphemus doubled in the water's reflection. Not formally named or ritualized, simply a pattern that exists.

## Landmarks of the Spires

*(Reference for the Western Frontier territory surrounding Sheltering Falls — hunting grounds, sacred sites, and neighboring dangers.)*

**The Northern Reaches** — Weeping Canyon and Shaded Falls mark the clan's furthest boundary to the north, cold country bordering territory the Astikasya rarely travel. Mountain's Drop descends from there toward Pebbled Strand and Father's Judgement, the latter a landmark whose name suggests old reckonings the clan has not fully told.

**The Contested Highlands** — Drowning Rock and the RDA mining scar northeast of it sit uneasy neighbors to The Idle Plateau, Torn Thread River, and Little Bowl — territory close enough to industrial encroachment that hunting parties travel it carefully. The Crying Mountain looms at the far eastern edge, its name alone enough to keep most from asking why.

**The Home Territory** — Tethered Spires, Overlook Falls, Traveler's Lookout, and Serenity Falls ring closer to Sheltering Falls itself, everyday country the clan knows by heart. Knotted River and Canyon's Tear feed the lower valleys, while Forager's Return, Ikran's Peril, Mother's Rise, and Last Plunge mark the more perilous ground where the Spires demand real respect — names that read like warnings passed down from hunters who did not always survive learning them.

**The Falls' Doorstep** — Hunter's Cry, The Grieving Steps, Tumbling Rapids, and Flowing Spirit River sit closest to home, alongside Eldest Sister and Homeward Ravine — the last stretch of country before the settlement itself, the ground every returning hunter crosses to know they are safe.

**The Southern Descent** — Deep Breath Highland, Towering Flatlands, Bathing Twins, and Hunter's Valley mark the long slope down from the mountains toward warmer country, with The Lonely Lake sitting apart from the greater waters entirely — its own small, separate stillness.

**The Lakeshore** — Where the rivers finally empty into the great Lake, the land softens into Whispering Meadow, Jagged Cove, Great Waterleaf, and The Gentle Islands, the calmest country in all the Western Frontier, and the closest the Astikasya come to the water that holds everything the Spires have ever sent down to it.
