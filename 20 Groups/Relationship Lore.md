# Avatar: Into the Mists — Relationship Lore

**By Millie Caicedo**

> *This file holds the full emotional and narrative depth of the AU's most significant relationships — the ones that need more than a line to carry correctly. Organized by type. For a fast scannable list of who is bonded to whom, see AITM_Relationships_QuickRef.md.*

---

## Mates & Romantic Bonds

### Atanvi & Mìreya
The bedrock couple. Mìreya is the person who reminds Atanvi of her worth when she forgets it, which is often. Atanvi is the steady heart that gave Mìreya somewhere to put all the love she had always been afraid to show. They met in circumstances that put them on opposite sides of tension — Tsu'ong once challenged Atanvi for Mìreya's attention and was humiliated — and what emerged was a bond built on choosing each other repeatedly, deliberately, across years. Mìreya maintains a controlled exterior with almost everyone; Atanvi is one of the few people in whose presence she lets it go entirely. Their children Rawìl and Notä are the living proof of what they built.

### Kaymi & Sänrri
Protective force and vulnerable brilliance. Kaymi is the kind of warrior who does not need to justify her love, it manifests as action, as presence, as an ikran waiting outside a ceremony so that Sänrri does not walk out alone. Sänrri was a former dreamwalker who arrived fragile and brilliant and terrified of not belonging, and Kaymi chose her with the same ferocity she brings to combat. There is something in Sänrri that Kaymi recognizes as worth guarding at all costs. There is something in Kaymi that Sänrri found before she found herself. Their relationship with Lì'may is its own arc — the found daughter who made them a family before they fully realized they already were one.

### Kayan & Txolaw
Discipline and peace. Kayan is all duty and iron self-control; Txolaw is legendary laziness and the deepest spiritual gift in the Haykxa family. They balance each other with the particular ease of two people who knew exactly what they were getting into. Kayan is one of the only people Txolaw allows physical contact with, a significant fact given the overwhelming empathy that makes Txolaw flinch from touch. Txolaw is one of the only people who can make Kayan stop running toward the next responsibility and simply rest.

### Txewan & Syulang
The composer and the voice. Txewan's anxiety is a lived daily reality; Syulang has organized her entire relational life around creating safe spaces, and the one she built for Txewan is the most carefully tended. Syulang does not fix Txewan's anxiety — she builds environments where it has less power. Their household, with the twins and baby Netezu and the Woven Harmony gatherings, is something Txewan could not have imagined for herself when she was young and silent and convinced music was the only thing she would ever have. They met through music. They stayed because music revealed who they both were underneath it.

### Tsu'ong & Ketxe
Two people who found redemption in each other. Tsu'ong was arrogant and had to be humiliated before he could become wise. Ketxe was captured and weaponized and had to escape before she could become herself. They did not fall into each other's arms — they recognized each other as people who had survived becoming something they did not want to be. Their bond is quieter than most, built on mutual respect for the distance each traveled. Ketxe's first love was Tsa'rawm; she disappeared to protect him from Varang's reach, and that sacrifice still lives in their history, unspoken but present.

### Rängay & Txafwe
Two wanderers who rebuilt themselves and then found each other across three hundred miles of Pandora. Both underwent gender transition through Eywa's eye. Both carry the specific weight of having chosen who they are against significant resistance — Rängay through spiritual discipline, Txafwe through his literal journey across the continent. Their intimacy is expressed in a language of small private things: a fruit balanced on a head during meditation, shells arranged just so, jokes that require no words. The Mangkwan captivity nearly destroyed what they had built. They are rebuilding it, slowly, through the same small language.

### Pxayu & Apayì
The crybaby and the witness. Pxayu built herself into a monument against abandonment; she adapted perfectly, dissolved herself into whatever role others needed, convinced that making herself indispensable was the only protection against being left. Apayì's gift is witnessing — seeing the specific, frightened, messy reality of people and refusing to look away. What Apayì did for Pxayu was not fix her. It was see her. The legendary bickering between them is the sound of a bond that has been tested and has not broken. They are raising Lawarey together and arguing about everything and staying anyway.

### Syayvi & Nì'syu/'Awll
An alliance that became love that became the most honest thing in 'Awll's fractured life. Nì'syu/'Awll was living a lie — born 'Awll, forced into her dead twin sister's identity to preserve a political alliance. She had been observed and performed and mirrored for so long that she had almost forgotten what it felt like to be seen. Syayvi understood her truth through tsaheylu without being told — a moment of connection so profound it became 'Awll's anchor. Their unborn son Sìlpey is both terror and hope: terror because deception has surrounded his conception, hope because new life is the most honest thing either of them will ever make.

### Tsu'lan & Yukeyo
Slow, tender, long-distance, still becoming. Tsu'lan treated Yukeyo with genuine respect during a two-week visit and left her completely unprepared for the depth of what developed. She stutters around him; he does not make her feel ridiculous for it. She asked him, before the Wild Hunt Arc, a single question: *"Is this justice or is this grief?"* He answered honestly. That honesty is the foundation of whatever they are building across the distance between the Anurai clan and Sheltering Falls. The Nine Spears essentially adopted her without asking and she is overwhelmed and secretly wonderful about it.

### Sänume & 'Eweyn
Rival energy that refuses to resolve cleanly into something simple. They drive each other relentlessly. Their love is sharp-edged and competitive and absolutely genuine. Sänume hates almost all humans and 'Eweyn has spent years around Sky People technology without flinching; they are not supposed to fit and they do anyway.

### Tìfyaw & Waynhì
The chaos and the order. Tìfyaw drinks too much and plays music that bends tradition and annoys Waynhì regularly. Waynhì is strict and proud and expresses affection primarily through tsundere criticism. Their relationship is held together by the specific understanding that Tìfyaw's chaos is genuine love expressed sideways and Waynhì's strictness is genuine love expressed frontward, and neither of them is wrong, and they know it.

### Katirì & Tswesya
Balance in practice. Katirì reaches outward; Tswesya retreats inward. Where Tswesya's silence reads as coldness to outsiders, Katirì translates it as depth, and where Katirì's warmth could overwhelm, Tswesya's reserve gives it weight. They do not complete each other in the romantic sense — they are already complete — they simply fit in the particular way of two people who chose well and know it.

### Lamaytxa & Kobeni
Ongoing, terrifying (to Kobeni), delightful (to Lamaytxa). Lamaytxa thinks she's "funny for a pinkskin." This is the highest possible praise from someone who doesn't trust humans. Kobeni is perpetually convinced she is about to be killed or adopted or both.

## Blood-Oath Siblings

### Atanvi, Tsa'rawm & Txewan
The three of them are blood-oath siblings — a bond chosen rather than born, sealed through ritual and made permanent before Eywa. Tsa'rawm is the reason the other two became more extroverted; his boisterous warmth pulled them both outward over years of sustained patience. Atanvi and Txewan underwent their gender transitions through the Eye of Eywa together, deepening a bond that was already profound. All three would die for each other without deliberation.

### Tsa'rawm, Kayan, Lamaytxa & Sänume
The Haykxa chosen-family unit. Not blood-oath in the formal Atanvi/Tsa'rawm/Txewan sense, but adopted siblings in every meaningful way — Kayan adopted Tsa'rawm, and Lamaytxa and Sänume followed. The four of them bicker constantly and are collectively immovable.

> [!note] Ruled 2026-11-12
> They are adopted, and so close that it is basically blood. "Blood-brother" and "blood-sister" on their notes mean exactly that.

## Mentor & Student Bonds

### 'Ipxerey → Sänrri
The tsahìk and her tsakarem. 'Ipxerey recognized in Sänrri the specific quality of a soul that had crossed a great divide — a dreamwalker who had given up everything human to become something true. She did not make Sänrri's path easy. She made it possible. Sänrri is now the tsakarem, 'Ipxerey's heir in spiritual leadership. The relationship has evolved from guidance into partnership.

### 'Ipxerey → Sänume
'Ipxerey's finest student — a distinction Sänume has earned through sheer relentless intelligence. Sänume is her right hand in healing matters alongside Mìreya and Sänrri. The mentor bond here is rigorous, demanding, and deeply mutual in its respect.

### Tìfyaw → Txewan
Tìfyaw mentored Txewan in music before anyone else saw what she could become. She is also the one who suggested, years earlier, that Txewan and Atanvi commune with Eywa through the Eye to undergo gender transition — having done it herself. She gave Txewan the compass. Txewan built her own path.

### Tswesya → Txewan/Txewì
A quieter mentorship. Tswesya gave Txewan the nickname Txewì and guides her through complex musical structures with patient wordless encouragement. This relationship happens inside the Woven Harmony, where Tswesya is allowed to be generous without performance.

### Tse'hayu → Txewan
The earliest music mentor. Tse'hayu was the first to put an instrument in Txewan's hands. He recognized something in her before she recognized it in herself.

### Tsu'lan → clan children (combat)
Tsu'lan's teaching methods are clan-famous — adaptability over rigid form, protecting others above personal glory. Young warriors trained by him are recognizable by their stance. His relationship with the children is warm and mentor-like in a way that complements rather than replaces their parents.

### Sänrri → Tulìpey (healing)
Informal apprenticeship, still forming. Tulìpey's interest in healing caught Sänrri's attention and is now developing into something more structured.

### Mìreya → Lawarey (healing)
Also informal and emerging. Lawarey's quiet, precise attentiveness to what people need is exactly the disposition Mìreya recognizes as the seed of a healer. She has begun to notice her.

## Rivalries

### Notä & Eantu
Volatile and creative. They are not enemies but they are never entirely at peace, and their conflict has an energy that mirrors the old tension between Tsu'ong (Eantu's father) and Atanvi (Notä's mother). Something about that lineage keeps generating the same charged dynamic across generations.

### Kxayì & Notä
Cousins who rival each other constantly and are also deeply family. Kxayì hero-worships Kaymi to an embarrassing degree; Notä got over a crush on Nì'syu and came out the other side of it with more dignity than expected.

### Rängay & Kaymi — The Pact
This needs its own section because it is not a simple rivalry and it is not a simple resolution. Rängay and Kaymi clashed over Sänrri from almost the beginning. Rängay is a disciplined ritualist who earned her own identity through spiritual practice; she found Kaymi's instinctive, overwhelming approach to protection borderline heretical. Kaymi is a warrior who does not justify her love in words — she acts. For a long time each found the other's method inadequate. Their unspoken pact formed over time: Rängay's secret approval of the Kaymi/Sänrri union was the ultimate concession. She trusted the warrior to protect the songbird in ways ritual alone could not. The terms of the pact, never spoken aloud: *guard her with your life, or I will spend mine undoing you.* Post-Siege, this has deepened into something neither would name but both act on. In a crisis they operate as one without speaking. They still disagree on almost everything about the clan's spiritual health and still have sharp, frequent debates about it. The respect is enormous and completely unacknowledged.

## Found Family & Clan Circles

### The Woven Harmony
Txewan, Syulang, Katirì, Tswesya. A music circle that became a chosen family. They found each other through music and decided to keep showing up. For Tswesya, the Harmony is one of the few spaces where their outsider feeling inside the clan dissolves. For Txewan, it is where her anxiety has the least power. For Syulang, it is where her scattered warmth becomes focused and purposeful. For Katirì, it is where she is allowed to lead through feeling rather than through structure.

### The Lost Cohort
Sänrri, Rängay, Pxayu, Apayì, Pamyaw. Formed around Sänrri's spiritual training under 'Ipxerey. Each arrived with a different wound and a different reason. They held each other through the Mangkwan captivity. They survived as a unit. Post-Siege, the Cohort's bonds are tested and deepened — trauma that would have scattered a group of strangers has instead braided them tighter. Pamyaw's version of belonging is appearing and disappearing as she pleases and being certain she will be understood when she returns.

### The Scattered Ashes
Syayvi, Tìmeyo/Kerevi, Nì'syu/'Awll, Txafwe, Yawyay. An exile's found family, built around shared exclusion and the need to forge something real out of displacement. Their loyalty to each other survived the Mangkwan corruption, the Siege, and the slow process of being slowly integrated back into the Astikasya's world. Post-Siege, Yawyay has formalized her new bond with Tìmeyo and is making plans to search for her lost clan. This is the beginning of the Ashes fragmenting in loving directions.

> [!note] Ruled 2026-11-12
> Yawyay is not searching for a lost clan. The Ta'unui are her home, and she leaves for her reef with Tìmeyo at the end of the Siege.

### The Nine Spears
Kaymi, Atanvi, Txewan, Tsa'rawm, Rewon, Tsu'lan, Syayvi, Tsu'ong, Ketxe. An elite warrior circle but also an emotional unit of the clan's most tested people. The Spears have bled for each other. They carry shared trauma from the Siege. Syayvi's inclusion — earned through combat, through protecting the children while pregnant, through choosing the clan over exile — changed the composition of the Spears in ways that are still rippling. The relationships within the Spears overlap heavily with the found-family and blood-oath categories above.

## Parent & Child Dynamics (Notable)

### Eyfuwen & his children (Kaymi, Atanvi)
Extraordinarily loving father. He pushed them toward their dreams and maintained patient, nurturing presence through their growth. Atanvi doubts herself until her family reminds her of her worth; Eyfuwen has been reminding her since childhood.

### 'Ipxerey & her children (Kaymi, Atanvi)
More complex. 'Ipxerey was not always the compassionate teacher she is now — she came from darkness and vengeance and has been building her way toward light for years. Her children have watched this transformation and been shaped by it. Her bond with Kaymi is the one most tested by history and most clearly strengthened by it.

### Tsa'rawm & Kxayì
Kxayì inherited her father's volume and Lamaytxa's chaos and hero-worships Kaymi to a degree Tsa'rawm finds hilarious and slightly humbling. She is desperately trying to prove she is more than "Tsa'rawm's loud daughter," which is the exact same motivation Tsa'rawm once had about his own family.

### Rängay & Mawpay
Mutual respect built on order and shared discipline. Rängay recognizes in Mawpay the same satisfaction in doing things correctly. Their relationship is one of the few places where Rängay's sternness does not read as intimidating — Mawpay simply responds to it as information.

### Txewan & her children (Mìyo, Mìrì, Netezu)
The discovery of being a mother gentled something in Txewan that even music could not fully reach. Mìrì is the spitting image of her. This is either very funny or very alarming depending on who you ask.

## Secrets That Shape Relationships

### Txewan's secret: she killed Sua
Txewan killed Sua during the Siege in a legendary knife duel through the mist-shrouded passages. Mizi, Sua's partner, does not know and will never know through Txewan. Txewan carries this like a stone, waking sometimes to apologize to empty air. The relationship between Txewan and the memory of this killing is part of what gives her Spear its weight.

### 'Awll's secret: she is not Nì'syu
Born 'Awll, forced to assume her dead twin sister's identity to preserve a political alliance. Syayvi knows the truth through tsaheylu. No one else does. Post-Siege, 'Awll and Nì'syu have almost merged — she moves through the world as herself now, neither name quite capturing it — but the secret still exists structurally and will eventually need to be resolved.

> [!note] Ruled 2026-11-12
> 'Awll took Nì'syu's name herself. She was not forced into it, and she knows who she is.

### Tìmeyo's old bond with Kaymi
Tìmeyo was once destined to be tsakarem and to mate with Kaymi. Exile severed both callings. Tìmeyo carries this loss in ways they rarely speak about. Kerevi occasionally references it with childlike directness and then has to watch Tìmeyo move through the complicated grief of hearing the truth out loud.

### Luka's obsession with Tsu'lan
Luka's fixation is on Tsu'lan. At the end of the Siege, Tsu'lan lets him go, and the vow that follows becomes [[The Wild Hunt]], where Luka dies.

> [!quote]- Earlier version (discarded 2026-11-12)
> Luka is studying Tsu'lan — his honor, his tactics, his way of being loved — in order to wear him like a mask and destroy the Astikasya from within. The relationship between them is not a relationship yet. It is the beginning of a threat that the clan does not know is coming.
